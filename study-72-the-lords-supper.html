STUDY 72 · DIGITAL STUDY

The Lord’s Supper

The Cup, the Bread, and the Body’s Remembrance

What did the Lord give at the table, what did Paul deliver to the Corinthians, and what does the cup carry?

The bread and the cup are among the most familiar signs in Christian life, and among the most argued over. The accounts in Matthew, Luke, and 1 Corinthians do not read alike, and each is addressed to its own table and its own hearers.

This study reads each account in its setting, follows Paul’s own statement of what he received and delivered, traces the cup’s wording to the covenant text it quotes, and works through the correction given at Corinth. It holds to one order throughout: what the text says first, what it leaves unsaid second, application third.

ABOUT THIS STUDY

Read Each Table in Its Own Setting

You will compare the accounts of the Supper in Matthew 26, Luke 22, and 1 Corinthians 11, follow Paul’s statement that he received it of the Lord, trace the words “new testament” back to Jeremiah 31:31–34, and work through the disorder at Corinth in 1 Corinthians 11:17–34. You will then read the breaking-of-bread references in Acts 2:42 and 20:7 for what they record, and test four common readings against the text.

Readers who already own Study 12 (The New Covenant), Study 65 (Acts 2:42: The Apostles’ Doctrine), or Study 53 (Redemption) will recognize adjoining ground. Study 12 reads the New Covenant as a covenant; this study reads only the cup’s wording and the covenant text it points to, and does not take up the covenant’s full content. Study 65 reads Acts 2:42 as a whole and asks what the Twelve’s doctrine was; this study notes the breaking of bread there only as a recorded fact and does not take up the doctrine. Study 53 follows redemption across Scripture; this study reads the blood only as the cup names it. The question this study alone answers: what do the passages say the Supper is, and what does the Body do when it takes the cup?

How These Studies Relate

Study 72 reads the Supper in its own passages. The studies below supply the neighboring ground it deliberately does not repeat.

WHAT YOU’LL STUDY

Trace the Words Before You Conclude

The Accounts Side by Side

Compare the Supper accounts in Matthew 26, Luke 22, and 1 Corinthians 11 word by word, recording what each says about the setting, the bread, and the cup before drawing any conclusion.

The Words “New Testament”

Trace the phrase “new testament” from the cup back to Jeremiah 31:31–34, noting which parties the covenant text itself names.

What Paul Received

Follow Paul’s statement in 1 Corinthians 11:23 and Galatians 1:12 about how he received the Supper, and what he says it was given to do among the Corinthians.

A Phrase as Written

Observe the exact wording of 1 Corinthians 11:26 and practice reporting a phrase as written instead of supplying what the verse does not say.

The Disorder at Corinth

Work through 1 Corinthians 11:17–34 to learn what “unworthily,” “examine himself,” and “discerning the Lord’s body” mean in the letter’s own setting.

Bread Recorded in Acts

Read the breaking-of-bread references in Acts 2:42 and 20:7 for what they record, and separate that from what they do not record.

The covenant text the cup’s wording points back to.

The correction: what Paul says is not the Lord’s supper.

The cup of blessing and the one bread.

Acts 20:7

The disciples come together to break bread.

FROM THE STUDY

A Rule for Reading Both Tables

Record what each account says before assigning it to any wider setting.

The same bread and cup at a different table, with a different audience, is a different passage to be read on its own terms.
