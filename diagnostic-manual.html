HOW TO USE THIS PROTOCOL

Follow each diagnostic question in sequence. Do not proceed to doctrinal application until audience, program, chronology, and markers have been identified.

01

Who Is Speaking?

Identify the principal speaker or actor. Is the passage centered on Peter, the Twelve, another Prophecy-program figure, or Paul?

Diagnostic Purpose: Establish the governing agent of the passage.

02

Who Is the Audience?

Determine whether the passage addresses Israel, a Jewish audience, Gentiles, a mixed audience, or those associated with Paul’s ministry.

Diagnostic Purpose: Identify the group to whom the message is directed.

03

What Divine Program Governs the Passage?

Identify whether the passage belongs to Israel’s Prophecy Program, Paul’s Mystery Program for the Body of Christ, or the historical Acts 9–28 setting in which both programs are operating concurrently.

Diagnostic Purpose: Determine whether Prophecy, Mystery, or the Acts 9–28 overlap is in view.

▤

Is the passage grounded in Israel’s prophetic covenants and promises, or in the Mystery revealed through Paul?

Diagnostic Purpose: Identify the covenantal or revelatory basis governing the passage.

⊞

Note elements such as signs, miracles, water baptism, synagogue ministry, covenant appeal, or distinctly Pauline Mystery doctrine.

THE ACTS 9–28 OVERLAP

The Chronology Behind the Diagnosis

Every passage in Acts must be understood within its proper historical and doctrinal setting. The diagnostic method of The Diagnostic Manual therefore pays careful attention not only to what a passage says, but also to where it stands within the Acts chronology.

PROPHECYBegins EarlierIsrael’s Prophecy Program begins before Acts, rooted in God’s covenants with Israel and foretold through Moses and the prophets. It establishes Israel’s national identity, kingdom expectations, covenant promises, and prophetic framework—the historical and theological setting already in operation when the Acts record begins.
→
ACTS 9Mystery BeginsWith Paul’s calling and commission, the Mystery Program begins to be made known—a previously hidden program distinct from Israel’s prophetic covenants and kingdom expectations. Revealed through Paul, it centers upon God’s purpose for the Body of Christ and introduces a second divine program alongside the continuing Prophecy Program.
→
ACTS 9–28Concurrent OverlapFrom Acts 9 through Acts 28, the Prophecy and Mystery Programs operate concurrently, each retaining its own audience, message, and divine purpose. This is the critical diagnostic setting of Acts: two distinct programs operating side-by-side without becoming one, requiring the interpreter to identify which program governs each passage.
→
ACTS 28Prophecy SuspendedActs 28 marks the suspension point of Israel’s Prophecy Program as Israel is officially set aside. Israel’s prophetic promises are not cancelled; their fulfillment awaits God’s future resumption of His prophetic dealings with the nation, while the Mystery Program continues beyond the Acts period.
→
BEYOND ACTS 28Mystery ContinuesBeyond Acts 28, the Mystery Program continues without the concurrent operation of Israel’s Prophecy Program. God’s present purpose centers upon the Body of Christ and the heavenly calling revealed through Paul, continuing until the fullness of the Gentiles.

During Acts 9–28, God’s Prophecy Program for Israel and Mystery Program for the Body of Christ operate concurrently, each retaining its own audience, message, and divine purpose. Recognizing this overlap prevents the interpreter from collapsing the two programs into one or treating Acts as though a single doctrinal administration governs every passage.

At Acts 28, Israel’s Prophecy Program reaches its suspension point, while the Mystery Program continues toward the fullness of the Gentiles.
