What Does “What Is Man” Mean in Psalm 8 and Hebrews 2?

From Reader Questions, Volume 6: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 6 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

Psalm 8 marvels at man’s place over God’s works. Hebrews reads the psalm in light of the world to come: the rule is not yet seen, but Jesus is seen, made lower and crowned. Hebrews applies the psalm to the Son of man and to Israel’s hope.

Where the Verses Stand

Hebrews 2 speaks of the world to come and quotes Psalm 8. The readers are Hebrews. Psalm 8 speaks of man’s dominion.

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
Ps 8:3–6“When I consider thy heavens, the work of thy fingers, the moon and the stars, which thou hast ordained; What is man, that thou art mindful of him? and the son of man, that thou visitest him? For thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honour. Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet:”“What is man … and the son of man”; “a little lower than the angels”; “all things under his feet.”
Heb 2:5–8“For unto the angels hath he not put in subjection the world to come, whereof we speak. But one in a certain place testified, saying, What is man, that thou art mindful of him? or the son of man, that thou visitest him? Thou madest him a little lower than the angels; thou crownedst him with glory and honour, and didst set him over the works of thy hands: Thou hast put all things in subjection under his feet. For in that he put all in subjection under him, he left nothing that is not put under him. But now we see not yet all things put under him.”“The world to come, whereof we speak”; the quotation; “now we see not yet all things put under him.”
Heb 2:9“But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels for the suffering of death, crowned with glory and honour; that he by the grace of God should taste death for every man.”“But we see Jesus” made lower and crowned.
Heb 2:16“For verily he took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Abraham.”“The seed of Abraham.”
Mt 21:15–16“And when the chief priests and scribes saw the wonderful things that he did, and the children crying in the temple, and saying, Hosanna to the Son of David; they were sore displeased, And said unto him, Hearest thou what these say? And Jesus saith unto them, Yea; have ye never read, Out of the mouth of babes and sucklings thou hast perfected praise?”The Lord quotes Psalm 8:2 to the chief priests about the children’s praise.
1 Cor 15:25–27“For he must reign, till he hath put all enemies under his feet. The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death. For he hath put all things under his feet. But when he saith, all things are put under him, it is manifest that he is excepted, which did put all things under him.”Paul quotes “all things under his feet” of Christ’s reign, to the Body.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the world to come is the subject.
  • Shown by the context: the rule is not yet seen; Jesus is.
  • Inferred: Hebrews reads the psalm in Israel’s hope; Paul uses the same line differently for the Body.

The Other Readings

  • Christ alone: strains at the psalm’s “man.”
  • Humanity alone: strains at Hebrews’ “we see Jesus.”
  • Both in order: fits the text.
ReadingWhat it must sayWhere it strains
Humanity in generalThe psalm is about every man.“The world to come”; “not yet.”
The Messiah onlyThe psalm is about Christ from the start.“What is man”; “babes and sucklings.”
Man’s rule, seen first in JesusThe psalm states the appointment; Hebrews shows its first sight.None; it joins both texts.

What It Means for the Reader

The psalm’s wonder is fulfilled in Jesus and awaits the world to come.
