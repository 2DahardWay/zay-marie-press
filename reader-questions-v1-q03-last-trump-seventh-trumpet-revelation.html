Is “the Last Trump” the Seventh Trumpet of Revelation?

From Reader Questions, Volume 1: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 1 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

No. The last trump of 1 Corinthians 15 and the trump of God of 1 Thessalonians 4 are the Body’s trumpet, sounded at a gathering Paul calls a mystery. The seventh trumpet of Revelation 11:15 sounds for the kingdoms of this world to become the Lord’s, and Paul never mentions it.

Where the Verse Stands

First Corinthians 15 argues for the resurrection of the dead, and at verse 50 Paul turns to a new subject: “Behold, I shew you a mystery.” The trumpet belongs to that mystery. First Thessalonians 4:13–18 gives the same event to a church grieving its dead, and names “the trump of God.” Revelation 11:15–18 is a different scene in a different book, with its own audience and its own result.

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
1 Cor 15:51–52“Behold, I shew you a mystery; We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed, In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed.”A mystery, shown to “you.” The trumpet raises the dead and changes the living.
1 Thess 4:16–17“For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord.”The trump of God. The result is meeting the Lord and being with him.
Rev 11:15“And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.”The seventh angel. The result is the kingdom.
Rev 11:18“And the nations were angry, and thy wrath is come, and the time of the dead, that they should be judged, and that thou shouldest give reward unto thy servants the prophets, and to the saints, and them that fear thy name, small and great; and shouldest destroy them which destroy the earth.”Anger, wrath, judgment, reward, destruction.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: Paul introduces the trumpet as part of “a mystery” (1 Corinthians 15:51), something shown to the Corinthians that had not been shown before.
  • Stated: the two Pauline passages share a trumpet, a rising of the dead, a change of the living and being with the Lord. Revelation 11 shares the word “trumpet” and the dead, but the result is the kingdom, wrath and judgment, and reward to servants.
  • Shown by the context: the word “last” closes Paul’s own sentence. He gives the Corinthians no series of trumpets to count and names no seventh. Revelation 11 is in a book of seals, trumpets and bowls, where the trumpets are numbered.
  • Shown by the context: Paul tells the Thessalonians to “comfort one another with these words” (4:18). The trumpet of Revelation 11 brings the nations’ anger and God’s wrath (11:18), which is not what Paul offers as comfort.
  • Inferred: that “last” means the last call of the Body, the final trumpet in Paul’s own use, and not the last in Revelation’s numbered series. The text does not say which series “last” belongs to, and the argument rests on the audience and the result.

The Other Readings

The same trumpet. George Eldon Ladd (The Blessed Hope) argues that the gathering comes at the end of the tribulation. Writers who hold that view commonly read Paul’s trumpet together with the final trumpet of Revelation. The reading places the church in the scenes that Revelation 11:18 describes as wrath. Separate trumpets. John Walvoord (The Rapture Question) argues that the gathering precedes the tribulation, and writers who hold that view commonly read Paul’s trumpet as distinct from Revelation’s. That is the reading taken here, and the entry argues it from the two passages’ own settings.

ReadingWhat it must sayWhere it strains
Same trumpet as Revelation 11:15The gathering comes at the seventh trumpet.Different audience, result and sequence; believers placed under the wrath of 11:18.
Distinct trumpetsPaul’s trumpet gathers the Body; Revelation’s announces the kingdom.“Last” has to be read within Paul’s own sentence.

What It Means for the Reader

The hope of 1 Thessalonians 4 does not wait for the sequence of Revelation’s trumpets. The Body’s trumpet and Revelation’s seventh trumpet sound for different companies and different results, and the reader should not carry the scenes of Revelation 11 into 1 Corinthians 15.
