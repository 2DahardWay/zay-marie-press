Joel

The day of the LORD.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreMinor Prophets
Primary SettingJudah amid devastating locust plague
Key MovementFrom present devastation to the day of the LORD and restored Zion
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

Joel interprets a catastrophic plague as a summons to national repentance and as a preview of the day of the LORD. The prophet announces judgment, calls priests and people to return, promises the Spirit upon Israel, and looks toward judgment of the nations and blessing from Zion.

Historical and Biblical Context

The book gives few explicit chronological markers. Its temple, priesthood, elders, and Zion-centered message clearly place the prophecy within Judah’s covenant life.

Evidence and inference: Prophetic texts join historical setting, covenant proclamation, symbolic imagery, and future promise. This summary distinguishes explicit textual claims from debated conclusions about date, structure, chronology, or fulfillment.

Structure and Outline

  1. Plague and lament — Joel 1
    Locust devastation ruins crops, offerings, and communal joy.
  2. The day approaches — Joel 2:1–17
    An invading force intensifies alarm, and the nation is called to wholehearted return.
  3. Restoration and the Spirit — Joel 2:18–32
    God answers, restores, and promises His Spirit before the great day.
  4. Nations judged and Zion blessed — Joel 3
    The LORD judges nations for their treatment of His people and dwells in Zion.

Major Themes

The Day of the LORD

Present crisis opens onto decisive divine judgment.

Repentance

The call is inward and communal, not merely ceremonial.

The Spirit

The promised outpouring belongs to Israel’s prophetic restoration horizon.

Zion and the Nations

God vindicates His people and reigns from Jerusalem.

Key Passages

Joel 1:13–20Priests and land lament before the LORD.
Joel 2:1–11The day is announced with terrifying imagery.
Joel 2:12–17Judah is summoned to return with the whole heart.
Joel 2:18–27The LORD restores land and removes reproach.
Joel 2:28–32The Spirit and cosmic signs precede deliverance in Zion.
Joel 3:9–21Nations gather for judgment and the LORD dwells in Zion.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Distinguish the locust crisis, invading army imagery, and wider day-of-the-LORD horizon.
  • Joel’s Spirit promise is quoted in Acts 2 within Israel’s prophetic setting; the quotation does not erase its stated features.
  • Zion, Judah, Jerusalem, land, and nations should retain their textual identities.
  • Uncertain dating should be acknowledged without controlling the book’s clear message.

Study Questions

  1. How does the plague function prophetically?
  2. What marks genuine return in Joel 2?
  3. Which features accompany the Spirit promise?
  4. How does the book contrast the nations’ judgment with Zion’s future?
