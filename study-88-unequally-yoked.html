STUDY 88 · DIGITAL STUDY

Unequally Yoked

What Paul Commands, What He Quotes, and What He Applies in 2 Corinthians 6:14–7:1

In 2 Corinthians 6:14–7:1, what does Paul command the Corinthian assembly, what does he quote from Israel’s Scriptures, and what does he apply from them?

“Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers” is read as a rule for marriage, business, friendship and church membership, sometimes all in one sermon. Then the passage turns, and Paul quotes Israel’s Scriptures in a chain of promises. The reader is left holding a command, a stack of quotations and no clear way to say how they fit together.

This study walks the passage in order. It keeps three questions apart: what Paul commands, what he quotes, and what he applies. Each quoted line is read in its own chapter, audience and setting before Paul’s use of it is read.

ABOUT THIS STUDY

Keep the Command, the Quotation and the Application Apart

You will read the frame of 6:11–13 and 7:2–4, the command of 6:14a, the five questions of 6:14b–16a, Paul’s own statement in 6:16a, and then the lines gathered in 6:16b–18 beside Leviticus 26, Ezekiel 37, Isaiah 52 and 2 Samuel 7, each in its own chapter. The study then reads the application in 7:1 and lists what the passage does not say.

Readers who already own Study 39 (The Church’s Relationship to Israel’s Scriptures), Study 37 (What Does Paul Mean by “New Creation”?), Study 45 (Sanctification Under Grace) or Study 52 (Sonship and Adoption) will recognize adjoining ground. Study 39 sets out the repeatable method for how the Body learns from Israel’s Scriptures, while this study walks one passage; Study 37 reads what “new creation” itself asserts, and this study does not enter 5:17; Study 45 reads the several things Paul means by sanctification language, and this study handles only 7:1; Study 52 reads sonship and adoption in Paul’s letters, and this study reports the Father and sons language only as part of the quoted chain. The question this study alone answers: in 2 Corinthians 6:14–7:1, what is Paul’s own command, what is quoted from Israel’s Scriptures, and what does he apply to Corinth?

Five Paired Contrasts

Each Source in Its Own Setting

The Application in 7:1

KEY SCRIPTURES

Read the Word in Its Context

2 Corinthians 6:14–16a

“Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers,” followed by five questions built on “what.”

2 Corinthians 6:16a

“Ye are the temple of the living God,” in Paul’s own voice and before the words “as God hath said.”

Leviticus 26:11–12

“And I set my tabernacle among you,” spoken in a chapter of blessings and warnings.

Ezekiel 37:26–27

“My tabernacle also shall be with them: yea, I will be their God, and they shall be my people.”

Isaiah 52:11

A command addressed to those who “bear the vessels of the LORD,” in the setting of Zion’s redemption.

2 Samuel 7:14

“I will be his father, and he shall be my son,” spoken to David through Nathan.

2 Corinthians 7:1

“Let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit.”

Deuteronomy 22:10

“Thou shalt not plow with an ox and an ass together,” read as an echo and nothing more.

FROM THE STUDY

A Rule for Reading This Subject

In 2 Corinthians 6:14–7:1 there are three kinds of statement: what Paul commands, what he quotes, and what he applies. Keep them in three separate columns in your notes as you read.

The study keeps those three columns apart from the first section to the last.
