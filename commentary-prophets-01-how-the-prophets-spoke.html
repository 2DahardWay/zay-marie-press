THE PROPHETS SERIES · VOLUME 1

How the Prophets Spoke

The Office, the Tests, and the Word the LORD Swears

The prophets say “Thus saith the LORD.” By their own account, how is that word known to be his, and what can turn an announced word aside?

Moses gives Israel a test for a prophet whose word does not come to pass. He gives another for a prophet whose word does. Jeremiah tells the people to ask where a vision came from, and the prophet Hananiah, who contradicted him, is dead within the year. Jonah announces the end of Nineveh in forty days, and the city stands. Balaam says God is not a man that he should repent, and Jeremiah says the LORD repents of the evil. How do these fit together?

It argues that the prophets give not one test but several, each answering a different question, and that they mark a word the LORD has sworn as a different kind of word from a judgment he announces. In Chapter 8 it sets its reading beside modern writers who see it differently.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not list the views held about prophets and leave the choice to the reader. It says what the author believes the prophets themselves teach about how they spoke, gives the evidence from their own sentences, and answers the strongest objections in the objectors’ own words.

It is the first volume of The Prophets Series, each volume of which is built around one narrower question the prophets raise themselves, not a walk through the prophetic books. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which report what a passage says. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout, checked word for word, and the writers named in Chapter 8 are quoted only from texts the author has read.

WHAT THE VOLUME COVERS

From the Office to the Objections

The Tests the Prophets Give

Learn to name each test in the prophets’ own words — source, loyalty, fulfilment — and to ask of each what question it answers and when it can be applied.

Source and Loyalty

Follow Jeremiah 23 and Ezekiel 13 on the word that comes from the prophet’s own heart, and Deuteronomy 13 and Jeremiah 28 on the sign that leads from the LORD.

Fulfilment

Read Deuteronomy 18:22 and 1 Kings 22 together, and mark what an event can settle after the fact and what it cannot settle beforehand.

The Conditional Word

Trace Jeremiah 18, Jeremiah 26, Jonah 3 and 4, and Ezekiel 33 to see what turns an announced judgment aside, and what the LORD says it does not.

The Sworn Word

Set Numbers 23, 1 Samuel 15, Isaiah 54 and 55, Psalm 89 and Malachi 3:6 beside the conditional passages, and see how the prophets mark a word that does not turn.

The Objections

Hear named modern writers who read the passages differently, then weigh each reply against the text.

FROM THE VOLUME

What This Book Sets Out to Do

The prophets give not one test but several, and each answers a different question. A reader who runs them in the wrong order will call true prophets false, or false prophets true.

A prophet whose sign comes to pass may still be refused. A prophet whose word fails may have been given a condition. And a word the LORD has sworn is never turned back. The volume asks the prophets how they tell these apart, and the reader will see why no single test is enough.
