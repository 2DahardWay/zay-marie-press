STUDY 69 · DIGITAL STUDY

The Eternal State

The Two-Realm Kingdom

Where does Scripture place Israel and the Body in the eternal state — and how are the two realms joined?

Scripture ends where it begins, with heaven and earth. Some passages describe a renewed earth with houses, vineyards, and nations; others describe a hope laid up in heaven, where Christ sits. The way through is to read each passage where it stands, and to whom it speaks, before deciding how the pictures fit.

This study begins with the four new-creation passages, follows the earthly setting, the city of Revelation 21, and Paul’s heavenly language, tests Galatians 4:26 and Hebrews 12:22–23, and reads the Kingdom passages side by side, holding to one order throughout: meaning first, assignment second, application third.

ABOUT THIS STUDY

Read the Passages in Order: Meaning, Assignment, Application

You will collect the passages that speak of new heavens and a new earth, read the earthly setting in Isaiah, the Psalms, Jesus, and John, and follow the city of Revelation 21 by its own markers. You will trace Paul’s heavenly language, read Ephesians 1:10 and Colossians 1:16–20 for the way they join heaven and earth, and only then draw the assignment and application.

Readers who already own Study 5 (The Heavenly Calling), Study 57 (The Eternal Purpose of God), or Study 1 (The Bride of Christ) will recognize adjoining ground. Study 5 maps the Body’s whole heavenly calling, including blessings, citizenship, and purpose; this study takes the heavenly setting as one of two realms and does not re-derive the calling. Study 57 traces God’s purpose before the ages and its future fullness; this study reads Ephesians 1:10 only for the two realms it names. Study 1 establishes to whom the bridal identity belongs; this study notes that the city of Revelation 21 is called a bride and defers the question of the identity. The question this study alone answers: where does Scripture place Israel and the Body in the eternal state, what do the passages state about each realm, and how are the two joined under Christ?

WHAT YOU’LL STUDY

Trace the Passages Before You Conclude

The New-Creation Passages

Collect the texts that speak of new heavens and a new earth (Isa. 65:17; 66:22; 2 Pet. 3:13; Rev. 21:1) and observe who speaks, to whom, and what each describes.

The Earthly Setting

Trace the markers of the earthly setting in Isaiah 65:17–25, Psalm 37:29, Matthew 5:5, and Revelation 21:1–4, noting the people, the place, and the conditions each passage names.

The City by Its Markers

Follow the city of Revelation 21 by its own markers, including the names on its gates and foundations, its bridal language, and its temple, alongside Revelation 3:12 and Hebrews 11:10, 16.

Paul’s Heavenly Language

Trace the heavenly language of Paul in Ephesians 1:3; 2:6; Philippians 3:20; Colossians 1:5; 3:1–4; and 2 Timothy 4:18 by asking where each passage places the hope and to whom it is written.

Testing Two Texts and the Kingdom

Test Galatians 4:26 and Hebrews 12:22–23 by their contexts, and read Ephesians 1:10, Colossians 1:16–20, and 1 Corinthians 15:24–28 beside Luke 1:33, Daniel 7, and Revelation 11:15 without forcing a resolution beyond the text.

Testing Four Readings

Apply a repeatable discipline for testing four common readings of the eternal state without transferring any passage to a different audience.

KEY SCRIPTURES

Read the Word in Its Context

Isaiah 65:17–25

New heavens and a new earth, Jerusalem, and the LORD’s people.

Matthew 5:1–12

The meek, the earth, and the Kingdom, spoken to disciples in Galilee.

Revelation 21:1–27

The new heaven and earth, and the holy city with its gates and foundations.

Ephesians 1:3–23

Blessings in the heavenly places and the gathering of all things in Christ.

Ephesians 2:4–7

Raised up together and seated together in heavenly places.

Philippians 3:17–21

The commonwealth in heaven and the Saviour from thence.

Galatians 4:21–31

An allegory of two covenants, and Jerusalem which is above.

1 Corinthians 15:24–28

The end, the delivering up of the kingdom, and God all in all.
