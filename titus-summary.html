Titus

Paul directs Titus to establish qualified leadership, silence destructive teaching, and cultivate the good works that properly adorn sound doctrine and saving grace.

TestamentNew Testament
GenrePauline Epistle
RecipientTitus in Crete
Central EmphasisSound doctrine expressed in good works
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

Paul has left Titus in Crete to set unfinished matters in order and appoint elders in every town. Qualified leaders must hold firmly to the trustworthy word so they can teach sound doctrine and refute rebellious deceivers.

Titus must also teach conduct fitting sound doctrine across age and household groups. The grace that saves trains believers to reject ungodliness and become zealous for good works while awaiting Christ’s appearing.

Historical and Biblical Context

Titus was a Gentile coworker who accompanied Paul in sensitive ministry situations, including the Jerusalem meeting reflected in Galatians 2 and the Corinthian collection. Crete presented a setting of immature assemblies and aggressive false teachers.

The mission in Crete is not narrated in Acts, so the letter is commonly placed after Paul’s first Roman imprisonment. As with 1–2 Timothy, Pauline authorship is disputed by some scholars; the letter itself presents Paul’s apostolic commission and personal directions to a proven delegate.

Structure and Outline

  1. Appoint elders and silence deceivers — Titus 1
    Titus must establish blameless leaders who hold the word and rebuke corrupt teachers.
  2. Teach conduct fitting sound doctrine — Titus 2
    Every group receives instruction grounded in saving and training grace and Christ’s appearing.
  3. Practice good works in society — Titus 3
    Grace-produced conduct, mercy-based salvation, avoidance of foolish disputes, and decisive discipline mark healthy assemblies.

Major Themes

Truth According to Godliness

Truth and conduct belong together. Sound doctrine produces self-control, faithfulness, love, endurance, honesty, and useful service.

Qualified Elders

Elders must be blameless in home, character, relationships, and doctrine. They guard the assembly by teaching and refuting error.

Grace That Trains

Saving grace actively educates believers to deny ungodliness and live soberly, righteously, and godly while awaiting Christ.

Salvation by Mercy

God saves not because of righteous works performed by sinners but according to His mercy, producing heirs who then devote themselves to good works.

Publicly Credible Conduct

Believers adorn the doctrine of God through reliability, submission, gentleness, generosity, and readiness for useful work.

Key Passages

Titus 1:1–4Paul’s apostleship, truth, godliness, and hope.
Titus 1:5–16Elder qualifications and the exposure of false teachers.
Titus 2:1–10Conduct fitting sound doctrine across the community.
Titus 2:11–15Saving grace, present training, blessed hope, and a zealous people.
Titus 3:1–8Civic conduct and salvation according to mercy.
Titus 3:9–15Avoiding fruitless disputes, correcting divisiveness, and meeting needs.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Good works are repeatedly required as salvation’s proper fruit, never presented as its meritorious cause.
  • Elder qualifications must be read as an integrated character portrait; selecting one trait while neglecting the others defeats Paul’s purpose.
  • The quotation about Cretans in 1:12 is used to expose a recognized local pattern, not to authorize ethnic contempt.
  • “The blessed hope” centers on the appearing of the glory of Jesus Christ and motivates present purity; it should not be detached from the surrounding grace instruction.
  • The washing and renewal language of 3:5 is attributed to God’s mercy and the Holy Spirit, not to human works performed for justification.
  • Meaningful correction includes both patient warning and a boundary: a divisive person is rejected after repeated admonition.

Study Questions

  1. Why must an elder be able both to teach and refute?
  2. How are false teachers identified by doctrine, motive, and conduct?
  3. What conduct fits sound doctrine for each group in chapter 2?
  4. What does grace teach believers to reject and pursue?
  5. How does Titus 3 distinguish the basis of salvation from its fruit?
  6. Which controversies are unprofitable, and how should divisiveness be handled?
