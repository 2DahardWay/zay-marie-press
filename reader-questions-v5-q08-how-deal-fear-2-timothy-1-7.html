How Do I Deal with Fear? 2 Timothy 1:7

From Reader Questions, Volume 5: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 5 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

Fear is met by grounding the heart in what God has given the Body. Paul says “God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind,” and that believers have not received “the spirit of bondage again to fear.” The Body is not appointed to wrath: “God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation.” God’s peace “shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus” when truth is believed, and the Spirit strengthens courage as the mind is renewed. Meditation on Scripture and fellowship with other believers steady the heart. Fear is a call to deeper trust, as David wrote: “What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee,” a general truth that Paul’s teaching confirms.

Where the Verses Stand

Paul writes 2 Timothy from prison, near the end, and tells Timothy to stir up the gift of God and not to be timid. Romans 8 describes the Spirit of adoption who casts out the spirit of bondage. Philippians 4 gives the peace that keeps the heart. Psalm 56 is David’s word in a time of fear.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: God “hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind” (2 Timothy 1:7), and believers have received “the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father” (Romans 8:15).
  • Stated: Paul says the Body’s destiny is not wrath: “God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ” (1 Thessalonians 5:9).
  • Shown by the context: the means is the mind and the peace of God: “the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus” (Philippians 4:7), and “to be spiritually minded is life and peace” (Romans 8:6).
  • Shown by the context: Psalm 56:3 is David’s word in a time of fear, and the believer today may take it as a general truth of trusting God; it is cited here as a principle and not as the Body’s instruction. Hebrews 10:24 and Paul’s words in Colossians 3:16 encourage mutual stirring-up.
  • Shown by the context: Timothy is told in the same chapter not to be “ashamed of the testimony of our Lord” (2 Timothy 1:8). Paul addresses a man who is afraid and does not rebuke him as unbelieving; he reminds him of what God has given.
  • Inferred: that fear is addressed chiefly by believing what God has given and removed, and not by rebuking a spirit. The texts name the gift and the removal; the argument infers the method.

The Other Readings

Fear as a spirit to cast out. Frank Hammond (Pigs in the Parlor) teaches that fear can be a demonic spirit to be rebuked. It must explain why Paul tells Timothy what God has given and not a ritual. Fear and the God of rest. Some treat fear as a call to know God’s character and to take refuge in him. This is close to the reading taken here.

What It Means for the Reader

The reader is told that fear is not what God gave, that the Spirit gives power, love and a sound mind, that the Body is not appointed to wrath, and that peace keeps the heart. Fear then becomes the signal to remember these and trust. The passage does not say a believer will never be afraid, and it does not call every fear sin. Timothy is encouraged, not scolded, and the reader is given reasons to trust and the peace that follows.
