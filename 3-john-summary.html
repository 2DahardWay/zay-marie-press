3 John

The elder commends Gaius for supporting faithful workers, confronts Diotrephes’s self-promoting obstruction, and recommends Demetrius as a trustworthy witness.

TestamentNew Testament
GenreGeneral Epistle
RecipientGaius, the beloved
Central EmphasisHospitality and faithful partnership in truth
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

The elder writes personally to Gaius, rejoicing that visiting believers testify to his faithfulness and love. He urges Gaius to continue sending faithful workers forward in a manner worthy of God, thereby becoming a fellow worker for the truth.

The letter also exposes Diotrephes, who loves prominence, rejects the elder’s authority, slanders the apostolic circle, refuses hospitality, and removes those who offer it. In contrast, Demetrius receives testimony from everyone, from the truth, and from the elder.

Historical and Biblical Context

Third John reflects a network of congregations and traveling workers dependent on local hospitality. The elder’s authority reaches beyond one meeting, but Diotrephes dominates a local assembly and resists outside accountability.

The author is unnamed beyond “the elder.” Vocabulary, themes, and close relationship with 2 John support common Johannine authorship. Several people named Gaius appear in the New Testament, but the letter does not provide enough evidence to identify this recipient with certainty.

Structure and Outline

  1. Greeting and prayer for Gaius — 3 John 1–4
    The elder loves Gaius in truth and rejoices that he walks in truth.
  2. Commendation of faithful hospitality — 3 John 5–8
    Gaius supports traveling brothers and becomes a fellow worker for the truth.
  3. Exposure of Diotrephes — 3 John 9–10
    Love of prominence produces rejected authority, malicious speech, blocked hospitality, and exclusion.
  4. Commendation of Demetrius and closing — 3 John 11–15
    Gaius must imitate good; Demetrius has multiple witnesses; personal fellowship is anticipated.

Major Themes

Walking in Truth

Truth is visible in conduct. Gaius’s life brings the elder deep joy because confession and practice agree.

Hospitality as Gospel Partnership

Supporting faithful workers is not secondary kindness. Those who send them forward become fellow workers for the truth.

Healthy and Abusive Authority

The elder uses authority to commend truth and protect service. Diotrephes uses influence to seek first place, slander, control access, and expel dissenters.

Imitating Good

Conduct reveals spiritual allegiance. Gaius must not imitate evil but the good demonstrated by faithful witnesses.

Confirmed Testimony

Demetrius’s reliability is not self-asserted; it is supported by the community, the truth itself, and apostolic testimony.

Key Passages

3 John 1–4Love in truth, prayer, and joy over faithful walking.
3 John 5–6Faithful love shown to traveling brothers.
3 John 7–8Sending workers forward and sharing in truth’s work.
3 John 9–10Diotrephes’s ambition, slander, obstruction, and exclusion.
3 John 11–12Imitating good and Demetrius’s threefold testimony.
3 John 13–15Face-to-face hope, peace, friends, and personal greetings.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Read 2 and 3 John together: faithful hospitality supports truth, while discernment refuses support to deceivers.
  • Do not infer Gaius’s exact office or identity beyond the evidence; his central role is a faithful host and beloved believer.
  • Diotrephes’s problem is not mere personality conflict. The text names ambition, rejection of apostolic communication, slander, blocked service, and coercive exclusion.
  • Hospitality in this setting includes material participation in traveling ministry, which explains why careful endorsement matters.
  • The statement about physical prosperity in verse 2 is a personal prayer, not a universal promise that spiritual faithfulness guarantees wealth or health.
  • Meaningful accountability includes multiple witnesses: reputation, conduct aligned with truth, and apostolic confirmation.

Study Questions

  1. What reports about Gaius bring the elder joy?
  2. How does hospitality make someone a fellow worker for the truth?
  3. Which behaviors expose Diotrephes’s misuse of authority?
  4. Why must Gaius imitate good rather than evil?
  5. What testimony supports Demetrius?
  6. How do 2 and 3 John together govern hospitality?

Continue your study.
Compare Gaius, Diotrephes, and Demetrius by their relation to truth, testimony, hospitality, authority, and the good.
