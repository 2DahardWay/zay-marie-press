Suffering and Glory

Romans 8 and 2 Corinthians 4 in Their Own Settings

What do Romans 8:17–39 and 2 Corinthians 4 say about suffering and glory, and who is speaking to whom in each?

Romans 8 and 2 Corinthians 4 are among the most quoted passages on hardship. A reader meets “the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory” or “our light affliction, which is but for a moment” and the mind moves at once to a doctrine of suffering for every believer. This study slows that movement and reads each passage in the order Paul wrote it.

It asks four things of every sentence: who is speaking, to whom, what is said of suffering or of glory, and what the passage leaves unsaid. It records what each passage says and stops where the text stops.

ABOUT THIS STUDY

Ask Who Is Speaking and to Whom

You will read Romans 1:7 and 2 Corinthians 1:1 for the two addresses, follow the words “suffer,” “sufferings,” “tribulation” and “affliction,” read Romans 8:17–39 in order, read 2 Corinthians 4:1–5:1 in order, mark who “we” is in each verse, and set other passages of Paul beside the two chapters to see what each states and what it leaves unsaid.

Readers who already own Study 46 (Secure in Christ), Study 52 (Sonship and Adoption), Study 53 (Redemption) or Study 81 (Praying in the Spirit) will recognize adjoining ground. Study 46 reads Romans 8:28–39 for the security of the believer; Studies 52 and 53 read adoption and redemption; Study 81 reads Romans 8:26–27 on praying. This study asks only what these two chapters say of suffering and of glory, and who is speaking to whom. The question this study alone answers: what do Romans 8:17–39 and 2 Corinthians 4 say about suffering and glory, and who is “we” in each verse?

WHAT YOU’LL STUDY

Follow Each Passage Through Its Own Markers

Two Letters, Two Addresses

Read Romans 1:7 and 2 Corinthians 1:1 and note who each letter names as its hearers before reading chapter 8 or chapter 4.

“If So Be That We Suffer”

Record what Romans 8:17–18 states of heirs, a clause and a reckoning, and what the verses leave unsaid.

The Bridge and the List

Read 8:26–30 as written, then count the afflictions of 8:35 and the things named in 8:38–39.

Who Is “We”?

Follow the speakers through 2 Corinthians 4:1–15, marking where “us” is set beside “you” in 4:12.

Light Affliction, Weight of Glory

Trace the words “light,” “weight,” “seen” and “not seen” in 4:16–18 and note where the passage stops at 5:1.

Five Passages Set Beside

Set Philippians, Colossians, 2 Corinthians 1 and 2 Timothy beside the two chapters, listing where the wording agrees and where it differs.

KEY SCRIPTURES

Read the Word in Its Context

Romans 8:17–18

Heirs, a clause beginning “if so be that we suffer with him,” and Paul’s reckoning of “this present time.”

Romans 8:19–25

The creature, “ourselves also,” the adoption and the waiting “with patience.”

Romans 8:26–30

The Spirit’s help, “all things” and the sequence of verbs, reported as written.

Romans 8:31–39

The questions, the seven afflictions of verse 35 and the closing list.

2 Corinthians 4:1–6

“This ministry,” and the speakers who hold it.

2 Corinthians 4:7–15

The treasure in earthen vessels, four pairs, and “death worketh in us, but life in you.”

2 Corinthians 4:16–5:1

“Light affliction,” “a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory,” and where the passage stops.

2 Timothy 2:11–12

“If we suffer, we shall also reign with him,” read in its own letter beside the two chapters.

FROM THE STUDY

A Rule for Reading This Subject

Read each passage in its own setting, with its speaker, its audience and its occasion. Make a link between passages only where a passage makes it.

When a text leaves a matter unsaid, record that and stop.
