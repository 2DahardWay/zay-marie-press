Who Is “All Israel” in Romans 11:26?

From Reader Questions, Volume 2: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 2 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

“All Israel” is the nation of Israel, saved at the coming of the Deliverer, after “the fulness of the Gentiles” has come in. It is not the Body, not “spiritual Israel” and not the whole company of the elect from every nation. Romans 9–11 is spoken to Israel and concerns Israel.

Where the Verses Stand

Romans 9 to 11 turns from the Body’s standing in chapters 1 to 8 to the nation of Israel. Paul begins with grief over “my brethren, my kinsmen according to the flesh,” and ends the section with a doxology. The words “all Israel” stand at the center of the last chapter, in a passage Paul introduces as a mystery.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the blindness is “in part … to Israel,” with a time limit: “until the fulness of the Gentiles be come in.” The saving of “all Israel” follows that limit.
  • Stated: the proof is from Isaiah, with the Deliverer coming “out of Sion” to turn away “ungodliness from Jacob.” The name “Jacob” is the nation.
  • Shown by the context: the word “Israel” in 11:1 is Paul’s own nation, and in 11:7, 11:25 and 11:26 it is the same. Nothing in the passage marks a change of meaning at 11:26.
  • Shown by the context: the covenant of 11:27 is “my covenant unto them,” the covenant of Isaiah 59:21 given to Jacob’s seed. It is Israel’s covenant.
  • Inferred: that the “mystery” of verse 25 is not the Body’s mystery. It is a truth about Israel’s partial blindness stated during the administration in which Paul wrote, and it concerns Israel’s program. The text does not call it the mystery of Christ.

The Boundaries the Chapter Keeps

Four things govern every reading of this chapter. The olive tree is prophetic blessing, not the Body of Christ. The Gentiles in Romans 11 are prophetic participants, not Body Gentiles. The wild branches are not Body members. Grafting changes participation, not identity. Participation is not identity. Blessing is not covenant. Standing is not program membership. The wild branch is grafted in among the branches, not into Israel; it partakes of the root and fatness, but it does not become a natural branch. “Salvation is come unto the Gentiles” (11:11) means salvation, not covenant, prophetic identity or Israel’s program. Any reading that turns Gentile blessing into identity transfer violates Paul’s warning and collapses the distinction between Israel’s program and the Body’s. Identity is fixed by divine program, not by metaphor. Gentile blessing does not relocate Gentiles into Israel, and Israel’s diminishing does not relocate Israel into the Body. Concurrency is operational, not transformational.

The Other Readings

All Israel is the whole elect, Jew and Gentile. N. T. Wright (The Climax of the Covenant) takes it as the one family of God made of Jew and Gentile. This reading has to give “Israel” a different meaning in 11:26 from that of 11:25, and it places the elect of the nations in the covenant of Isaiah 59. All Israel is the nation, at the end. Douglas Moo (The Epistle to the Romans) reads it as the nation as a whole at the end, and argues that the structure of chapters 9–11 requires it. William Hendriksen (Israel in Prophecy) takes it differently again, as the elect Jews of all ages, which keeps “Israel” ethnic but gathers the saved across the whole age. That is the reading taken here, and the entry adds that the passage is Israel’s, not the Body’s.

What It Means for the Reader

The hope of 11:26 belongs to Israel. The Body is not waiting for it, is not part of it and is not threatened by its delay. The reader is asked to wonder at God’s ways with the nation, as Paul does, and not to find the Body’s own place in an olive tree that was never planted for it.
