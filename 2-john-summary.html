2 John

The elder joins truth and love, urging the elect lady and her children to walk in Christ’s commandment while refusing support to deceivers who deny His incarnation.

TestamentNew Testament
GenreGeneral Epistle
RecipientsThe elect lady and her children
Central EmphasisTruth-governed love and discernment
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

The elder rejoices that some of the recipient’s children are walking in truth and renews the command that believers love one another. In John’s reasoning, love is not mere sentiment but walking according to God’s commandments.

Because many deceivers have gone into the world denying Jesus Christ come in the flesh, the recipients must watch themselves, remain in Christ’s teaching, and refuse hospitality that would materially advance the deceivers’ work.

Historical and Biblical Context

Second John shares vocabulary and concerns with 1 John and identifies its author simply as “the elder.” Early testimony and literary relationship support association with John. “The elect lady” may identify a particular Christian woman and household or symbolize a local congregation; interpreters remain divided.

Traveling teachers depended on hospitality. Receiving a teacher into one’s house could include lodging, public welcome, and support for the teacher’s mission. John therefore addresses a practical boundary between brotherly love and complicity in false teaching.

Structure and Outline

  1. Greeting in truth and love — 2 John 1–3
    Truth abides in the faithful, and grace, mercy, and peace accompany truth and love.
  2. Walk in truth and love — 2 John 4–6
    The elder rejoices in obedient children and restates the command to love one another.
  3. Guard against deceivers — 2 John 7–11
    Incarnation-denying teachers must not be allowed to undermine reward or receive mission-advancing support.
  4. Hope for face-to-face fellowship — 2 John 12–13
    The elder reserves further instruction for a personal visit and conveys family greetings.

Major Themes

Truth That Abides

Truth is not temporary information. It abides in the faithful and governs affection, confession, obedience, and fellowship.

Love as Obedient Walking

Love is defined by walking according to God’s commandments, especially the command heard from the beginning to love one another.

The Doctrine of Christ

Faithful teaching confesses Jesus Christ come in the flesh and remains within His teaching. Denial marks the deceiver and antichrist.

Discernment in Hospitality

Love does not finance or endorse destructive teaching. Fellowship in truth requires boundaries against participation in evil work.

Full Reward and Joy

The recipients must guard what has been accomplished so they receive a full reward, while face-to-face fellowship promises completed joy.

Key Passages

2 John 1–3Election, abiding truth, love, grace, mercy, and peace.
2 John 4–6Walking in truth and love according to commandment.
2 John 7Deceivers and denial of Jesus Christ come in the flesh.
2 John 8–9Watchfulness, full reward, and abiding in Christ’s teaching.
2 John 10–11Hospitality withheld from those advancing false doctrine.
2 John 12–13Face-to-face fellowship and concluding greetings.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Acknowledge the recipient question without making it control the letter. Whether an individual household or congregation, the commands concern a real believing community.
  • Do not separate love from truth or obedience. John defines each through the others.
  • “The doctrine of Christ” in context concerns teaching that remains faithful to Jesus Christ and His incarnation, not a slogan for every disputed secondary issue.
  • The hospitality prohibition addresses active support and endorsement of deceiving teachers; it should not be used to forbid ordinary kindness or truthful engagement with unbelievers.
  • John’s warning about losing reward distinguishes faithful continuance and recompense from casual assumptions about profession.
  • The letter’s antichrist horizon and apostolic setting should be retained before principles of discernment are applied elsewhere.

Study Questions

  1. How are truth and love connected in the greeting?
  2. How does John define walking in love?
  3. Which confession distinguishes faithful teaching from deception?
  4. What must the recipients guard, and what is at stake?
  5. Why would hospitality make someone share in a deceiver’s work?
  6. What does John prefer to written communication, and why?

Continue your study.
Mark each use of truth, love, walk, commandment, teaching, abide, and receive; then trace how they define faithful fellowship.
