Song of Solomon

A poetic celebration of mutual desire, covenant love, beauty, exclusivity, longing, and delight between a bride and bridegroom.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreWisdom / Poetry
Primary SettingPoetic scenes associated with court, countryside, garden, and household
Key MovementFrom longing and courtship through union, separation, reunion, and enduring love
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

Song of Solomon celebrates embodied marital love through lyric poetry. The speakers praise one another, express desire, endure absence, seek reunion, and affirm love’s strength and exclusivity. The book treats faithful romantic love as a good gift rather than an embarrassment or merely a vehicle for another subject.

Historical and Biblical Context

The title associates the song with Solomon, though interpreters debate whether he is author, central male figure, royal background, or collection marker. The poetry shifts among a woman, a man, and a chorus often identified as the daughters of Jerusalem. Precise scene divisions are sometimes uncertain, so the literary voices and repeated refrains provide important controls.

Evidence and inference: Poetic and wisdom texts often communicate through voice, imagery, arrangement, and observation. This summary distinguishes explicit textual claims from conclusions about authorship, date, speakers, setting, or literary structure that remain debated.

Structure and Outline

  1. Longing and attraction — Song 1:1–2:7
    The lovers voice desire, admiration, and the first refrain not to awaken love prematurely.
  2. Seeking and anticipation — Song 2:8–3:5
    Springtime invitation and a night search express longing and reunion.
  3. Wedding and delight — Song 3:6–5:1
    Royal procession, extended praise, garden imagery, and mutual invitation portray union.
  4. Separation and renewed praise — Song 5:2–7:9
    A missed encounter leads to searching, suffering, recollection, and renewed admiration.
  5. Mature mutual love — Song 7:10–8:14
    The lovers affirm belonging, desire, exclusivity, and love’s unpurchasable strength.

Major Themes

Mutual Desire and Delight

Both woman and man speak actively, praise one another, and take joy in mutual belonging.

Love, Timing, and Exclusivity

Repeated refrains and garden imagery protect love from premature or intrusive awakening.

Beauty and Embodied Praise

The poetry uses abundant imagery to honor physical presence, character, and attraction.

Absence, Search, and Reunion

Longing intensifies through separation and finds answer in renewed communion.

Love’s Strength

The closing declaration presents faithful love as powerful, enduring, jealous, and beyond purchase.

Key Passages

Song of Solomon 1:2–7The woman voices desire and seeks the beloved’s presence.
Song of Solomon 2:8–17The beloved invites her into springtime joy.
Song of Solomon 2:7; 3:5; 8:4The repeated refrain guards the proper awakening of love.
Song of Solomon 4:1–16The man praises the woman with garden and landscape imagery.
Song of Solomon 5:2–8Separation produces a difficult night search.
Song of Solomon 6:1–3The woman affirms mutual belonging.
Song of Solomon 7:10–13Mutual desire is expressed with initiative and delight.
Song of Solomon 8:6–7Love is declared strong, ardent, and impossible to purchase.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Read the work first as love poetry celebrating a woman and man; symbolic applications should not erase that plain literary subject.
  • Avoid forcing the poems into a detailed dramatic plot when the text leaves speakers or scene changes uncertain.
  • Poetic comparisons are evocative rather than photographic and should be interpreted within ancient imagery.
  • The book’s celebration of desire is framed by mutuality, exclusivity, timing, and committed love.
  • Traditional readings concerning God and His people may offer analogical reflection, but they should be distinguished from the song’s primary human subject.

Study Questions

  1. How do the alternating speakers create mutuality?
  2. What function does the repeated refrain serve?
  3. How do garden and landscape images communicate exclusivity and delight?
  4. What do the search scenes contribute to the movement of the song?
  5. How does Song 8:6–7 summarize the book’s vision of love?
