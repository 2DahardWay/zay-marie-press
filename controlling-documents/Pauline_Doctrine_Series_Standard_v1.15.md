## Ruled by the publisher (2026-10-11)
- **Why a topic series:** Paul's epistles do not fit a commentary series organised by letter. It would be thin, and it would collide with the Acts Commentary Series (Galatians 1–2 against Acts Volumes 3 and 4; Thessalonians against Studies 7 and 8; Ephesians and Colossians on the Mystery against Prophecy & Mystery Volume 2; Galatians 3 against Covenantal Volume 2; Romans 11 against Prophetic Identity Volume 4).
- **Shape (Option 3):** a topic-based series for the large synthesis subjects, plus individual Digital Studies (Mode 1) for the single-term gaps.
- **Name:** Pauline Doctrine Series (not "commentary").
- **Placement:** the Commentary Collection page (`commentary.html#pauline`) as the **eighth** series, outside the Digital Studies catalog, bundles and Library Edition. No separate series page. Volume slugs: `commentary-pauline-0N-<slug>.html`.
- **Order:** the five volumes first; the individual Digital Studies afterwards.

## Volume 1 — Adam and Christ (thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-11)
**Title and subtitle.** Adam and Christ — The Two Heads in Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15, and What Each Passage Answers. About 55 pages.

**Thesis.** Paul sets Adam and Christ side by side twice, and the two passages do not answer the same question. Romans 5:12–21 asks how sin, death and righteousness reached "all men". The parallel of one man's trespass and one man's obedience is universal anthropology, and its scope clauses are read inside Paul's own sentences, including "they which receive" (5:17). 1 Corinthians 15:21–22 and 45–49 ask how the dead are raised and with what body: the first man and the last Adam, the natural and then the spiritual, the earthy and the heavenly image. The volume argues that "in Adam" and "in Christ" name two headships with different membership, that the two passages are not one doctrine said twice, and that neither one enrols Israel's covenants in either head.

**Outline (about 55 pages).** Preface; Thesis; ch. 1 why Paul sets two men side by side (Romans 5:12; 1 Corinthians 15:21); ch. 2 Romans 5 in its place (1:18–5:11); ch. 3 "by one man sin entered" (5:12–14); ch. 4 "the figure of him that was to come" (5:14); ch. 5 offence and gift (5:15–17); ch. 6 one trespass, one righteousness (5:18–19); ch. 7 the law entered, grace did much more abound (5:20–21); ch. 8 1 Corinthians 15 in its place: the gospel and the denial (15:1–20); ch. 9 "in Adam all die… in Christ" and the order (15:21–28); ch. 10 sown and raised (15:35–44); ch. 11 the first man and the last Adam (15:45–49); ch. 12 flesh and blood, and the close (15:50–58); ch. 13 the two passages side by side; ch. 14 what the passages do not say; ch. 15 objections and replies (two to four modern writers who fortify the thesis beside named modern writers who hold the opposite view, closing table); Conclusion; appendices (the passages at a glance, the key words, key passages in full, Where the Passages Are Read); Bibliography. Chapters are added or merged at drafting to reach 50–70 pages.

**Neighbours and narrower question.** Study 49 and Prophecy & Mystery Volume 4 (the order and company of the resurrections; 1 Corinthians 15:23 read narrowly as the Body's own order); the Romans Doctrinal Packet (Romans 5:12–21 as one key passage); Study 59 (the Law's jurisdiction); Study 80 (Genesis 3:15 and the foreordaining passages); Readers Question Series Volume 2 (1 Corinthians 15:29). This volume asks what Paul says each head does for those who are in him, and how the two passages differ in what they answer.

**Rulings (publisher, 2026-10-11, "i approve", all eight as recommended):**
1. Romans 5:12–21 is read as universal anthropology and assigned to no program; Rule 19 applies wherever faith is named.
2. "All" and "many" (5:12, 15, 18–19) are reported clause by clause; the volume does not claim every person is justified, since 5:17 says "receive" and the means is set elsewhere in Romans.
3. The Augustinian, federal and realist readings of 5:12 appear in the objections chapter; the volume builds no doctrine of inherited guilt beyond the text.
4. 1 Corinthians 15:23 follows Prophecy & Mystery Volume 4 ruling 6 (the Body's own order); Study 49 is cited, not restated.
5. 1 Corinthians 15:45–49 is read as the Body's resurrection body (Prophecy & Mystery Volume 4 ruling 11, heavenly glorification); 15:50 is reported without assigning the Body to the Kingdom program.
6. Genesis 1–3 appears only as the background Paul draws on (Genesis 2:7 in 15:45); nothing is said on creation dating or on Genesis 3:15 (Study 80's ground).
7. Romans 5:13–14 and 5:20 are reported beside the Law's jurisdiction (Study 59, cited); the standing Galatians 3 sentence applies if Galatians 3 is touched.
8. If Romans 9–11 is touched, Rule 32 and the Romans 11 triad apply; "two humanities" never means Israel and the Body (Rule 17).

**Voices (as drafted; quoted only from free full texts read in full; otherwise described without quotation marks).** Matt Perman, "What Is the Biblical Evidence for the Imputation of Adam's Sin?" (Desiring God, 23 January 2006), quoted; the article quotes John Piper's *Adam, Christ, and Justification*, whose sermons were not read, so Piper is quoted only as the article quotes him. Thomas L. Constable, *Notes on Romans* and *Notes on 1 Corinthians* (2026 Editions, soniclight.com), quoted. Constable fortifies the thesis on two headships with different membership and on the believers' own rank in 1 Corinthians 15:23, and holds the opposite view on imputed guilt and on adding other ranks to 15:23 from other passages. Two objections (every person is saved; the last Adam makes the church the new Israel) and the one-headship-twice objection are stated in the volume's words with no writer named, because none was read. Murray, Cranfield, Moo, Wright, Gaffin and Thiselton were not read and no view is attributed to them. The exact extracts are in `build-tools/commentary/pauline01/source_extracts.txt`; `srccheck.py` fails the build if a non-KJV quotation is not in that list.

## Volume 2 — The Cross in Paul's Letters (thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-11)
**Title and subtitle.** The Cross in Paul's Letters — What Each Passage Says the Cross Did. About 55 pages.

**Thesis.** Paul gives no single account of the Cross. Passage by passage he names several things it did, each against a different problem: God's righteousness declared at the place of propitiation (Romans 3:21–26), the curse borne (Galatians 3:10–14), one died for all (2 Corinthians 5:14–21), the old man crucified with him (Romans 6; Colossians 2:20), the record taken out of the way and the powers triumphed over (Colossians 2:13–15), the enmity slain and peace made (Ephesians 2:13–16; Colossians 1:20). The volume argues these are not rival theories to choose between and are not one doctrine said six times; each is read as the passage states it.

**Outline (about 55 pages).** Preface; Thesis; ch. 1 how Paul names the Cross; ch. 2 the Cross in the gospel Paul preached (1 Corinthians 15:1–4; 1:18–25); ch. 3 the place of propitiation (Romans 3:21–26); ch. 4 "delivered for our offences" and "died for the ungodly" (Romans 4:25; 5:6–11); ch. 5 the curse (Galatians 3:10–14; 2:20–21; 6:14); ch. 6 one died for all (2 Corinthians 5:14–21); ch. 7 dying with him (Romans 6; Colossians 2:20; 3:3); ch. 8 the record and the powers (Colossians 2:13–15); ch. 9 the blood, the cross and the peace (Ephesians 1:7; 2:13–16; Colossians 1:20; Romans 5:9); ch. 10 offering and sacrifice (Ephesians 5:2; 1 Corinthians 5:7; Romans 8:3, 32; Philippians 2:8; 3:18); ch. 11 clause by clause; ch. 12 the passages side by side (problem, what the Cross did, for whom, result); ch. 13 what the passages do not say; ch. 14 objections and replies (opposing readings: moral influence only; Christus Victor alone; expiation without propitiation; rejection of penal substitution; limited or definite extent; Galatians 3:13 as Israel's exile curse; closing table); Conclusion; appendices (A passages at a glance, B key words, C questions for study, D key passages in full, E Where the Passages Are Read); Bibliography.

**Closer check (2026-10-11, from site pages and Standards; study PDFs are not in the repo).** Study 53 carries "redemption" for Israel and the Body (company, price, timing), Galatians 3:13 and 1 Peter 1:18–19; Study 54 justification (Romans 3:24, 5:1); Study 55 enmity and peace (Romans 5, 2 Corinthians 5, Ephesians 2, Colossians 1); Study 12 the blood as the New Covenant's basis, the Body not a party; Studies 37 and 42 new creation and the one baptism (Romans 6:3); Study 59 the Law's jurisdiction (Colossians 2:14 touches it); Readers Question Series Volume 5, entry 7 (Colossians 2:13–14 on guilt, as a short answer); Covenantal Volume 2 (Galatians 3); Doctrinal Packets Romans, Galatians, Colossians and 2 Corinthians. This volume asks what each Pauline passage says the Cross did, to what, for whom and with what result.

**Rulings (publisher, 2026-10-11, "i approve", all nine as recommended):**
1. Method: one passage, one question (what does this passage say the Cross did, to what, for whom, with what result?); no single theory is imposed.
2. Romans 3:25: "propitiation" as the KJV has it; God sets it forth; "sins that are past" and "forbearance" reported as written; nothing is said of what earlier believers' salvation rested on (Rules 19 and 23).
3. Galatians 3:13: "us" is Paul's own word; "the curse of the law" is the law's curse on those under it (Deuteronomy 27:26 quoted at 3:10); Christ bore it; the volume does not say the Body was under it; closes with the standing Galatians 3 sentence. This is Claude's reading, flagged for the publisher.
4. Romans 6: "baptized into his death" is the Spirit's baptism (Study 42 cited); "crucified with him" (6:6) read as accomplished; "reckon" (6:11) as the response.
5. Colossians 2:14–15: the record is "taken out of the way" for the Body, in Ephesians 2:14 wording; nothing claimed for Israel; the principalities reported with no map or identification; Study 59 cited.
6. Extent: "for us" (Romans 5:8; Galatians 3:13) and "for all" (2 Corinthians 5:14–15; 1 Timothy 2:6) each reported clause by clause; no doctrine of limited or universal extent.
7. Faith and its content: Paul's gospel includes "Christ died for our sins" (1 Corinthians 15:3) and the Body is saved by believing it; Israel's remnant or earlier believers are never said to be saved by it (Rule 19); "unto the Jews a stumblingblock" (1:23) under Rule 18; Acts' preaching of the Cross left out.
8. Blood and the New Covenant: the Body's blood passages (Ephesians 1:7, Colossians 1:14, Romans 5:9) read for the Body; 1 Corinthians 11:25 left to Study 72 and Volume 4; Study 12 cited; the Body is never made a party to Israel's New Covenant.
9. Scope: Hebrews and 1 Peter are outside (the volume covers Paul's letters); Study 53 keeps 1 Peter 1:18–19.

## Volume 3 — The Person of Christ in Paul (thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-11)
**Title and subtitle.** The Person of Christ in Paul — Who He Is, Before and Apart from What He Did. About 55 pages.

**Narrower question.** What does each of Paul's passages say Christ is, in whose terms, and for what purpose in its own argument? The volume does not ask what he did (Volume 2), whom the Body is in him (Volume 1) or what the creeds say.

**Thesis.** Paul says who Christ is in several passages, each in its own terms and against its own problem: in the form of God before he took the form of a servant (Philippians 2:5–11); the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature, before all things and Creator (Colossians 1:15–20; 2:9); God manifest in the flesh (1 Timothy 3:16); the great God and our Saviour (Titus 2:13). The passages are not one creed said five times and none uses later creedal words. Read together without those words, they present one person who is God and who became man, and each leaves unsaid how the two are one.

**Outline (about 55 pages).** Preface; Thesis; ch. 1 how Paul names Christ; ch. 2 Philippians 2:5–11 in its place (2:1–4); ch. 3 "form of God" and "equal with God"; ch. 4 the emptying and the name (Isaiah 45:23 beside 2:10–11); ch. 5 Colossians 1:15–20 in its place (the rivals of 2:8); ch. 6 image and firstborn; ch. 7 "by him … for him … before all things"; ch. 8 the fulness dwelling bodily (1:19; 2:9); ch. 9 Romans 9:5 in its setting; ch. 10 1 Timothy 3:16 in the house of God; ch. 11 Titus 2:13; ch. 12 the man Christ Jesus (1 Timothy 2:5; Galatians 4:4; Romans 1:3–4); ch. 13 clause by clause; ch. 14 the passages side by side; ch. 15 what the passages do not say; ch. 16 objections and replies; Conclusion; Appendices A–E; Bibliography.

**Closer check (2026-10-11, from site pages and Standards; study PDFs are not in the repo).** No study, volume, packet or parable is built on who Christ is. Studies 57 and 69 and Covenantal Volume 4 touch Colossians 1:15–20 for the purpose of God and for heaven and earth; Study 55 reads Colossians 1:19–23 for reconciliation; Study 53 reads Colossians 1:14 and Titus 2:14 for redemption; Study 30 reads Colossians 2:9–10 for the Head and the Body; Study 52 uses "firstborn" only for Israel (Exodus 4:22); Prophecy & Mystery Volume 3 rules on Colossians 1:13; Typology Volume 3 carries 1 Timothy 2:5; the Philippians, Colossians and 1 Timothy Packets list the key passages without arguing them.

**Rulings (publisher, 2026-10-11, "i approve", all ten as recommended):**
1. Method: one passage, one question; no creed is read back into Paul.
2. Philippians 2:6–7: KJV words reported; "form of God" and "equal with God" speak of him before the emptying; no theory of what was given up.
3. Colossians 1:15: "firstborn" is a title of rank, argued from 1:16 and 1:17, not "first created"; what the sentence leaves unsaid is stated; Colossians 1:13 is cited beside Prophecy & Mystery Volume 3 and not re-argued.
4. Colossians 1:20 and the reconciliation of "all things" are left to Volume 2 and Study 55.
5. 1 Timothy 3:16: read as the KJV has it, "God was manifest in the flesh"; the disputed Greek reading is noted fairly in one paragraph; the thesis does not rest on this verse alone.
6. Titus 2:13: argued as one person, "the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ," the other punctuation noted; the volume does not say which coming "the glorious appearing" is.
7. Romans 9:5: a short chapter, read in the setting of Israel's privileges (Rule 32), the punctuation question reported.
8. "Firstborn": Israel's use (Exodus 4:22; Study 52) and Christ's use (Colossians 1:15, 18; Romans 8:29) kept separate; no sonship passes to the Body.
9. Scope: Paul's letters only; John 1 and Hebrews 1 at most as neighbours; no author from before 1900.
10. Objections chapter: at least three modern writers from free full texts read by Claude, some who agree and some who oppose, each reply from Scripture; a view with no free full text is stated in the volume's own words as a tradition's, no writer named.

**Status.** Thesis, outline and rulings approved; drafting in `build-tools/commentary/pauline03/`.

## Volume 4 — The Assembly in Order (thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-11)
**Title and subtitle.** The Assembly in Order — How the Gathered Body Is Ordered, and What Each Instruction Rests On. About 55 pages.

**Narrower question.** How does Paul order the gathered Body, and what does each instruction say it rests on? The volume does not take up the Supper (Study 72), the prayer and tongues passages as prayer (Study 81), the household (Study 48), the identification of an assembly (Study 60), the collection (Study 47) or the crowns (Study 33).

**Thesis.** Paul orders the assembly by separate instructions, and each names its own ground: one Body with many members, edification, the Lord's commandment, creation and the law, the pattern in all the churches, what a leader must be. They are not one rule restated. Where Paul names no ground, the volume says so and builds none.

## Volume 5 — Liberty, Conscience and the Walk (thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-11)
**Title and subtitle.** Liberty, Conscience and the Walk — What Governs the Believer Where Paul Gives No Command, and How He Sets Liberty Against the Weaker Brother's Conscience. About 55 pages.

**Narrower question.** Where Paul gives no command, what governs the believer's conduct, and how does he set liberty against another believer's conscience? **Thesis.** Paul does not give a rule for every case. Where he commands, there is no liberty (1 Corinthians 5; 6:9–10; Galatians 5:19–21). The passages answer different questions and are not merged into one rule such as "do all to the glory of God."
