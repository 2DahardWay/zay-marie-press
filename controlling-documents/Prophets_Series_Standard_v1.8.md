## Proposed (not yet ruled)
- **Why a topic series, and not a walk through the prophetic books:** every prophetic book already has a Bible Book Companion (all 66 are built; the pages still carry the former Doctrinal Packet file names), and the largest single passages are already argued elsewhere: Daniel 9 (Prophetic Commentary Volume 4, Study 10), Isaiah 7:13–16 (Study 63), the Servant in Isaiah 41–53 (Study 86), the day of the LORD and Gog and Magog (Study 7, Prophetic Commentary Volume 5), the remnant (Study 9, Prophetic Identity Volume 2), the New Covenant (Study 12, Prophetic Identity Volume 3), Ezekiel 40–48 (Typology Volume 1), the second exodus (Typology Volume 4) and the Davidic throne (Covenantal Volume 5, Study 73). A series organised by book would be thin and would collide with all of these, for the same reason the Pauline Doctrine Series was organised by topic and not by letter. The series is therefore four volumes (the fourth conditional), each built around one narrower question the prophets themselves raise.

Near-name caution: the Commentary Collection already carries the Prophetic Commentary Series (Revelation and Daniel) and the Prophetic Identity Commentary Series. The Prophets Series differs in subject (the Old Testament prophets as prophets, not the final ruler or Israel's identity), but the three names sit close in the series strip and jump bar. Ruled: the name stands as The Prophets Series, anchor `#prophets`.
- **Placement:** the Commentary Collection page (`commentary.html#prophets`) as the **ninth** series, outside the Digital Studies catalog, bundles and Library Edition. No separate series page. ## Proposed wording (built from the sibling Standards)
- **Scope:** four volumes on the Old Testament prophets (the fourth conditional), each built around one narrower question, not a walk through a book. Where a volume enters ground already argued in another series or in a Digital Study, it cites that work as a neighbour and does not repeat the argument.
- **Length:** 50–70 pages including the cover. **Imprint:** Zay-Marie Press. **Translation:** KJV only. **Typeface:** Roboto.
- **Price:** $14.99 per volume unless the publisher says otherwise. Series bundle and Complete Commentary Library figure: see the pricing item above (ruled).
- **Footer:** "ZAY-MARIE PRESS THE PROPHETS SERIES, VOLUME V • N" (V the volume number within the series, N the page number, restarting at 1 on the Table of Contents page).
- **Cover:** "VOLUME V" upper right, the title, and "THE PROPHETS SERIES" at the bottom; 1600 × 2400 px artwork (2:3), 1024 × 1536 px website covers (.jpg and .webp), full-bleed interior cover page. Artwork supplied by the publisher; Claude removes any badge, sets any missing lettering and shows proofs before the cover is approved.
- **Citation and sources:** passage citation (book, chapter, verse or section); modern sources only (twentieth century and later; no author from before 1900 is cited, quoted or listed in a bibliography); a page number is printed only where the text was read in full; a writer is quoted only from a free full text read, otherwise described in Claude's own words without quotation marks; verse-level index "Where the Passages Are Read" as the last appendix before the Bibliography (Master Standard §20 Rule 29).
- **Preview pages:** four, as Master Standard §20 Rule 29 fixes: Table of Contents, Preface, one page of the argument at work and one page of objections and replies, rendered from the final PDF at 1041 × 1560 px and labelled by interior page number; shown to the publisher and approved before any push.
- **Binding rules:** Rules 7, 11 and 13–19 bind; Rules 8, 12, 20–28 and 30 do not. **Rule 32** (Romans 9–11 and the Romans 10:9–10 formula) binds every volume, so it applies wherever a prophetic text is quoted in Romans 9–11 (Hosea 1–2, Isaiah 10, 28, 29, 59 and Joel 2:32 among them). Standing sentences are carried wherever the passage is touched: Galatians 3 ("The seed is prophetic fulfillment in Christ; the Body receives blessing through Christ alone, not through participation in the Abrahamic covenant."); Rule 17 (the remnant and the Body); Rule 18 (Paul's synagogue ministry); Rule 19 (ground of salvation and content of faith); the Romans 11 triad ("Participation is not identity. Blessing is not covenant. Standing is not program membership."). **Rule 33** (Signs after Acts 28) is not in the Master Standard's Mode 2 binding list; because Volume 4 treats Elijah's signs and the signs of the two witnesses, the standing sentence is carried there as the Framework's own ruling, and the publisher is asked to confirm that reading.
- **Reader-facing text:** a volume opens with a plain statement that it argues a position. The mode label, the Framework, rule numbers and every other working term never appear in anything the reader sees (Rule 14), including text inside diagrams, covers and preview images (OCR pass).
- **Objections chapter:** the Covenantal pattern (two to four modern writers who fortify the thesis beside named modern writers who hold the opposite view, each objection in the opponent's own words, short extracts checked against a free full text, answered from Scripture, closing table).
- **Build toolkit:** `build-tools/commentary/` (build.py, content.py, kjvlib.py, kjvcheck.py, tocmap.py, mkcover.py, mk.sh). KJV text is verified from a source and checked by hash with `kjvcheck.py` before publication.

## Volume 1: How the Prophets Spoke — closer check, thesis, outline and rulings (publisher approval, 2026-10-15)

**Passages and question.** Numbers 12:6–8; Deuteronomy 13:1–5; 18:15–22; 1 Samuel 3:19–21; 1 Kings 22; Jeremiah 18:7–10; 23:9–40; 26:12–19; 28; Ezekiel 13; 33:13–16; Jonah 3–4; Amos 3:7; Hebrews 1:1; 1 Peter 1:10–12; 2 Peter 1:19–21. Question: by the prophets' own statements, how is a word of the LORD known, what can turn an oracle aside, and what does the LORD swear that cannot be turned? Verdict: fit by narrower question.

**Closer check (neighbouring pages read, 2026-10-15).** Prophetic Commentary Vol 2 argues Deuteronomy 13 and 18:20–22 to identify Revelation 13's false prophet and reports Jeremiah 23:25, 27 and 1 Kings 22:23; Volume 1 reads them for the prophet's office, cites Vol 2 and does not re-argue Revelation 13. Acts Commentary Vol 1 argues Deuteronomy 18:15–19 as the prophet like Moses (Acts 3, 7); the Acts application stays there. Study 75 reports Jonah 3:5 and 1 Peter 1:10–12 on what people believed; Volume 1 reads Jonah 3–4 for the conditional word. Prophecy & Mystery Vol 2 reads Amos 3:7 beside the Mystery not being foretold; Volume 1 stays inside that ruling. Study 68 uses Ezekiel 13:9 only as a register of names; Study 67 reads Hebrews 1:1 only as an audience marker. Open ground: Numbers 12:6–8, 1 Samuel 3, Jeremiah 18:7–10, 26 and 28, Ezekiel 33:13–16, 2 Peter 1:19–21.

**Thesis.** The prophets give the tests themselves. A word is the LORD's if it comes from him and not the prophet's heart (Jeremiah 23, Ezekiel 13); it must not lead Israel from the LORD, whatever sign accompanies it (Deuteronomy 13); an announced event must come to pass (Deuteronomy 18:22) unless the LORD has attached a condition (Jeremiah 18:7–10; Jonah 3–4); what the LORD swears is a different kind of word and is never turned back.

**Provisional outline, eight chapters:** (1) the office; (2) the source; (3) the test of loyalty; (4) the test of fulfilment; (5) the conditional word; (6) the sworn word; (7) the New Testament testimony (Hebrews 1:1; 1 Peter 1:10–12; 2 Peter 1:19–21); (8) objections and replies.

**Condition on chapter 6 (found 2026-10-15 by the passage run).** The sworn-word passages are among the most heavily argued in the catalog: Genesis 22:16–18 (Covenantal Vol 1 and 2, with Studies 15 and 71 and the Genesis companion), Psalm 89:34–37 (Study 73, Covenantal Vol 2 and 5), Jeremiah 31:35–37 (Studies 12 and 72, Covenantal Vol 2, Prophetic Identity Vol 3), Jeremiah 33:19–26 (Covenantal Vol 2), Hebrews 6:17–18 (Study 28, Covenantal Vol 1) and 2 Samuel 7:12–16 (five items). Chapter 6 therefore cites these neighbours and does not re-argue them. Its own ground is the form of the swearing as the prophets speak it (Numbers 23:19; Isaiah 55:10–11; Malachi 3:6; Isaiah 54:9–10 with Prophetic Identity Vol 1 as neighbour) and the contrast with the conditional word. A page budget is made at the outline stage; if chapters 1–8 cannot reach 50 pages with the cover without repeating a neighbour, the publisher is told before drafting.

**Rulings (all approved 2026-10-15).**
1. Conditional versus sworn: Jeremiah 18:7–10 governs announced judgment and blessing on nations and is never set against the sworn covenant promises (consistent with Covenantal Vol 1).
2. Jonah: Nineveh's unfulfilled forty days does not make Jonah a false prophet; Jonah 4:2 shows he knew the word was conditional, and Deuteronomy 18:22 is read together with Jeremiah 18:7–10.
3. Amos 3:7 is the LORD's pattern with the prophets about announced events; it does not say everything was revealed to them, and the Mystery stays hid (Prophecy & Mystery Vol 2).
4. 1 Peter 1:10–12 is reported as written; no later revelation is added to the prophets' faith (Rule 19).
5. 1 Kings 22:23: the lying spirit is judgment on Ahab after he refused Micaiah's word (as Prophetic Vol 2).
6. Ezekiel 33:13–16: the "righteousness and life" language is read in Israel's setting and is never made the Body's ground of salvation.
7. Hebrews 1:1 and 2 Peter 1:19–21 are cited on the prophets' mode of speaking, each letter on its own audience markers, with no program assigned beyond that.

Read through its first part only; the closing 15,684 characters and the Deuteronomy 13 treatment are not yet read.

## Volume 2: What the Prophets Condemned — closer check, thesis, outline and rulings (publisher approval, 2026-10-15)

**Passages and question.** 1 Samuel 15:22; Psalm 40:6; 50:7–15; 51:16–19; Isaiah 1:10–20; 58; 66:1–4; Jeremiah 6:20; 7:1–15, 21–23; Hosea 6:6; Amos 4:4–5; 5:21–27; Micah 6:6–8; Zechariah 7; Malachi 1:6–14; 3:1–4; Matthew 9:13; 12:7; Hebrews 10:5–10. Question: what does each prophet condemn, in whose worship, and what does he leave standing? Verdict: clear (the largest gap in the catalog).

**Closer check (2026-10-15).** Nothing argues Hosea 6:6, Psalms 40, 50 and 51, Jeremiah 6:20 and 7, Amos 4, Micah 6, Zechariah 7, Malachi 1 or Hebrews 10:5–10. Isaiah 1:10–20 and 58 are cited in passing (Study 76, Prophetic Identity Vol 2, Study 78). Acts Commentary Vol 1 reports Amos 5:25–27 as Stephen quotes it. Study 24 argues Hebrews 10:1–18 on "no more sacrifice"; Typology Vols 1 and 3 use Hebrews 10:1–4 as the shadow. Matthew 9:13 and 12:7 are crowded (Study 78, Typology Vol 5, Reader Questions Vol 2, Prophecy & Mystery Vols 3 and 5) and are cited here, not argued. Ezekiel 40–48 stays with Typology Vol 1, Malachi 3:8–12 with Study 47, Isaiah 66:23 and the Sabbath with Study 78, Malachi 3:1 (the two messengers) with Volume 4.

**Thesis.** The prophets condemn the worshipers and the way they worship, never the worship the LORD commanded. Each indictment names the offerings as the LORD's appointment, charges the people's conduct and use of the house, and sentences the house and people to judgment (Shiloh, exile), not abolition; the remedy named is turning back, and the offerings reappear in the prophets' own hope (Isaiah 56:7; 66:20–23; Malachi 3:3–4). "Mercy, and not sacrifice" and "I spake not … concerning burnt offerings" are the "not … but" idiom of rank. Hebrews 10 gives the same verdict from the other side, inside Israel's line, and does not place the Body under those sacrifices or that charge.

**Outline, nine chapters (about 58 pages with the cover).** (1) the earlier voices: 1 Samuel 15:22; Psalm 40:6; 50:7–15; 51:16–19; (2) Isaiah 1:10–20; (3) Amos 4:4–5, 5:21–27; (4) Hosea 6:6 and Micah 6:6–8; (5) Jeremiah 6:20, 7:1–15, 21–23; (6) Isaiah 58, 66:1–4, Zechariah 7; (7) Malachi 1:6–14, 3:3–4; (8) Hebrews 10:5–10; (9) objections and replies; Conclusion; appendices.

Amos 5:25 a question about the wilderness years; Acts 7 stays with Acts Commentary Vol 1. 4. Matthew 9:13 and 12:7 cited in their settings. 5. Hebrews 10:5–10 within Israel's line on its own audience markers and the covenant it quotes; Psalm 40:6 reported in both wordings with no theory. 6. Isaiah 58 and 66:1–4 Israel's indictment; Sabbath of 58:13 left to Study 78. 7. Malachi 3:1 left to Volume 4; Volume 2 takes 3:3–4. 8. Psalm 51:16–19 reported as written. 9. Ezekiel 20:25–26 goes in the objections chapter as the opponent's strongest text. 10. No program assigned beyond the passages' own audience; no critique of Christian ritual stated.
