## Volume 2 — Tongues and Prophecy (closer check, thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-17)

**Narrower question.** What were tongues and prophecy, whom did they serve, and where do they end? Verdict: fit by mode and by narrower question.

**Closer check (2026-10-17).** Study 81 reports 1 Corinthians 12–14 for its prayer language and takes no position; this volume argues one. Pauline Volume 4 takes 12:12–31 and 14:26–40 for the order of the assembly and its Standard keeps cessation undecided for that series; this volume cites it and does not re-argue order. Acts Volume 2 reports the four Spirit scenes and holds Acts' tongues as a sign to Israel, with 13:8–10 reported as written; this volume cites Acts 2, 10 and 19 only as instances. Volume 1 (chapter 11.8) already rules on 1 Corinthians 13:10 and says this volume reads 1 Corinthians 12–14 in order. Crowded passages (Acts 2:4, 10:46, 19:6; Ephesians 3:5) are cited, not re-argued.

**Thesis.** The tongues of 1 Corinthians 14 are languages (14:10–11), given as a sign to unbelieving Israel on the authority of Isaiah 28:11–12 (14:21–22). The Corinthian gifts belong to the overlap. They end with the national suspension of Israel's Prophecy Program at Acts 28, so no present practice of tongues is the gift the text describes, and prophecy goes with them. The volume reports 1 Corinthians 13:8–10 exactly as written and leaves "that which is perfect" unidentified, because the ruling rests on 14:21–22, Isaiah 28 and Acts 28.

**Outline (about 54 pages).** Preface; Thesis; ch. 1 the question and the method; ch. 2 the words ("tongue", "prophesy", "interpret", the italic "unknown"); ch. 3 Isaiah 28:11–12 in its own chapter; ch. 4 tongues in Acts 2, 10 and 19 (instances only); ch. 5 1 Corinthians 12; ch. 6 1 Corinthians 13; ch. 7 1 Corinthians 14:1–19; ch. 8 14:20–25; ch. 9 14:26–40 (cited to Pauline Volume 4); ch. 10 prophecy (1 Thessalonians 5:20; 1 Timothy 1:18, 4:14; Acts 21:9–11; Ephesians 3:5 and 4:11 left to Volume 4); ch. 11 the end at Acts 28 (Rule 33 applied); ch. 12 what the volume does not say; ch. 13 objections and replies with a closing table; Conclusion; Appendices A–E (passages at a glance, key words, questions, key passages in full, Where the Passages Are Read); Bibliography.

**Rulings (publisher, 2026-10-17, "i approve", all four as recommended):**
1. The Corinthian gifts are overlap phenomena reported as written under the standing overlap wording, their sign function argued from 14:21–22, not assigned to Israel's program, and ending at Acts 28.
2. Prophecy is stated to have ended with the sign program, argued from "prophecies shall fail" (13:8, as written), from 14:30 (prophecy as a revealed word) and from the Acts 28 suspension; no verse says prophecy ended at Acts 28 and the volume says so; the Ephesians 3:5 question stays with Volume 4.
3. The private-prayer-language passages (14:2, 4, 14–15 and 13:1) are read as the same tongues (13:1 as a conditional, like 13:2); no present practice of tongues is the biblical gift.
4. Modern writers: fortifying voices Thomas, Gaffin and Schreiner; opposing writers Fee, Carson, Grudem (adding his work on prophecy) and Keener; positions described, not quoted.

## Volume 3 — Healing in the Body (closer check, thesis, outline and rulings 1–4 approved 2026-10-17; built, 55 pages with the cover, interior and cover approved)

**Subtitle (publisher, 2026-10-17, sent mid-turn as a bare phrase and taken as the subtitle).** *The Sign-Gift of Healing and the Body’s Non-Sign Pattern.* (My earlier default was “Whether Healing Is a Gift in the Body Today.”) The supplied cover (5120 × 7680, lettered "VOLUME 3", "Healing in the Body", the series line) carries no subtitle.

**Narrower question.** Does any passage addressed to the Body give healing as a gift, an authority or a promise, and what do the letters addressed to the Body do with sickness? Verdict: fit by narrower question (1 Corinthians 12:9 and 12:28 are crowded chapters, argued in Pauline Volume 4 and in Volume 2 chapter 5; this volume takes only the healing clauses).

**Closer check (2026-10-17).** Volume 1 chapter 9.4 reports Epaphroditus (Philippians 2:25–27), Timothy's stomach (1 Timothy 5:23) and Trophimus (2 Timothy 4:20) as three facts with no healing recorded, and promises that this volume takes healing and the Body "in full"; this volume reads the three passages with the rest of what the letters say of sickness and does not repeat chapter 9.4's table. Volume 2 chapter 5 reads the two gift lists of 1 Corinthians 12 for their order; this volume reads "gifts of healings" (12:9, 28, 30) as one clause and cites chapter 5 for the lists. Pauline Volume 4 argues 12:9 and 12:28 for the order of the assembly and keeps cessation undecided; this volume cites it. Study 86 argues Isaiah 53 as the servant passage; this volume reads 53:4–5 only beside Matthew 8:17 and 1 Peter 2:24. Study 38 reports Acts and the three companion passages without a position; Study 81 and Study 25 do not treat healing; Readers Question Volume 5 treats the thorn (2 Corinthians 12:7–10) as unanswered prayer in a topical answer. James 5:14–16 is argued by no product (the James companion only); Matthew 8:17 is reported by Study 86 and one Readers Question. Acts 28:8–9 is left to Volume 1 chapter 10.

**Thesis.** Healing in Scripture is given to a hearer and for a purpose the passage names. In the Gospels and Acts 1–8 it confirms a messenger to Israel, and James 5:14–16 is written to the twelve tribes (James 1:1) and read in that setting. The "gifts of healings" of 1 Corinthians 12 belong to the Corinthian overlap and end with the national suspension of Israel's Prophecy Program at Acts 28 (Rule 33), as Volumes 1 and 2 argue for the sign program as a whole. In the letters addressed to the Body, sickness is met with prayer, with ordinary care and with God's mercy (Philippians 2:27; 1 Timothy 5:23; 2 Timothy 4:20; 2 Corinthians 12:7–10), and no letter gives the Body a healing ministry, an authority to heal or a promise of healing. Isaiah 53:4–5, Matthew 8:17 and 1 Peter 2:24 are read in their three settings and do not join into a healing in the atonement. The Body may pray for the sick; whether anyone recovers is God's sovereign mercy, never a continuation of the Kingdom signs, and the volume makes no statement about God's power.

**Outline (about 54 pages).** Preface; Thesis; ch. 1 the question and the method; ch. 2 the words (iaomai, therapeuo, astheneo, sozo at James 5:15, aleipho and chrio, "gifts of healings"); ch. 3 healing in Israel's Scriptures (Exodus 15:26; Psalm 103:3; 2 Kings 5 and 20; Isaiah 53); ch. 4 the Lord's healings and Matthew 8:17; ch. 5 the Twelve and the Seventy sent to heal (Matthew 10:8; Luke 10:9) and healing in Acts 1–8; ch. 6 healing in Acts 9–28 (Acts 14:8–10; 19:11–12; 28:8–9, cited to Volume 1); ch. 7 "gifts of healings" in 1 Corinthians 12:9, 28, 30; ch. 8 James 5:13–20 in its setting; ch. 9 what the letters to the Body do with sickness (Philippians 2:25–30; 1 Timothy 5:23; 2 Timothy 4:20; Galatians 4:13–14; Philippians 4:6); ch. 10 2 Corinthians 12:7–10 and the grace sufficient; ch. 11 1 Peter 2:24 and "by whose stripes"; ch. 12 the end of the healing gift at Acts 28 and what the Body does (Rule 33's expanded text); ch. 13 what the volume does not say; ch. 14 objections and replies with a closing table; Conclusion; Appendices A–E; Bibliography.

**Proposed rulings (for the publisher's approval):**
1. "Gifts of healings" (1 Corinthians 12:9, 28, 30) are overlap phenomena, reported under the standing overlap wording, not assigned to Israel's program, and they end at Acts 28; the volume says that no verse states that healing ended at Acts 28, and the ruling is an inference from the sign program's end (Volume 1 ch. 10, Volume 2 ch. 11).
2. James 5:14–16 is read in the setting James 1:1 fixes (the twelve tribes; "the coming of the Lord" at 5:7–8; the assembly of 2:2); it is not carried to the Body, and the volume's statement that the Body may pray for the sick rests on the letters addressed to the Body (Philippians 4:6; 2 Corinthians 12:8), not on James.
3. Isaiah 53:4–5, Matthew 8:17 and 1 Peter 2:24 are three settings: Matthew names the Galilean healings as the fulfilment; Peter writes to the dispersion (1 Peter 1:1) and ties "healed" to sins and straying (2:24–25). No healing in the atonement is stated and none is denied beyond what the passages say.
4. The thorn (2 Corinthians 12:7–10) is read as opposition (the messenger of Satan buffeting Paul), as in the Readers Question ruling, with "my grace is sufficient" as the answer to Paul's prayer; Galatians 4:13–14 is reported as written.
5. Modern writers (positions described, not quoted, named only where a source read in full or a free full text supports it): fortifying Thomas, Gaffin and Warfield; opposing Grudem, Keener, Storms and Carson; Fee noted for holding both that gifts continue and that healing is not promised in the atonement.

**Rulings recorded (publisher, 2026-10-17; rulings 1–4 approved as stated, ruling 5 not yet answered, drafted to the proposed default):**
1. Healing ends programmatically at Acts 28. No verse states it; the program ends, and the gift ends with it. Healing is a sign-gift, sign-gifts belong to Israel's program, and the gift ends when the sign-program ends (Volume 1, Rule 33).
2. James 5 is Israel's passage. The Body prays for the sick because Paul commands prayer (Philippians 4:6; 2 Corinthians 12:8), not because James promises healing. The anointing, the elders and the promised raising are Kingdom signs, not norms of the Mystery.
3. Isaiah 53, Matthew 8 and 1 Peter 2:24 stand in different settings and are not merged: Isaiah 53:4–5 is Israel's national atonement prophecy; Matthew 8:17 applies it to the Lord's earthly healing ministry to Israel; 1 Peter 2:24 applies the cross to the believing remnant's sins, not to physical healing. None promises healing to the Body.
4. The thorn is opposition, not sickness. Paul asked for its removal and the answer was not removal but "My grace is sufficient." That is the Body's pattern: sufficiency, not removal, standing against any claim of healing gifts in the Mystery. The volume reports the text of 12:7–9 as written and states the pattern as its reading of that text.

## Volume 4 — Apostles and Prophets (Fit Check, closer check, thesis, outline and rulings 2026-10-17; rulings given by the publisher the same day; nothing drafted)

**Narrower question.** Do the offices of apostle and prophet continue, and what do the passages mean by each word? Verdict: fit by narrower question. No product asks it. Conditional on the page count (below).

**Fit Check (2026-10-17).** Clear or thin ground: Romans 16:7 (cited only in the Romans companion); 2 Corinthians 11:5, 11:13 and 12:11–12 (Study 1 and Study 64 touch them); Revelation 2:2 (Study 26); Acts 14:4, 14; 1 Corinthians 4:9, 9:5–6, 15:7; 1 Thessalonians 2:6; Philippians 2:25 and 2 Corinthians 8:23 as the word apostolos; the New Testament prophets (Acts 11:27–28; 13:1; 15:32; 21:9–11) as persons; Luke 11:49; Hebrews 3:1. Crowded ground, cited and not re-argued: Matthew 10:1–8 and 19:28 (Gospel Volumes 2 and 4, Prophecy & Mystery Volume 3); Acts 1:21–26 (Acts Volume 1 chapter 3, which reads Matthias and the Twelve in full); Galatians 1–2 and Paul's call (Acts Volume 3, Studies 18 and 66); Ephesians 4:11–13 and 1 Corinthians 12:28, 14:29–37 (Pauline Volume 4 chapter 5, Sign Gifts Volumes 2 and 3); Ephesians 2:20 and 3:5 (about six products); Revelation 21:14 (Studies 1, 64 and 69, Prophetic Identity Volumes 1 and 5).

**Closer check.** (1) Sign Gifts Volume 1 chapter 9.3 reads 2 Corinthians 12:12 only as the signs wrought among the Corinthians and leaves "the signs of an apostle" to this volume; chapter 11.9 already answers the objection that the signs ended with the apostles (Gaffin, MacArthur, Thomas, Schreiner) and says Ephesians 2:20 states no date; this volume cites chapter 11.9 and does not repeat it. (2) Volume 2 chapter 10.6 and Volume 3 chapter 13.6 each send "the offices of apostle and prophet" and "what became of them" here; Volume 2 asks whether prophecy as a gift is the same as the office, so this volume asks about the persons and the office, not the gift. (3) Pauline Volume 4 chapter 5 reads the three lists, treats Ephesians 4:11 as gifts and not appointments, reports Ephesians 2:20 without a date and keeps cessation undecided; this volume takes the persons in those lists and cites it for the order of the assembly. (4) Acts Volume 1 chapter 3 reads Matthias; this volume cites it and reads only the qualifications as a test of who may be called an apostle. (5) Acts Volume 3 reads Paul's call, Barnabas and the Gentile commission; this volume cites it and reads the marks Paul himself claims in his letters.

**Proposed thesis.** Scripture uses "apostle" of three kinds of person and "prophet" of two. The Twelve are chosen by the Lord on earth, sent to Israel and promised thrones (Matthew 10:1–8; 19:28; Revelation 21:14). Paul is the apostle of the Gentiles, the last to see the risen Lord (1 Corinthians 15:8) and the steward of the mystery (Ephesians 3:2–5), and he names the marks of his apostleship: the sight of the Lord, the work and the signs (1 Corinthians 9:1–2; 2 Corinthians 12:12). Others are called apostles in the plain sense of sent ones (Acts 14:14; Romans 16:7; Philippians 2:25) and no sign or foundation is claimed for them. The apostles and prophets on whom the household of God is built (Ephesians 2:20) are those to whom the mystery was revealed (3:5); the foundation is the revelation, laid once, and no verse names a successor to either office. The office of the Twelve and Paul's office as sign-authenticated witness do not continue; Paul's letters and the Body's sent ones do. The volume makes no statement about God's power (Rule 33).

**Outline (about 52 pages with the cover).** Cover (1); Table of Contents (1); Preface; Thesis (about 2). Ch. 1 the question and the method (2); ch. 2 the word apostolos and the sent one (John 13:16; Hebrews 3:1; 2 Corinthians 8:23; Philippians 2:25) (2); ch. 3 the Twelve and the qualifications, Matthias cited (Luke 6:13; Matthew 10; 19:28; Acts 1:21–26) (3); ch. 4 others called apostles (Acts 14:4, 14; Romans 16:7; 1 Corinthians 9:5–6; 15:7; Galatians 1:19; 1 Thessalonians 2:6) (3); ch. 5 Paul's apostleship and its marks (Romans 1:1; 11:13; 1 Corinthians 9:1–2; 15:8–10; Galatians 1:1, 11–12; 2:7–9; 1 Timothy 2:7) (3); ch. 6 the signs of an apostle (2 Corinthians 12:11–12; Romans 15:18–19; Hebrews 2:3–4 cited) with Rule 33's standing sentence stated once (3); ch. 7 false apostles and the test (2 Corinthians 11:4–5, 12–15; Revelation 2:2) (2); ch. 8 the prophets of the New Testament as persons (Acts 11:27–28; 13:1; 15:32; 21:9–11; Luke 11:49; 1 Corinthians 14 cited to Volume 2) (3); ch. 9 the foundation (Ephesians 2:20; 3:5; Colossians 1:25–26; 1 Corinthians 3:10–11; Revelation 21:14 kept apart) (4); ch. 10 the gifts of the ascended Christ (Ephesians 4:8–13, persons only, cited to Pauline Volume 4) (3); ch. 11 what continues and what does not (3); ch. 12 what the volume does not say (1); ch. 13 objections and replies with a closing table (5). Conclusion (1); Appendix A key passages (5); Appendix B Where the Passages Are Read (6); Bibliography and imprint (2).
Total about 52 (range 50–56). Margin over the 50-page floor is thin. The chapters that rest on cited ground (9, 10, 11) are the ones most likely to shrink at drafting; if the chapter drafts run short the series closes at three volumes (bundle $34.99, Library re-ruled).

**Rulings recorded (publisher, 2026-10-17; they replace the proposed defaults above where they differ):**
1. The apostolic office as a sign-authenticated witness ends with the sign program at Acts 28. Paul's authority continues in his post-Acts-28 letters.
2. The Twelve's office is suspended with Israel's program at Acts 28 and resumes in the Kingdom.
3. The prophets of Ephesians 2:20 and 3:5 are Mystery-era prophets: not the Twelve, not Old Testament prophets and not Israel's Kingdom prophets. (This replaces the proposed wording "persons to whom the mystery was revealed alongside the apostles"; the thesis sentence on the foundation is read with it.)
4. Ephesians 4:13 is not dated. The volume reports the verse and leaves the date open; it does not extend apostles or prophets beyond Acts 28.
5. Romans 16:7 is reported in the KJV wording; the volume decides nothing on whether Andronicus and Junia held the office.
6. Modern writers (described, not quoted; named only where a source read in full supports it): Gaffin, Thomas and Schreiner for the thesis; Grudem and Carson against it.
