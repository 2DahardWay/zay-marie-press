## Ruled by the publisher (2026-10-09)
- **Name:** Gospel Commentary Series.
- **Scope:** five volumes on the four Gospels, each built around one narrower question, not a verse-by-verse walk through a Gospel, so the series reads the Lord's ministry before the Cross through the two-program lens (what was told and done for Israel). Where a volume enters ground already argued in another series or in a Digital Study, it cites that work as a neighbour and does not repeat the argument.
- **Length:** 50–70 pages including the cover. **Imprint:** Zay-Marie Press. **Translation:** KJV only. **Typeface:** Roboto.
- **Price:** $14.99 per volume unless the publisher says otherwise. Series bundle $54.99 (individually $74.95; save $19.96). The Complete Commentary Library price for 35 volumes is **not yet ruled**; until the publisher rules it and the series is added to the Commentary Collection page, the page keeps the 30-volume price ($274.99).
- **Footer:** "ZAY-MARIE PRESS GOSPEL COMMENTARY SERIES, VOLUME V • N" (page number restarts at 1 on the Table of Contents page).
- **Cover:** "VOLUME V" upper right, the title, and "GOSPEL COMMENTARY SERIES" at the bottom; 1600 × 2400 px artwork (2:3), 1024 × 1536 px website covers (.jpg and .webp), full-bleed interior cover page. Artwork supplied by the publisher; Claude removes any badge and sets any missing lettering.
- **Citation and sources:** passage citation, modern sources only, verse-level index "Where the Passages Are Read" as the last appendix before the Bibliography (Master Standard §20 Rule 29).
- **Placement:** Commentary Collection page (`commentary.html#gospel`), outside the Digital Studies catalog, bundles and Library Edition. No separate series page. Volume slugs: `commentary-gospel-0N-<slug>.html`.
- **Preview pages:** four, as the Master Standard §20 Rule 29 (v1.81) fixes: the Table of Contents, the Preface, one page of the argument at work and one page of objections and replies, rendered from the final PDF; the method and objections pages are shown to the publisher with their interior page numbers and approved with the preview.
- **Binding rules:** Rules 7, 11 and 13–19 bind; Rules 8, 12 and 20–28 do not.
- **Objections chapter:** the Covenantal pattern (two to four modern writers who fortify the thesis beside named modern writers who hold the opposite view, each objection in the opponent's own words, answered from Scripture, closing table).

Publisher rulings, 2026-10-09: (a) **John 14:2–3** is reported as written, to the eleven in the Upper Room, and placed in the Prophecy line's hope with no claim that it is the Body's gathering; the Body's gathering passages are named only as a contrast; (b) **John 15, the vine** is read within Israel's line (the vine of Psalm 80, Isaiah 5, Jeremiah 2:21), spoken to the eleven; the branch warnings stay with them and are not made warnings to the Body, and the Romans 11 olive-tree sentences are not drawn in; (c) **John 17:20–21** ("them also which shall believe on me through their word") is read as the believers who come through the Twelve's word (Acts 1–8 and the circumcision ministry); the Body is not named in it, and the passage is left open on anything beyond that. ## Volume 1 — The Sermon on the Mount: The Kingdom's Standard for Israel (thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-09)
**Title and subtitle.** The Sermon on the Mount — The Kingdom's Standard for Israel, Not the Body of Christ. About 55 pages.

**Thesis.** Matthew 5–7 opens with the Lord teaching "his disciples" with the multitudes listening (5:1–2; 7:28). Its promises are the Kingdom's (the poor in spirit have "the kingdom of heaven," 5:3; the meek "inherit the earth," 5:5) and its terms are those of Israel's Kingdom before the Cross (gifts at the altar, 5:23–24; the law and the prophets, 5:17–20 and 7:12; "ye shall not enter," 5:20; conditional forgiveness, 6:14–15; the judgment of 7:21–23). The sermon states the standard of the promised Kingdom for those Israel was told to prepare; its terms are program-specific and do not pass to the Body. The Body's conduct is taught in Paul's letters; the two are contrasted and never merged. The volume does not say the sermon's wisdom is false, only that it is not addressed to the Body as its rule.

**Outline (about 55 pages).** Preface; Thesis; ch. 1 setting and audience (4:17–5:2; 7:28–29); ch. 2 the Beatitudes and the Kingdom's promises (5:3–12); ch. 3 salt, light and the law (5:13–20; Study 62 cited as a neighbour); ch. 4 the six "ye have heard" sayings (5:21–48); ch. 5 alms, prayer and fasting, with the Lord's Prayer (6:1–18); ch. 6 treasure, anxiety and "seek ye first the kingdom" (6:19–34); ch. 7 judging, asking and the two ways (7:1–14); ch. 8 the fruit, the "I never knew you" judgment and the two builders (7:15–29); ch. 9 Luke 6:17–49 beside Matthew; ch. 10 what the sermon does not say; ch. 11 what Paul's letters say of the Body's conduct; ch. 12 objections and replies (closing table); Conclusion; appendices (scenes at a glance, key passages in full, Where the Passages Are Read); Bibliography.

**Neighbours and narrower question.** Study 62 (5:17–20 only), Study 59 (the Law's jurisdiction), Study 77 (Romans 13), Prophecy & Mystery Volume 3 and Study 2 (the Kingdom offered to Israel), and the Parable Studies (the builders and similar sayings). This volume asks to whom each part is addressed, and what the text says it is for.

**Rulings (publisher, 2026-10-09, "i approve", all eight as recommended):**
1. 5:1–2 and 7:28 are reported as written; the disciples are the Lord's followers in Israel before the Cross, and the Body is not read into them.
2. "The kingdom of heaven" is the Kingdom promised to Israel; 5:5 is the earthly inheritance.
3. 5:20, 6:12–15 and 7:21–23 are reported as the Kingdom's terms for Israel; never converted into conditions for the Body; faith stays the means of salvation (Rule 19).
4. 5:31–32, 5:38–42 and the oaths are reported in their setting, with no added doctrine on divorce, war or policing.
5. The Lord's Prayer (6:9–13) is read as given to the disciples in Israel's setting ("Thy kingdom come"); the volume does not forbid anyone to pray it.
6. Luke 6:17–49 is set beside Matthew with every difference reported; no claim of the same occasion and no harmonizing.
7. Ephesians 4–6 and Colossians 3 are set alongside as contrast only, with no merger.
8. The standing Romans 11 and Galatians 3 sentences apply wherever those texts are touched.

**Objections chapter.** Two to four modern writers who support the thesis beside named opponents, quoted only from free full texts; likely supporters Walvoord, Toussaint, Ryrie; likely opponents Carson, Stott, Wright, Hagner. Voices fixed at drafting; any not read in full are described without quotation marks.

**Status.** Plan approved 2026-10-09; interior (54 pages including the cover) approved 2026-10-09; the cover was replaced on 2026-10-09 at the publisher’s request with new publisher artwork (a Galilean hillside above the lake, the Lord seated among his disciples and the multitudes, supplied 2748 × 4096 px, cropped to 2:3 and set at 1600 × 2400 px), lettered by Claude in cream-and-gold serif capitals: “VOLUME 1” at the upper right, “THE SERMON ON THE MOUNT”, “THE KINGDOM’S STANDARD FOR ISRAEL, NOT THE BODY OF CHRIST” and “GOSPEL COMMENTARY SERIES” at the bottom; the first painted cover (mosaic frame) is retired; modern voices used: Constable and Walvoord (supporting), Piper and Crump (opposing); web page live at `commentary-gospel-01-the-sermon-on-the-mount.html`, shown on the Commentary Collection page (`#gospel`) before the Acts series.

## Volume 2 — The Lord’s Ministry to Israel: Sent Only to the Lost Sheep (thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-09)

**Title and subtitle.** The Lord’s Ministry to Israel: Sent Only to the Lost Sheep — Why the Lord’s Earthly Ministry Was to the Nation. About 55 pages.

**Thesis.** Each Gospel reports the Lord, and then the Twelve and the Seventy, sent to Israel: to the lost sheep of the house of Israel, to announce the Kingdom, confirm the promises made to the fathers and call the nation to repent. The meetings with the Canaanite woman, the centurion, the Samaritans and the Greeks are reported as exceptions that show the order and do not abolish it. The Body of Christ is not in the earthly ministry. The volume does not limit the reach of the Cross. Narrower question: what does each Gospel report the Lord and the Twelve being sent to do, and to whom?

**Outline (about 55 pages).** Preface; Thesis (six claims); ch. 1 why he came (Matthew 1:21; 2:6; Luke 1:54–55, 68–75; John 1:11, 31); ch. 2 the shepherd and the lost sheep (Ezekiel 34; Matthew 9:36; 10:6; 15:24); ch. 3 the Twelve sent (Matthew 10; Luke 9; Matthew 19:28); ch. 4 the Seventy and the cities (Luke 10; Matthew 11:20–24); ch. 5 John the Baptist; ch. 6 “a minister of the circumcision” (Romans 15:8–12); chs. 7–10 the exceptions (Canaanite woman, centurion, Samaria, the Greeks and Nazareth); ch. 11 what the Cross changes within the Gospels (Matthew 26:28; Luke 24:46–47; John 12:32); ch. 12 what the volume does not say; ch. 13 reading it today without merging it with the Body; ch. 14 objections and replies (closing table); Conclusion; appendices (scenes at a glance, key words counted, study questions, six-session guide, FAQ, terms, key passages in full, Where the Passages Are Read); Bibliography.

**Neighbours.** Study 2 and Prophecy & Mystery Volume 3 (the Kingdom offered and refused; not restated); Studies 66, 41 and 75; Acts Commentary Volume 1.

**Rulings (publisher, 2026-10-09, “i approve”, all eight as recommended):**
1. Matthew 15:24 is reported as the Lord’s own answer, with the woman’s “great faith” (15:28) beside it; no shift between programs is drawn.
2. The centurion and Matthew 8:11–12 are read as the Kingdom’s table with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob: Gentiles come in under Israel’s King, “the children of the kingdom” are warned, the Body is not named.
3. John 4 reports “salvation is of the Jews” (4:22) and “the Saviour of the world” (4:42) as written, with Matthew 10:5 beside it; Acts 8 is left to the Acts series.
4. John 12:20–24 is reported as written; the text does not say the Greeks were received and the volume does not say it.
5. John 10:16 and 11:51–52 (“other sheep”, “children … scattered abroad”): both readings are reported and the question is left open.
6. Matthew 10:23 is read as the mission to Israel’s cities continuing until the King’s return; the objection is answered in the objections chapter.
7. Romans 15:8–12 is read as the Lord’s earthly ministry to Israel with the Gentiles rejoicing with Israel; the standing Romans 11 sentences apply (participation is not identity; blessing is not covenant; standing is not program membership).
8. John 3:16–17 and 1 John 2:2 are reported as written and denied nowhere; salvation is by faith in every case and the content of faith is what had been revealed to that group, with no later revelation read back (Rules 19 and 23 of the Digital Studies are the model; Rule 19 binds).

**Objections chapter.** Two to four modern writers on each side, quoted only from free full texts read; likely supporters Constable, Walvoord, Toussaint; likely opponents Köstenberger, Carson, Piper. Voices fixed at drafting; any not read in full are described without quotation marks.

**Status.** Plan approved 2026-10-09; interior (56 pages including the cover; fourteen chapters and Appendices A–I) and publisher-supplied cover approved 2026-10-09; page `commentary-gospel-02-the-lords-ministry-to-israel.html`, Commentary Collection tile and sitemap entry prepared for the push. ## Volume 3 — The Olivet Discourse: Israel’s Tribulation, Israel’s Signs, Israel’s King (thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-09)

**Title and subtitle.** The Olivet Discourse: Israel’s Tribulation, Israel’s Signs, Israel’s King — The Kingdom Horizon of the King’s Last Teaching to His Disciples. About 55 pages.

**Thesis.** The Olivet Discourse is the King’s private answer to four Jewish disciples (Mark 13:3) on the Mount of Olives, about the temple, the sign of his coming and the end of the age. Its audience, geography and terms are Israel’s (Judaea, the sabbath day, the holy place, “Daniel the prophet”, “this gospel of the kingdom”, the elect, the Son of man’s coming on the clouds). The great tribulation is Israel’s (Jeremiah 30:7; Daniel 12:1). The Body is not in the discourse; its hope is taught in Paul’s letters. Matthew, Mark and Luke are set side by side with every difference reported, and the discourse points to the King’s return to the earth, not to the Body’s gathering.

**Outline (about 55 pages).** Preface; Thesis (six claims); ch. 1 the setting and the question (Matthew 24:1–3; Mark 13:1–4; Luke 21:5–7); ch. 2 the three accounts side by side; ch. 3 the beginning of sorrows (24:4–8); ch. 4 persecution and “this gospel of the kingdom” (24:9–14); ch. 5 the abomination of desolation and Daniel (24:15; Daniel 9:27; 11:31; 12:11); ch. 6 the flight from Judaea (24:16–22); ch. 7 false christs and the visible coming (24:23–28); ch. 8 the coming and the gathering of the elect (24:29–31); ch. 9 the fig tree and “this generation” (24:32–35); ch. 10 no man knows the day; Noah and “one taken, one left” (24:36–44); ch. 11 Matthew 25 (neighbours’ rulings cited); ch. 12 what the volume does not say; ch. 13 reading the discourse today; ch. 14 objections and replies (closing table); Conclusion; appendices A–I (including key passages in full and Where the Passages Are Read); Bibliography.

**Neighbours.** Study 29 and Typology Volume 2 (“one taken, one left”); Study 74 (the man of sin); Prophecy & Mystery Volume 5 (the courts; Matthew 25); Studies 7 and 8 (the Day of the Lord, the Day of Christ); Acts Commentary Volume 1. This volume asks what each account says, in order, and what it leaves open.

**Rulings (publisher, 2026-10-09, “i approve”, all eight as recommended):**
1. The discourse is reported as private (Matthew 24:3; Mark 13:3), to four disciples in Israel’s setting before the Cross; the Body is not read in.
2. The three accounts are set side by side with every difference reported; Luke 21 stands under the standing neutral ruling (the AD 70 section and the future section are stated; debated points reported neutrally); nothing forced into one sequence.
3. “This gospel of the kingdom” (24:14) is the Kingdom message of Matthew 10:7, preached before the end; not Paul’s gospel and not the church’s mission.
4. The abomination of desolation (24:15) names Daniel and is argued to be the Antichrist’s act in the temple at the midpoint of Daniel’s seventieth week (the series’ Revelation 13 / little-horn identification); Study 74 is a neighbour only; argued as a position in the objections chapter.
5. The flight (Judaea, sabbath day, winter) and the great tribulation are Israel’s; “the elect” are Israel’s elect (Isaiah 45:4; 65:9); none of it passes to the Body.
6. “One taken, one left” (24:40–41) is Israel’s Tribulation separation, not a rapture; Study 29 and Typology Volume 2 are cited, not restated.
7. The coming of 24:29–31 is the Son of man’s return to the earth; the fig tree is Israel; “this generation” is the generation that sees these things; other readings are reported in the objections chapter; no date is set (24:36).
8. Matthew 25 cites Prophecy & Mystery Volume 5’s rulings without re-arguing them: 24:45–25:30 is Israel’s and not the judgment seat of Christ; 25:31–46 is the living nations at the King’s return, with “my brethren” Israel’s company.

**Objections chapter.** Two to four modern writers on each side, quoted only from free full texts read; likely supporters Toussaint, Walvoord, Constable; likely opponents France (fulfilled in AD 70), Hendriksen, Carson. Voices fixed at drafting; any not read in full are described without quotation marks. Objections: fulfilled in AD 70; addressed to the church and its elect; “taken” means raptured; “this generation” failed; Matthew 24:14 is the church’s mission.

**Status.** Plan approved 2026-10-09; interior (57 pages) and supplied cover approved 2026-10-09; page `commentary-gospel-03-the-olivet-discourse.html` prepared (previews: interior pages 1, 2, 20 and 34).

## Volume 4 — What the Twelve Understood: The Road to the Cross (thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-09)

**Title and subtitle.** What the Twelve Understood: The Road to the Cross — How the Twelve Heard the Prophets, and What Was Not Yet Given. About 55 pages.

**Thesis.** Each Gospel reports the Twelve hearing the Lord through Israel's prophets. They looked for a King and a Kingdom for Israel, and that hope is never called wrong. What they did not grasp was the suffering, death and rising before the glory, told them three times and "hid from them" (Luke 18:34). Understanding came after the resurrection, when the Lord opened the Scriptures (Luke 24:25–27, 44–46). The volume reports what each Gospel says the disciples heard, grasped and missed, and does not read the Body's calling or Paul's revelation back into what they knew. Narrower question: what does each Gospel report the disciples hearing, grasping and missing before the Cross?

**Outline (about 55 pages).** Preface; Thesis (six claims); ch. 1 what the Twelve had been told (the Kingdom hope in the prophets and the Baptist); ch. 2 Peter's confession (Matthew 16:13–20); ch. 3 the first announcement of the Cross and the rebuke (16:21–23); ch. 4 the second and third announcements (Mark 9:30–32; Luke 9:44–45; 18:31–34); ch. 5 the request for thrones (Matthew 20:20–28; Luke 22:24–30); ch. 6 "hid from them" (Luke 18:34); ch. 7 what the Upper Room table promised and did not yet explain (John 13:7; 16:12 only; the rest left to Volume 5); ch. 8 the scattering and the denial (Matthew 26:31–35, 56, 69–75); ch. 9 the empty tomb (John 20:9; 2:22; 12:16); ch. 10 the road to Emmaus (Luke 24:13–35); ch. 11 the Lord opens the Scriptures (Luke 24:36–49); ch. 12 what this volume does not say; ch. 13 reading the Gospels today without reading later revelation back into the Twelve; ch. 14 objections and replies; Conclusion; Appendices A–I; Bibliography.

**Neighbours.** Study 75 and Rule 23 (faith before the Cross; cited, not restated); Acts Commentary Volume 1 (Acts 1:6–8); Gospel Volume 5 (the Upper Room); Gospel Volumes 1–3.

**Rulings (publisher, 2026-10-09, "i approve", all eight as recommended):**
1. Luke 18:34 ("hid from them") is reported as written; the volume does not say who hid it or why.
2. The Twelve's Kingdom hope (Luke 24:21; Acts 1:6) is reported without correction as error; Luke 24:25 rebukes slowness to believe the prophets on the suffering, not the hope of the glory; Acts 1:7 is left to Acts Volume 1.
3. Peter's confession (Matthew 16:16–17) is real faith; the content is what had been revealed to him, and no later revelation (the finished work of Christ) is added (Rule 19 binds).
4. John 20:9, 2:22 and 12:16 are reported as written: understanding came after the resurrection; the volume does not say they then received the Mystery.
5. John 16:12 and 14:26 are reported as written and not equated with Paul's revelation; the earlier Spirit rulings stand.
6. Luke 24:44–47 ("among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem") is reported as written with no program change drawn; Acts is left to the Acts series.
7. The flight and Peter's denial are reported from the text, with no claims about motives or inner states.
8. Luke 24:27 and 24:44 are reported as written; the volume does not name the passages the Lord used and does not say the prophets foretold the Body.

**Objections chapter.** Objections: the disciples fully understood the Cross beforehand; their Kingdom hope was an error the Lord corrected; their faith was already faith in the finished work; Luke 24:27 shows the prophets taught the Body; "hid from them" shows only dullness. Likely supporters Walvoord, Constable, Toussaint; likely opponents Carson, Wright, France. Voices fixed at drafting; any not read in full are described without quotation marks.

**Status.** Plan approved 2026-10-09; interior (56 pages including the cover; fourteen chapters and Appendices A–H with the verse-level index) drafted; supplied painted cover lettered by Claude (the lighter-veil version chosen by the publisher); page `commentary-gospel-04-what-the-twelve-understood.html`, Commentary Collection tile and sitemap entry prepared (previews: interior pages 1, 2, 13 and 32). Voices: Constable and Walvoord (supporting); Sproul, Duguid, Storms, Greear and Tabb (opposing). Library price for 35 volumes still unruled; no Gospel bundle offered.

## Volume 5 — The Upper Room: The King's Last Words to the Twelve Before the Cross (thesis, outline and rulings approved 2026-10-09)

**Title and subtitle.** The Upper Room — The King's Last Words to the Twelve Before the Cross. About 55 pages.

**Narrower question.** To whom are the promises of John 13–17 given, and in what setting?

**Thesis.** John 13–17 is the Lord's last night with the eleven (Judas leaves at 13:30). He speaks to men who followed him in Israel's line, and nothing in the chapters names the Body. They promise a place prepared (14:2–3), another Comforter (14:16–17, 26; 15:26; 16:7–15), the vine, the branches and the commandment of love (15), hatred and being put out of the synagogues (15:18–16:4), and a prayer for the eleven and for those who will believe through their word (17). The volume reads each promise as given to the eleven and to the believers who come through their word, and does not read any of them as the Body's gathering, indwelling or calling. The Body is named only as a contrast; Paul's passages are set beside the Upper Room's words and never merged with them.

**Outline (about 55 pages).** Preface; Thesis (six claims); ch. 1 setting and audience (13:1-5, 18, 30; 14:31); ch. 2 the towel and the commandment (13:1-38; Study 72 a neighbour); ch. 3 "In my Father's house" (14:1-6); ch. 4 "Another Comforter" (14:15-31); ch. 5 the vine (15:1-17); ch. 6 the world's hatred and the synagogues (15:18-16:4); ch. 7 the Spirit's work (16:5-15); ch. 8 "a little while" and the Lord's peace (16:16-33); ch. 9 the prayer (17); ch. 10 what the Upper Room does not say; ch. 11 Paul's letters set beside it as contrast only; ch. 12 objections and replies (closing table); Conclusion; Appendices A-H with the verse-level index; Bibliography.

**Rulings (publisher, 2026-10-09, "i approve"):** the three John rulings recorded above (14:2-3, the vine, 17:20-21) stand, with eight more as recommended: (1) 13:1-38 and 14:1-31 reported as written to the eleven, Judas gone at 13:30, the Body not read into "ye"; (2) the new commandment (13:34-35; 15:12, 17) is the Lord's commandment to the disciples, not converted into the Body's rule, Paul's teaching on love set beside it as contrast; (3) 14:16-17, 26; 15:26; 16:7-15 follow the earlier Spirit rulings, "shall be in you" not the Body's indwelling doctrine, no claim when or how the promise was kept; (4) 14:6 reported as written in its setting (Thomas and Philip), faith the means (Rule 19), no later revelation added to the eleven; (5) 14:12-14, 15:7, 16 and 16:23-24 reported to the eleven in their setting, no doctrine on prayer or healing built from them; (6) 15:18-16:4 reported in Israel's setting, not read as the Body's persecution, no claim on later events; (7) 16:12-13 reported as written and not equated with Paul's revelation, cross-referenced to Volume 4; (8) the standing Romans 11 and Galatians 3 sentences apply wherever touched, ch. 11 contrast only.
