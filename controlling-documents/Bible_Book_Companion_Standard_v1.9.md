# Zay-Marie Press Bible Book Companion Standard — v1.9

Status: working standard, approved piece by piece by the publisher on 2026-10-13. It replaces the Doctrinal Packet Standard (v0.4, five-page format). The Bible Book Companion Series is a separate product strand from the Digital Studies; this Standard governs companions only (Master Standard §16 and §17 govern studies).

## Name (publisher directive, 2026-10-13)
The series was called the Doctrinal Packets. On 2026-10-13 the publisher renamed it the **Bible Book Companion Series** (a single book is a **Bible Book Companion**). The name is used on the website, in the PDFs, in the controlling documents and in this folder. Earlier dated entries (Master Standard Version History, the CLAUDE.md entries, working trackers) keep the name in force when they were written. File names, URLs and CSS classes are unchanged: book pages are `packet-<slug>.html`, the hub is `doctrinal-packets.html`. The word "packet" may still appear in a URL or file name; it never appears in anything the reader sees. (An intermediate name, "Bible Companion", was in use for a few hours on 2026-10-13 and is retired.)

## 1. Purpose
A Bible Book Companion is the paid, interpretive companion to a book's free Bible Book Summary. The summary orients. The companion interprets: it gives the book's program identity, follows its movements, sets what belongs to a program apart from what holds for all people, applies the universal truths, and closes with a teaching outline and usage notes.

## 2. Product specification
- One companion per book of the Bible (66). $2.99, printable PDF, KJV only.
- Page size and margins as the Digital Studies (483.846 × 725.754 pt; margins 43.2 / 61.2 / 51.55 / 51.55 pt), Roboto only (Regular, Italic, Bold), body 10 pt black, headings 17 pt navy (#1B3350), table headers Slate Blue (#3B6E91).
- Length is whatever the eleven sections need, cover included (1 Chronicles 6 pages, Ephesians 7, Romans 6). The page count is one number everywhere: hero buyline, What's Included, buy panel, meta description.
- Naming: title "Bible Book Companion — [Book]"; a subtitle chosen per book.

## 3. Structure (publisher's template, 2026-10-13)
Interior page 1 opens with the kicker "ZAY-MARIE PRESS | BIBLE BOOK COMPANIONS", the title, the subtitle, a gold rule and a Table of Contents with real dot leaders aligned to the right edge. Then, in this order:
1. Book Overview (Program-Aware), with a boxed Program Identity statement.
2. Narrative Flow (three to seven movements).
3. Program Themes: "Prophecy-Program Themes" for Old Testament books; "Mystery-Program Themes" or "Program Themes" for New Testament books as the book requires.
4. Christ in [Book]: the prophetic trajectory (Old Testament) or fulfillment and revelation (New Testament).
5. Israel's Identity in [Book] (Old Testament); "Israel and the Body in [Book]" (New Testament, where the book addresses the question; Romans: "Israel in Romans").
6. Body-of-Christ Distinctions.
7. Program-Specific Truths Table (Israel-Specific rows for Old Testament books; Body-Specific and Israel-Specific rows as the book requires; Slate Blue header, four columns: Category, Truth, Program Identity, Scripture).
8. Universal Truths Table (All People, All Time): three columns.
9. Program-Safe Application Grid: a "Why This Section Exists" box and eight applications, each a bold lead-in and a short pastoral line. Old Testament books draw only from the universal truths. Where a book is addressed to the Body, the applications may also draw on the book's own instructions to believers, and the box says so in plain words; Israel's covenant commands or kingdom promises never become the Body's obligations.
10. Teaching Outline: five to eight movements in roman numerals with Scripture references, plain weight.
11. Companion Usage and Study Suggestions (placed last, closes the companion): three headings, each with one paragraph: How to Use This Companion in Personal Study; How to Use This Companion in Small Groups or Classes; How This Companion Fits Within the Acts Overlap Theology Series. For 1 Chronicles the paragraphs are the publisher's own text (with regular hyphens and the section name "Program-Safe Application Grid"); for other books they follow the same three-paragraph pattern, adapted to the book's program placement and approved with the book.

Bold is limited to headings, table headers, box titles and the lead-ins in section 9. Body text, item lines, the teaching outline and section 11 are regular weight.

## 3b. Passage classification (publisher directive, 2026-10-13; Master Standard v1.136)
Two-class ruling (2026-10-13; supersedes every Program Identity label and classification ruling above it in this section, including labels A and B, Text Only, Body-Specific, Israel-Specific, Prophetic Fulfillment and “General Christian Truth (Non‑Program‑Assigned)”; applies to all 66 Bible Book Companions, live, pending and future). Only two truth classes exist: Program-Specific Truth and Universal Truth. A Program-Specific row must carry the name of its program and there is no generic “Program-Specific Truth” label: “Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth” or “Body of Christ (Pauline) Truth.” A Universal row carries “Universal Truth” (truth that applies to all people, all time, regardless of program). Retired permanently: A, B, General Christian Truth, Text Only and any hybrid or non-program label. Greetings and addresses are classified by program identity, not neutrality: a greeting inherits the program of the book (2 John 1, 3 John 1 and James 1:1 = Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth; Pauline greetings = Body of Christ (Pauline) Truth; a greeting in an unassigned book = Universal Truth, only if it does not name Israel or Body mechanics). Row rulings for the Johns: 1 John 1:3, 1:7, 1:9, 2:1–2, 2:3–4, 2:18–23 and 4:1–3 = Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth; 1 John 1:5, 2:15–17 and 5:11–13 = Universal Truth; every 2 John row (verses 1, 4, 5–6, 7, 8, 9, 10–11 and, as the closing of the same letter, 12) = Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth; 3 John 3–4, 5–6, 7–8, 9–10 and 12 = Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth and 3 John 11 = Universal Truth, with 3 John 1 and, as the closing of the same letter, 3 John 13–14 = Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth. Applied 2026-10-13 to all 21 built companions: the former labels A and B and Body-Specific became Body of Christ (Pauline) Truth; Israel-Specific and Prophetic Fulfillment (Galatians 3:16) became Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth; Philippians 1:1 became Body of Christ (Pauline) Truth; no row outside the Johns was moved to Universal Truth, because no further row rulings have been made. The Section 7 heading on 1 Chronicles, Colossians and Ephesians became “Program-Specific Truths Table”. Open for the publisher: which rows in Hebrews, James, the Peters and the Pauline books, if any, are Universal Truth; and the closing verses 2 John 12 and 3 John 13–14, which were classified by the greeting-inherits-the-book rule.

Two-class ruling confirmed and applied (2026-10-13; closes the points left open in the two-class ruling above). (1) The closings 2 John 12 and 3 John 13–14 stay “Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth” under the rule that a greeting takes the program of its book. (2) The rows of Hebrews, James, 1 Peter and 2 Peter stay “Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth” under the Israel-addressed books ruling; no row outside the Johns is Universal Truth, and the Universal Truths Table (Section 8) of each companion is the place for truths that apply to all people. (3) Revelation belongs wholly to Israel’s Prophecy Program, so every Revelation row in the Truths Table carries “Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth”; the Body of Christ does not appear in its prophetic scenes, the great multitude of Revelation 7:9 is not the Body of Christ, and no ruling is asked for per row. (4) Standing instruction: apply a standing rule without asking; ask only where no rule covers the point. Jude (7 pages with the cover) and Revelation (8 pages) were built in the new format on 2026-10-13 and every row of both carries the Israel label.

The paragraphs below are kept as history. Where they conflict with the two-class ruling above, the two-class ruling governs.

Three labels, used in the Program Identity column of the Truths Table, and no others for books the Framework does not assign (except the label ruled for 1, 2 and 3 John in the non-program-assigned books ruling below). A. “Addressed to Believers (Identity / Doctrine)”: the verse teaches Body-of-Christ doctrine, defines Christian identity, explains salvation, grace, hope, inheritance, conduct or church order, or gives direct instruction to believers (for example Titus 2:11–14; 2 Tim. 1:9; 3:16). B. “Addressed to Believers (Warnings / Corrections)”: the verse warns believers, corrects false teaching, identifies threats inside the church, instructs leaders to silence, avoid, rebuke or guard against certain people or doctrines, describes future conditions believers must prepare for, or gives conditional statements about perseverance, reward or endurance (for example Titus 1:10, 1:14; 2 Tim. 2:12, 3:1). These verses are addressed to the Body of Christ and are never “Text Only.” C. “Text Only”: the verse is purely narrative, reports an event without instruction, quotes a saying without applying it, mentions a group without warning the church, or states a fact without directing believers to respond (historical references, travel notes, greetings, background statements); it is rare in epistles.

B versus C. If a verse tells Titus or Timothy what to do, it is B; warnings and corrections are instructions, not neutral text. If it identifies false teachers, it is B, even where the group is Jewish (circumcision, fables). If it describes future conditions believers must prepare for, it is B. If it contains a conditional promise or threat, it is B. “Text Only” is used only where the verse has no instructional force.

Israel-Specific. A verse is never classified “Israel-Specific” unless it explicitly references Israel’s covenants, land, Temple, priesthood or prophetic kingdom, is part of Israel’s prophetic program, and is not addressed to the Body of Christ. Titus and 2 Timothy contain no Israel-specific doctrinal commands.

Body-of-Christ doctrine. A verse is classified as Body doctrine (A) when it teaches grace, salvation, church leadership, sound doctrine, Christian conduct, perseverance, hope, Scripture’s authority, the gospel or the believer’s identity.

Standing sentence. “Do not label warnings, corrections, future conditions, or identification of false teachers as ‘Text Only.’ These are direct apostolic instructions addressed to the Body of Christ and must be labeled ‘Addressed to Believers (Warnings / Corrections).’” The rule is retroactive: every companion already built is brought to it. In books the Framework assigns to a program (Ephesians, Colossians, Galatians, Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians) the program labels Body-Specific, Israel-Specific and Prophetic Fulfillment stand beside label B. Hebrews and James, whose audiences the letters name (Hebrews 8; James 1:1), are held for the publisher’s ruling on how these labels apply, and keep their present labels until he rules.

Mandatory classification rule. “Never classify a verse as ‘Text Only’ if Paul uses it to instruct, warn, exhort, encourage, correct, strengthen, or guide believers — even if the verse is not a doctrinal identity marker. ‘Text Only’ may only be used when the verse has zero instructional force (pure narrative, greetings, travel notes, background statements).” Applied 2026-10-13: Philippians 1:12–14 and 4:3 and Philemon 9 (the appeal) moved from Text Only to Warnings / Corrections; bare greetings and addresses (Philippians 1:1, Philemon 2, James 1:1) stay Text Only; Hebrews is held for the publisher’s ruling.

Hebrews and James ruling (2026-10-13; the Text Only treatment is superseded by the Israel-addressed books ruling below). Hebrews is written to Hebrew believers within Israel’s prophetic and covenant setting, and James is addressed to “the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad” (James 1:1); neither letter is addressed to the Body of Christ. Their exhortations, warnings, priesthood and covenant language, perseverance warnings, faith-and-works argument, rich/poor warnings and healing instructions are not addressed to the Body and do not transfer to it. Every row in Hebrews and in James stays “Text Only” unless a verse expresses a universal truth (for example God’s faithfulness or character); neither letter receives “Warnings / Corrections” (or “Identity / Doctrine”) labels, and Body-Specific is never applied to Hebrews or James. Program labels stay in the books the Framework assigns: Body-Specific (Romans, Galatians, Ephesians, Colossians, 1 and 2 Corinthians, 1 and 2 Timothy, Titus, Philemon); Israel-Specific (Old Testament prophecy books, Gospels as appropriate, Acts 1–8, Hebrews, James, Revelation as appropriate); Prophetic Fulfillment (prophecy books, Gospels, Acts 1–8, Revelation as appropriate). Applied 2026-10-13: James’s table moved to Text Only throughout; Hebrews already stood as written. Two points are open for the publisher: the label for a universal-truth verse in Hebrews or James, and how Body-Specific for the Pastoral Letters and Philemon sits beside labels A and B.

Israel-addressed books ruling (2026-10-13; supersedes the Text Only treatment in the Hebrews and James ruling of v1.138). Where a book is addressed to Israel (Hebrews, James, 1 and 2 Peter, Jude, portions of Revelation and portions of the Gospels), every doctrinal statement, exhortation, warning, command and instruction is labeled “Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth” in the Program Identity column; these verses are never “Text Only,” and they are not addressed to the Body of Christ and do not transfer to it. “Text Only” is reserved for neutral narrative, greetings, travel notes and background statements. Program identity is decided by audience and covenant context, not by canonical placement: appearing in the New Testament does not make a truth apply to the Body. Applied 2026-10-13: all 8 rows of Hebrews and the 7 doctrinal rows of James carry the new label; James 1:1 (the address to the twelve tribes) was left Text Only and is corrected by the audience-marker ruling below. Open for the publisher: how Body-Specific for the Pastoral Letters and Philemon sits beside labels A and B.

Audience-marker ruling (2026-10-13; completes the Israel-addressed books ruling of v1.139). James 1:1 is not a neutral greeting. It is the audience marker that establishes the entire program identity of the book. Any verse that names Israel as the audience is program-identity content and must be labeled “Israel / Kingdom / Prophecy Explicit Truth,” not “Text Only.” Applied 2026-10-13: James 1:1 now carries the Israel label, and Philemon 2 (“the church in thy house”), which names the Body of Christ as the audience, now carries “Addressed to Believers (Identity / Doctrine)”.

Non-program-assigned books ruling (2026-10-13; applies to 1 John, 2 John and 3 John). The Framework assigns 1 John, 2 John and 3 John neither to the Body of Christ nor to Israel. Their audience markers (“you who believe,” “my little children,” “the elect lady,” “Gaius”) identify believers but do not assign a program. They are therefore not labeled as Body-of-Christ doctrine and not labeled as Israel-program truth. A new label is used in the Program Identity column for all doctrinal, ethical, moral and spiritual statements in these three letters: “General Christian Truth (Non‑Program‑Assigned).” “Text Only” is reserved for greetings, addresses and background statements. The labels A and B are not used in these three letters. Applied 2026-10-13 to 1 John, 2 John and 3 John: every doctrinal, ethical, moral and spiritual row carries the new label; the addresses (2 John 1; 3 John 1) and the closing notes (2 John 12; 3 John 13–14) stay Text Only. The table introduction in these letters defines the label in reader-facing words. Open for the publisher: whether any other book the Framework does not assign takes this label; until he rules, the books already built keep labels A and B.
