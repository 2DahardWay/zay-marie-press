1 John

John writes so his readers may share apostolic fellowship, resist antichrist deception, practice righteousness and love, and know that they possess eternal life in God’s Son.

TestamentNew Testament
GenreGeneral Epistle
SettingA community tested by secession and false teaching
Central EmphasisLife, truth, righteousness, love, and assurance
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

John proclaims the incarnate Word of life so his readers may have fellowship with the apostolic witnesses, the Father, and the Son, and so their joy may be full. He writes against deceivers who deny essential truth about Jesus Christ and whose departure has exposed their opposition.

He also writes so believers may not sin and may know that they have eternal life. Repeated tests of confession, righteousness, love, obedience, and perseverance distinguish God’s children from the world and antichrist deception.

Historical and Biblical Context

The epistle does not name its author or recipients. Its vocabulary and theological relationship to the Gospel of John, together with early Christian testimony, support traditional association with John the apostle. The audience appears to be a network of believers known personally to the writer.

Former associates have gone out from the community and deny that Jesus is the Christ or that He has come in the flesh. The repeated “last hour,” antichrist, abiding, anointing, and overcoming language belongs to John’s prophetic-apostolic setting and prepares readers for deception before Christ’s appearing.

Structure and Outline

  1. The Word of life and fellowship in the light — 1 John 1:1–2:2
    Apostolic witness grounds fellowship; confession and Christ’s advocacy address sin.
  2. Obedience, love, and abiding — 1 John 2:3–27
    Knowing God appears in obedience and brotherly love; antichrists deny the Son.
  3. Children of God and practiced righteousness — 1 John 2:28–3:24
    Future likeness motivates purity; love acts sacrificially and assures the heart.
  4. Testing spirits and perfected love — 1 John 4
    True confession distinguishes God’s Spirit; God’s prior love produces mutual love and confidence.
  5. Faith, testimony, eternal life, and final confidence — 1 John 5
    Faith overcomes the world, God testifies concerning His Son, and believers know they have life.

Major Themes

The Incarnate Son

The eternal life was manifested in the historical Jesus Christ. True confession affirms the Son’s coming in the flesh and inseparable relation to the Father.

Fellowship in Light

God is light. Walking in light includes truthful acknowledgment of sin, cleansing through Christ’s blood, and shared fellowship.

Righteousness and Love

God’s children practice righteousness and love fellow believers in action and truth. Hatred belongs to darkness and death.

Truth against Antichrist Deception

Antichrists deny Jesus as the Christ and separate Father from Son. The apostolic message and anointing enable the faithful to abide.

Assurance of Eternal Life

God’s testimony centers life in His Son. John’s tests are designed to expose false claims and give true believers knowledge and confidence.

Key Passages

Interpretive Considerations

  • Follow John’s cyclical development rather than forcing the letter into a strictly linear outline; truth, obedience, and love recur with increasing depth.
  • Distinguish isolated acts of sin, truthful confession, and settled practice. John opposes both claims of sinlessness and a pattern that contradicts divine birth.
  • Read “antichrist” from John’s definitions: denial of Jesus as the Christ, denial of Father and Son, and refusal to confess Jesus Christ come in the flesh.
  • First John’s assurance passages must be read together. Subjective confidence is tested by the objective apostolic message centered in God’s Son.
  • The “sin unto death” in 5:16 belongs to John’s immediate community, testimony, and apostasy context; interpretations should avoid unwarranted certainty where the text is concise.
  • Doctrinal use elsewhere does not erase John’s last-hour, appearing, and antichrist horizon or merge it indiscriminately with Paul’s Mystery teaching.

Study Questions

  1. What apostolic testimony grounds fellowship?
  2. How does John answer false claims about sin and knowing God?
  3. Which confessions and behaviors expose antichrist deception?
  4. How do righteousness and love identify God’s children?
  5. What is the source and pattern of Christian love?
  6. On what basis may readers know they have eternal life?
