Reading the Map: “To the Jew First”

During Acts 9–28 Israel's Prophecy Program is still operating alongside the Mystery. So in city after city Paul goes to the synagogue first, reasoning from Israel's Scriptures that Jesus is the Christ (Acts 17:2–3). He ministers within Israel's prophetic setting without administering Israel's program; his own apostleship and commission remain distinctly Mystery.

  • Local turns (Acts 13:46; 18:6; 19:9): after local rejection, Paul turns to the Gentiles in that city. Israel's national standing is unchanged, and Paul keeps entering synagogues elsewhere. These are not successive stages.
  • National judicial suspension (Acts 28:25–28): at Rome, citing Isaiah 6:9–10, Paul pronounces the national judicial suspension of Israel's Prophecy Program. It is categorically different from the local turns — and a suspension, not an abolition. Israel's prophetic promises remain to be fulfilled.
  • Signs and Acts-era practices: signs continue because Israel's prophetic setting is still operative (“the Jews require a sign,” 1 Corinthians 1:22). Paul's accommodations — Timothy's circumcision, the Cenchrea vow, the temple purification — and the baptisms in his ministry are read by their own context. None of them becomes doctrine or obligation for the Body of Christ.
