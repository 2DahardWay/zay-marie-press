What Does “Sell All That Thou Hast” Mean in Luke 18:22?

From Reader Questions, Volume 4: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 4 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

It is the Lord’s own test of one man, a command to an individual and not a universal rule, a ruler who asked what he must do to inherit eternal life, and it is part of the kingdom discipleship he set before Israel. The ruler said he had kept the commandments from his youth. The Lord answered, “Yet lackest thou one thing: sell all that thou hast, and distribute unto the poor,” and the man went away “very sorrowful: for he was very rich.” The command exposed what ruled him. It is not a rule that the Body must sell its possessions; the Twelve’s company in Acts 2–4 shared what it had in expectation of the kingdom, and Paul never commands the Body to sell all. Paul tells the Body’s rich to be “ready to distribute,” which is a different instruction, and nothing in the saying makes poverty the price of salvation.

Where the Verse Stands

A ruler comes to the Lord with a question, “what shall I do to inherit eternal life?” The Lord asks why he calls him good, lists the commandments, and hears that he has kept them all. Then comes the one thing he lacks. When the ruler goes away, the Lord says how hard it is for the rich to enter the kingdom, the bystanders ask “Who then can be saved?”, and the Lord answers that what is impossible with men is possible with God. Peter then says that the disciples have left all, and the Lord promises what those who leave all for the kingdom’s sake will receive.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the man asks “what shall I do to inherit eternal life?” and is first answered with the commandments (Matthew 19:17: “if thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments”). He is a ruler in Israel under the law, asking how to inherit.
  • Stated: the command is addressed to him in the singular and is tied to a particular lack: “Yet lackest thou one thing.” It ends with “come, follow me,” the call of the Lord’s disciples in his earthly ministry.
  • Shown by the context: the response shows what held him: “he was very rich.” The Lord then speaks of the rich entering the kingdom, and the disciples’ reaction, “Who then can be saved?”, shows that wealth was taken as a sign of favour. The answer is that salvation is possible with God and not by what men can do.
  • Shown by the context: the call to leave all belongs to Israel’s kingdom discipleship in the Lord’s ministry, as in Luke 14:33, “whosoever he be of you that forsaketh not all that he hath, he cannot be my disciple,” and as in Volume 3, entry 1. The Jerusalem believers’ sharing of their goods (Acts 4:34) is reported in Acts 1–8, within Israel’s program.
  • Inferred: that the saying is a particular test within that discipleship, and not a rule laid on every believer. The argument rests on the singular address, on Paul’s words to the Body’s rich (1 Timothy 6:17–19; Ephesians 4:28), which command giving and do not command selling all, and on the Body’s being saved by grace and not by what it gives up.

The Other Readings

A rule for every disciple. Some take the ruler’s story as a picture of the call to leave everything that stands between the disciple and Christ, one that comes to everyone who would follow. It must explain why Paul does not command the Body to sell all, and why the Lord answered a man who asked how to inherit eternal life by doing. A test of this man’s heart. Many commentaries read the command as exposing the ruler’s attachment to wealth, which is close to the sense taken here. It must say whether the command also applies beyond him, and the entry says it belongs to Israel’s kingdom discipleship.

What It Means for the Reader

The reader is asked what holds him, as the ruler was. For the Body, the instruction is to be “rich in good works, ready to distribute, willing to communicate” (1 Timothy 6:18), and to work, “that he may have to give to him that needeth” (Ephesians 4:28). Neither makes salvation the reward of giving.
