How Do I Know God’s Will? Romans 12:2

From Reader Questions, Volume 5: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 5 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

The Body of Christ knows God’s will through Paul’s revelation, not through signs, dreams or prophetic guidance. Paul says the way is a renewed mind: “be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.” The will he then names is stated in his letters: sanctification, thanksgiving, and being “filled with the knowledge of his will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding.” The Spirit guides by opening what has been revealed, not by outward signs. The methods of Israel’s prophetic setting, such as casting lots and waiting for a voice, are not set before the Body. Wisdom comes by applying Paul’s doctrine with prayer, and the aim of the will is “Christ be formed in you.”

Where the Verses Stand

Romans 12 turns from doctrine to the believer’s walk. Paul beseeches “brethren” to present their bodies, tells them not to be conformed to the world, and says the alternative is a renewed mind that proves God’s will. He goes on to the gifts, love and conduct that follow. The other passages Paul uses on the will of God are brief and direct: sanctification and thanksgiving in 1 Thessalonians, the knowledge of his will in Colossians, and Scripture’s power to equip in 2 Timothy.

Israel’s line knew other ways. The Twelve gave forth lots in Acts 1:26, before the Spirit came, and the proverb says “The lot is cast into the lap; but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD” (Proverbs 16:33). Those belong to Israel’s prophetic setting. A hard case sits in Acts 9–28: Paul is “forbidden of the Holy Ghost to preach the word in Asia” and a vision appears to him in the night (Acts 16:6–10). These are recorded in settings shaped by Israel’s Prophecy Program, which remained operative throughout Acts 9–28 even as the Mystery Program began. Acts 9–28 is the overlap; where Paul is the one guided, this entry reports the text and does not make the event a method for the Body, since his letters to the Body never set signs or visions before the believer as the way to know God’s will.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: Romans 12:2 gives the method: not conformed to the world but transformed by the renewing of the mind, “that ye may prove” what God’s will is. The will is proved by a renewed mind, not delivered by a sign.
  • Stated: Paul names parts of the will outright: “your sanctification” (1 Thessalonians 4:3) and thanks “in every thing” (5:18), and he prays that the Colossians be “filled with the knowledge of his will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding” (Colossians 1:9).
  • Shown by the context: the means Paul points to is the written word: “All scripture is given by inspiration of God … That the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works” (2 Timothy 3:16–17). The Spirit’s part is knowing “the things that are freely given to us of God” (1 Corinthians 2:12), which concerns what God has given, not a private direction.
  • Shown by the context: the lot is the method of the Twelve in Acts 1:26, within Acts 1–8, and Hebrews 1:1–2 says God spoke “in time past unto the fathers by the prophets” and “in these last days” by his Son. These belong to Israel’s prophetic line, and Paul’s letters to the Body never instruct a believer to cast lots or wait for a voice.
  • Shown by the context: the Spirit’s part in knowing the will is stated as knowledge of what God has given: “we have received, not the spirit of the world, but the spirit which is of God; that we might know the things that are freely given to us of God” (1 Corinthians 2:12). The Spirit opens what is already given and does not add a private word beside it.
  • Inferred: that where Scripture gives no command, the Body decides by wisdom applying Paul’s doctrine, with prayer (Philippians 4:6–7). The argument rests on 12:2, on Colossians 1:9 and on Paul’s silence about signs as guidance for the Body.

The Other Readings

Specific personal guidance. Henry Blackaby (Experiencing God) teaches that God speaks to the individual through Scripture, prayer, circumstances and the church, and that the believer’s task is to find where God is at work and join him. It must explain why Paul’s letters never promise this and why they put the will in a renewed mind. Wisdom within God’s revealed will. Garry Friesen (Decision Making and the Will of God) and Bruce Waltke (Finding the Will of God: A Pagan Notion?) argue that Scripture gives God’s moral will and that open decisions are made by wisdom, with no hidden individual plan to discover. That is close to the reading taken here, with the added point that the Body’s will is Paul’s revelation and Israel’s methods are a different program’s.

What It Means for the Reader

The reader is asked to read Paul, let the mind be renewed, give thanks, pursue holiness and pray, and to expect God’s will to be known that way. The goal of the will is stated: Christ “be formed in you” (Galatians 4:19). Waiting for a sign before obeying what is already written puts the order the wrong way round. The passage does not say that every choice has a single hidden right answer, and it does not promise that obedience will be felt as certainty. It names what God wills and the way the mind is changed, and it leaves the rest to wisdom, prayer and the counsel of other believers.
