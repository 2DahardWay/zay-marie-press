How Do I Deal with Doubt? Ephesians 1:13 and 2 Timothy 3:15

From Reader Questions, Volume 5: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 5 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

Doubt is met by returning to what Paul wrote, which steadies the mind, and the Body is not asked to look for signs to confirm it. Believers were “sealed with that holy Spirit of promise” when they believed, so doubt is not a sign of lost salvation. The Spirit helps by opening what God has given, “that we might know the things that are freely given to us of God.” The Lord’s refusal of a sign to Israel (“An evil and adulterous generation seeketh after a sign”) shows that asking for confirmations belongs to Israel’s setting. Doubt weakens as Scripture is read and believed, and Paul tells believers to “stand fast in the faith,” to pray with thanksgiving and to handle the word of truth rightly. Faith grows by hearing.

Where the Verses Stand

Ephesians 1 speaks to saints and faithful in Christ Jesus and says that after they heard “the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation,” and believed, they were sealed with the Holy Spirit. Paul’s other words on faith are to Timothy, who learned the Scriptures from a child, and to the Corinthians, whom he tells to stand fast. The Lord’s word on signs is spoken to Israel in Matthew 12.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the Body is sealed on believing: “after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise” (Ephesians 1:13). The seal is not removed by doubt.
  • Stated: Paul’s remedy for a troubled heart is prayer with thanksgiving, with the result that “the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds” (Philippians 4:6–7), and the instruction to “stand fast in the faith” (1 Corinthians 16:13).
  • Shown by the context: the Lord’s refusal of a sign to Israel (Matthew 12:39) is spoken to Israel’s scribes and Pharisees; signs as confirmation belong to Israel’s setting (“the Jews require a sign,” 1 Corinthians 1:22). The Body’s footing is the revealed word.
  • Shown by the context: Paul tells Timothy that the scriptures are “able to make thee wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus” (2 Timothy 3:15) and tells him to be “rightly dividing the word of truth” (2 Timothy 2:15). Faith is nourished by the word. Romans 10:17 states that faith comes by hearing, in the chapter addressed to Israel; the principle is used here only as a general truth and carries no weight for the argument.
  • Shown by the context: Timothy is reminded that “from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures” (2 Timothy 3:15). The steady mind he has been given comes from what he has long read and believed, not from a recent sign.
  • Inferred: that doubt is a lapse in the believer’s grip on what is true and not a loss of standing. The text says the believer is sealed and tells him to stand fast; the argument rests on those two statements.

The Other Readings

Doubt as a path to deeper faith. Some writers treat doubt as something to be faced honestly, distinguishing doubts of faith from doubts of unbelief. This fits the reading taken here where doubt is met by returning to the truth. Doubt answered by evidence. Gary Habermas (Dealing with Doubt) gives factual, emotional and volitional answers, and stresses the evidence for the resurrection. The reading taken here agrees that the word steadies the mind but does not rest the Body’s assurance on signs.

What It Means for the Reader

The reader who doubts is sent back to what is written, told that he is sealed, asked to pray with thanksgiving and to stand fast. Waiting for a sign is the way Israel was rebuked for seeking, and is not the Body’s way. The passage does not say the believer will never doubt, and it does not treat a doubting believer as an unbeliever. It speaks of a seal and a word, and it asks the reader to keep hold of both when his feelings and his questions press hard.
