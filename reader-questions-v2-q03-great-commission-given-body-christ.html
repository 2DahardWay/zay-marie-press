Is the Great Commission Given to the Body of Christ?

From Reader Questions, Volume 2: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 2 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

The commission of Matthew 28:18–20 was spoken to the eleven on a mountain in Galilee, after Matthew 10 sent them to “the lost sheep of the house of Israel.” It widens the field to the nations and ties the work to the kingdom commands and “the end of the world.” The Body’s commission is Paul’s, the ministry of reconciliation, and Paul says Christ “sent me not to baptize.”

Where the Verse Stands

The commission closes Matthew. Its first hearers are named: “the eleven disciples,” in “a mountain where Jesus had appointed them.” Eighteen chapters earlier the same men were sent out with a narrower charge. The passage cannot be read as if no earlier commission stood behind it.

Now then we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God did beseech you by us: we pray you in Christ's stead, be ye reconciled to God.”Paul’s commission: “the ministry of reconciliation,” “ambassadors for Christ.”

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the audience is “the eleven disciples,” and the place is the one “where Jesus had appointed them.” The commission is given to those men.
  • Stated: the content to be taught is “all things whatsoever I have commanded you,” and what the Lord had commanded these men is recorded in this Gospel from the Sermon on the Mount to chapter 24.
  • Shown by the context: the earlier commission (10:5–7) sends the Twelve to Israel only. The new commission names “all nations,” so the field widens, while the work stays that of the kingdom the Twelve had been sent to announce.
  • Shown by the context: the book of Acts opens with the same men asking, “wilt thou at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel?” The Lord does not correct the question; he speaks of times and seasons and sends them as witnesses, beginning in Jerusalem.
  • Inferred: that “the end of the world” is the same age Matthew has been speaking of, as in 24:3. The Greek word is the same in both places, so the promise “I am with you always” runs through the age that ends with the kingdom, not through a different one.

The Other Readings

The charter of the church. Many commentaries read the passage as the standing mission of all the Lord’s people to the end of the age, and many draw the church’s method from it. This reading must carry to the Body a commission whose terms include baptizing and the keeping of the kingdom commands, while Paul says Christ “sent me not to baptize.” The commission of the Twelve. This is the reading taken here. It leaves the Body its own commission, the one Paul names: “the ministry of reconciliation,” given to those who are “ambassadors for Christ.” Paul never repeats the commission of Matthew 28 and never commands baptism, and he is not recorded placing the Body under the kingdom commands.

ReadingWhat it must sayWhere it strains

The commission of the TwelveThe eleven are sent to disciple the nations under the Lord’s commands.The Body’s witness must stand on Paul’s commission.

What It Means for the Reader

The Body is not without a commission; it is not Matthew 28. Its commission is the ministry of reconciliation, given through Paul, whose apostleship to the Gentiles is what he calls his “office.” A reader who reads Matthew 28 as the Body’s charter takes over baptism and the kingdom commands that Paul does not command.
