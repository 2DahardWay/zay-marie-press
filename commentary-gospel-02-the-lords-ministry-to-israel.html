GOSPEL COMMENTARY SERIES · VOLUME 2

The Lord’s Ministry to Israel

Sent Only to the Lost Sheep

Whom does each Gospel say the Lord was sent to, and what does it say he was sent to do?

He says, “I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Israel,” and he sends the Twelve to the same sheep. Yet a woman of Canaan, a centurion, a village of Samaritans and a company of Greeks all come to him. This commentary reads each scene in order, asking whom the Gospel says he was sent to and what follows.

It argues that the Lord’s ministry before the Cross was a ministry to Israel, confirming the promises made to the fathers, and that the strangers who came to him show the order of that ministry and do not abolish it. In Chapter 14 it sets its reading beside modern writers who see it differently.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not survey how the Lord’s earthly ministry has been read and leave the choice to the reader. It states what the author believes the Gospels report, to whom the Lord and the Twelve were sent and what they were sent to do, sets out the evidence scene by scene, hears the writers who disagree in their own words, and answers them from Scripture.

It is the second volume of the Gospel Commentary Series, each volume of which is built around one narrower question on the four Gospels. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which report what a passage says. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout, and the writers named in Chapter 14 are quoted only from words the author has read in full. Its own question is narrow: whom does each Gospel say the Lord and the Twelve were sent to, and what does it say they were sent to do?

FROM THE VOLUME

What This Book Sets Out to Do

Each Gospel reports the Lord Jesus sent to Israel. He came to the lost sheep of the house of Israel, he sent the Twelve to the same sheep, and he sent the Seventy before his face into the cities where he himself would come.

The woman of Canaan, the centurion, the Samaritans and the Greeks are real, and each is reported with care. They show the order of the ministry and they do not abolish it.
