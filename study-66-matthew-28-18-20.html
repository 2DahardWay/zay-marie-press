STUDY 66 · DIGITAL STUDY

Matthew 28:18–20

The Great Commission

What did Jesus assign to the Eleven on the Galilean mountain — and to whom did that assignment belong?

Matthew 28:18–20 is called the Great Commission more often than it is read closely. Yet it is a short passage with a precise setting, a named audience, one command surrounded by three participles, and a time marker that Matthew defines elsewhere in his Gospel.

This study begins where Matthew does: on an appointed mountain in Galilee, with the eleven disciples and the risen King. It follows the Greek structure of verses 19–20, traces Matthew’s own use of “all the nations” and “the end of the age,” tests the standard “all things whatsoever I have commanded you” against what Matthew records, and only then asks where Paul’s commission stands, holding to one order throughout: meaning first, assignment second, application third.

ABOUT THIS STUDY

Read the Commission in Order: Meaning, Assignment, Application

You will read Matthew 28:18–20 as Matthew wrote it: after the resurrection, on an appointed mountain, to a named group, and against the earlier sending of the Twelve in Matthew 10. You will follow the single command and its three participles, trace “all the nations” through Matthew, Luke, and Acts, collect what Jesus is recorded to have commanded, and weigh Matthew’s use of “the end of the age” before drawing any application.

Readers who already own Study 18 (Peter and Paul: Distinct Apostolic Commissions), Study 65 (Acts 2:42), or Study 42 (One Baptism) will recognize adjoining ground. Study 18 compares the two apostolic assignments side by side; this study reads the wording of the Eleven’s commission itself and comes to Paul only in its seventh section. Study 65 asks what the Twelve taught at Jerusalem and to whom it belonged; this study stays in Galilee and asks what they were told to do. Study 42 classifies every water baptism in Acts and follows Paul’s letters to the one baptism of the Body; this study treats “baptizing” only as one word of the commission and does not re-run that classification. The question this study alone answers: what did Jesus assign to the Eleven in Matthew 28:18–20, by what wording, and to whom?

WHAT YOU’LL STUDY

Trace the Commission Before You Conclude

The Galilee Setting

Read Matthew 28:18–20 in its own setting by tracing the Galilee scene, the persons addressed, and the earlier sending of the Twelve in Matthew 10 before interpreting a single phrase of the commission.

One Command, Three Participles

Follow the structure of verses 19–20 in the Greek — one imperative and three participles — and test how each participle relates to the command to make disciples.

All the Nations

Trace how Matthew uses “all the nations” (panta ta ethnē) in 24:9, 24:14, and 25:32, and compare the wording with Luke 24:47 and Acts 1:8 to see where the commission begins and whom it names.

All Things Commanded

Read “all things whatsoever I have commanded you” by collecting what Jesus is recorded to have commanded the Eleven in Matthew, and by noting what Matthew’s Gospel does not contain.

The End of the Age

Weigh the phrase “the end of the age” (synteleia tou aiōnos) by tracing every use of it in Matthew’s Gospel, and note what the commission’s time marker attaches to.

Testing Four Readings

Apply a repeatable discipline for setting the commission beside the commission committed to Paul, so that you can receive what each passage says without transferring an assignment from one to the other.

KEY SCRIPTURES

Read the Word in Its Context

Matthew 28:16–20

The appointed mountain, the eleven disciples, and the words of the risen Lord.

Matthew 10:5–8

The earlier sending of the Twelve and the message they were to preach.

Daniel 7:13–14

The Son of man, the dominion given to Him, and the nations.

Matthew 24:3–14

The disciples’ question about the age, and the gospel of the kingdom for a witness to all nations.

Matthew 13:36–43

The parable of the tares explained, and the harvest at the end of the age.

Luke 24:44–49

The opened understanding of the disciples and the preaching in His name among all nations.

Acts 1:6–8

The Kingdom question and the witness that begins at Jerusalem.

Galatians 2:7–9

Paul’s account of the gospel of the circumcision and the gospel of the uncircumcision.

FROM THE STUDY

One Command

The commission contains a single imperative: make disciples.

Going, baptizing, and teaching are the participles that describe how. Reading the four English verbs as four separate commands changes what the passage says.
