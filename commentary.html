THE COMMENTARY COLLECTION

Whole‑Bible Doctrinal Arguments — Not Expanded Digital Studies

Digital Studies teach the architecture of Scripture — its audiences, settings, covenants, commissions, and programs.

Commentaries apply that architecture to one doctrinal question and defend a single position from Genesis to Revelation.

Each volume names opposing views in their own terms, cites their sources, and answers them with Scripture alone.

Digital Studies show readers how to read the Bible; Commentaries show what the Bible says when those rules are applied.

Prophetic Identity Commentary Series

A structured exploration of the prophetic identities that shape Scripture’s storyline—Israel’s bride, the faithful remnant, the covenant people, the olive tree, and the city at the end of the age—each read in its own setting without merging the programs.

Volume 1

The Bride Identity in Prophecy

The prophets call Israel the wife of the LORD, charge her with unfaithfulness and promise to take her back; Revelation shows the Lamb’s bride as a city with the tribes’ names on its gates. The volume argues these are one wife, and that Paul’s marriage pattern for the Body does not merge the two programs.

Volume 2

The Remnant

Through every judgment God has kept a believing portion of Israel alive, from the remnant of Jacob in the prophets to the sealed company with the Lamb on Zion. The volume argues the remnant is Israel’s from first to last and is never the Body.

Volume 3

The New Covenant

God promised a new covenant to the house of Israel and the house of Judah, and the Lord named it in his blood at the supper. The volume argues it is Israel’s from the first clause to the last, ratified at the cross and fulfilled in the Kingdom, with the Body benefiting from the blood without being the covenant party.

The Olive Tree

Paul’s olive tree in Romans 11 is read against Israel’s own use of the figure. The volume argues the tree is Israel’s from root to branch, and that the Gentiles grafted in take part in Israel’s blessing without becoming Israel, entering her covenants or becoming the Body.

The volume argues the New Jerusalem is the city of Israel’s hope, with the saved nations walking in its light on the new earth, and the Body’s place kept separate in the heavenly places.

Covenantal Commentary Series

A structured exploration of the covenant God made with Abraham and the themes that flow from it—his seed, the inheritance, the eternal purpose, and the throne sworn to David—each examined in its own biblical setting to show how the covenant shapes the entire prophetic program.

Volume 1

The Abrahamic Covenant

God cut one covenant with Abraham while he slept and confirmed it by oath. The volume argues it is unconditional, that its land, nation, name and blessing passed to Isaac, Jacob and Israel, and that Sinai neither added to it nor removed it.

View Volume 1 →
Volume 2

Abraham’s Seed

“Seed” in the promises to Abraham does not always name the same persons. The volume follows it from a chosen line through Isaac and Jacob to the house of David, and argues the line comes to its head in Christ, the one Seed, with the Body counted as his only through Christ.

Volume 3

Inheritance

God promised Abraham’s line a land held by oath, and Israel has possessed only part of it. The volume argues the prophets promise the whole for ever, secured by the Heir, and that the Body has its own inheritance kept in the heavenly places.

Volume 4

The Eternal Purpose of God

God’s purpose, settled in Christ before the ages, is served through two callings: Israel’s promises, land and Kingdom on the earth, and the Body’s mystery with its seat in the heavenly places. The volume argues the two meet in Christ’s headship over all things.

Volume 5

The Davidic Covenant

God promised David a son whose throne would have no end, confirmed by oath. The volume argues the son is Jesus and the throne is Israel’s, on the earth, taken up in the Kingdom, and that Christ’s present seat at God’s right hand and the Body’s seat in the heavenly places are different seats.

Prophecy & Mystery Commentary Series

A structured examination of how Scripture distinguishes Israel’s prophetic program from the Mystery revealed to Paul—clarifying Israel and the Body, the kingdom promises, the resurrections and the courts of judgment that belong to each program.

Volume 1

Israel & the Body

Scripture runs two programs: Israel’s, prophetic and earthly, and the Body’s, a Mystery with a heavenly calling. The volume argues they ran side by side through Acts 9–28 and never merged, and that Acts 28 reports a national suspension, not a replacement.

Volume 2

The Mystery Program

Paul names a secret kept in God before the world began and revealed to him. The volume argues its content is Jew and Gentile one body in Christ with a heavenly seat, a stewardship committed to Paul that began at Damascus and goes on beyond the Acts.

Volume 3

The Kingdom Program

Scripture promises Israel a kingdom with a King of David’s line, a throne and the nations under his rule. The volume argues it was announced as at hand, refused, offered again through Acts 1–8, set aside nationally at Acts 28, and comes with the King’s return.

An argued commentary that sets Christ’s resurrection, Israel’s and the Body’s in the order Scripture gives them, shows why the passages that name them do not run together, and answers five modern writers who read them as one.

Volume 5

Separate Courts

An argued commentary that reads the separate courts of Scripture, from the Cross and the judgment seat of Christ to Israel, the living nations and the great white throne, and answers those who read them as one judgment day.

Typology Commentary Series

A structured exploration of Scripture’s major typologies—sanctuary, priesthood, Exodus, kingdom, and end-of-age separation—each read in its own canonical setting to show how these patterns anticipate Christ and advance the prophetic program.

Volume 1

Tabernacle & Temple Typology

Scripture calls the tabernacle a shadow, a copy and a figure of heavenly things. The volume reads the court, altar, veil, mercy seat and Day of Atonement, marking which parts the New Testament names as pointing to Christ and which it only echoes.

View Volume 1 →
Volume 2

Prophetic Separation

Noah, Lot and Israel in Goshen each show God drawing a line before judgment falls. The volume reads those scenes in order, then asks what the Lord says in Matthew 24 and Luke 17, to whom, and what he leaves unsaid.

View Volume 2 →
Volume 3

Priesthood Typology: Aaron, Melchizedek & Christ

God set Aaron’s house in a perpetual priesthood and named another order, Melchizedek’s, for the Son. The volume reads both in order, from the garments and offices to the oath of Psalm 110 and the heavenly sanctuary of Hebrews.

$14.99 · 52 pages

View Volume 3 →
Volume 4

Exodus Typology

God redeemed Israel from Egypt by blood, by water and by his own hand, and Scripture returns to it as a pattern. The volume reads the Passover, the sea, the manna, the rock and Sinai in order, holding firm where the New Testament names Christ in them.

View Volume 4 →
Volume 5

Kingdom Typology

Scripture sets two kings side by side: David, chosen, anointed and refused, and Solomon, the son of rest who builds the house. The volume reads them in order, holding firm where the New Testament names Christ as David’s Son and one greater than Solomon.

Prophetic Commentary Series

A series of focused prophetic studies, each tracing one major theme across the whole canon—from Revelation’s riders and beasts to Daniel’s seventy weeks and the final campaign—so the reader sees how Scripture builds each event in its proper prophetic setting.

Volume 1

The Two Riders of Revelation

Revelation shows two riders on white horses, thirteen chapters apart, and many readers take the second as a repeat of the first. The volume argues they are not the same rider and defends that from the whole Bible.

The Beast and the False Prophet

The dragon, the beast and the false prophet stand together against God and his Christ. The volume argues who the third figure is, where he comes from and how his work fits that of the other two, from Revelation 13 to the lake of fire.

The Scarlet Beast and the Woman

Daniel’s Seventy Weeks

Daniel 9:24–27 gives a number, a starting command, a cutting off and a final week. The volume argues where the count begins, what follows the sixty-ninth week, who the prince of verse 26 is and what the seventieth week contains.

Armageddon and the Winepress

Revelation 14, 16 and 19 and Isaiah 63 show a harvest, a gathering, a rider and a figure with garments stained red. The volume argues how these passages fit together as the final campaign of the age.
