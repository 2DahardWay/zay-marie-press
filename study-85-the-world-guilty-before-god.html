STUDY 85 · DIGITAL STUDY

The World Guilty Before God

Romans 1:18–3:20 in Its Own Setting

What does Paul charge against whom in Romans 1:18–3:20, what witness does he name, and where does the charge stop?

Romans 1–3 is often quoted in short pieces: “all have sinned,” “there is none righteous,” “every mouth may be stopped.” The pieces are easy to carry away, and the order in which Paul wrote them is easy to lose. This study slows that movement and reads the passage turn by turn.

It asks four things of every part: whom Paul addresses, what he charges, what witness he names, and what the passage leaves unsaid. It records what each part says and stops at Romans 3:20.

ABOUT THIS STUDY

Ask Whom Paul Charges and With What

You will read Romans 1:7 and 1:16–18 for the address and the sentence on which the passage rests, follow the turns of address (“men,” “O man,” “a Jew,” “both Jews and Gentiles”), read what is revealed and what God “gave them up” to in chapter 1, read the Gentiles and “their conscience also bearing witness” in chapter 2, read the oracles of God and the written sentences, and set six passages on conscience side by side.

Readers who already own Study 54 (Justification), Study 43 (The Gospel of the Grace of God), Study 59 (The Law’s Jurisdiction and the Body of Christ) or Study 35 (Who Is the Israel of God?) will recognize adjoining ground. Study 54 and Study 43 read what begins at Romans 3:21; Study 59 reads the law’s place; Study 35 reads the identity question at Romans 2:28–29. This study stops at 3:20 and asks only what Paul charges against whom, what witness he names, and where the charge stops. The question this study alone answers: what does Romans 1:18–3:20 charge, against whom, and where does it stop?

WHAT YOU’LL STUDY

Follow the Passage Through Its Own Turns

The Turns of Address

Mark where Romans 1:18, 2:1, 2:17 and 3:9 change the person addressed, and note whom each turn charges.

What Is Revealed and Given Up

Read 1:18–32 for what is “manifest” and “clearly seen” and for each sentence in which God “gave them up” or “over.”

The Rendering and the Witness

Read 2:1–16 for the rendering “according to his deeds” and the four things named in verses 14–15, including conscience.

A Jew, Circumcision and the Oracles

Record what 2:17–3:8 says of “a Jew,” of circumcision and of the oracles of God, without adding a label the text does not give.

The Written Sentences

Set 3:10–18 beside the verse that introduces them and the verses that close the passage, and note where the charge stops.

Six Settings of Conscience

Set Romans 2:15 beside five other verses on conscience in Paul’s letters, noting the words beside each.

KEY SCRIPTURES

Read the Word in Its Context

Romans 1:18–20

What is revealed, from where, and what is “manifest” and “clearly seen.”

Romans 1:21–32

The exchange, three sentences in which God “gave them up,” and the list.

Romans 2:1–11

“O man” who judges, and the rendering “according to his deeds.”

Romans 2:12–16

Two groups, the Gentiles “a law unto themselves,” and “their conscience also bearing witness.”

Romans 2:17–29

“Thou art called a Jew,” circumcision, and what verses 28–29 state.

Romans 3:1–8

The advantage, the “oracles of God” and the objections.

Romans 3:9–18

“Both Jews and Gentiles … all under sin,” and the written sentences.

Romans 3:19–20

“Every mouth … stopped” and “all the world … guilty before God,” where the passage stops.

FROM THE STUDY

A Rule for Reading This Subject

Read each passage in its own setting, with its speaker, its audience and its occasion. Make a link between passages only where a passage makes it.

When a text leaves a matter unsaid, record that and stop.
