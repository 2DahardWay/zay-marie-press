2 Peter

Near death, Peter urges his readers to grow in confirmed truth, resist destructive teachers, and live holy lives in view of the prophetic day of the Lord.

TestamentNew Testament
GenreGeneral Epistle
RecipientsThose sharing precious faith with Peter’s audience
Central EmphasisGrowth, discernment, and prophetic certainty

Purpose

Peter writes his second letter as a final reminder before his death. He wants readers established in apostolic truth, diligent in spiritual growth, and assured of entrance into the everlasting kingdom of Jesus Christ.

He warns that false teachers will secretly introduce destructive errors, exploit believers, mock the promised coming, and live corruptly. God’s past judgments and prophetic word guarantee future judgment, the day of the Lord, new heavens and a new earth.

Historical and Biblical Context

Peter refers to Jesus’ prediction of his death, the transfiguration eyewitness, an earlier letter, the prophets, the apostles’ commandment, and Paul’s letters. These references locate the work within the apostolic deposit and late in Peter’s ministry.

Second Peter’s authorship has faced more historical and modern debate than any other New Testament book, particularly because of style and similarities with Jude. The letter explicitly identifies Simon Peter and presents eyewitness testimony. Literary relationship with Jude is real, but exact dependence and direction remain debated.

Structure and Outline

  1. Calling, growth, and confirmed truth — 2 Peter 1
    Divine provision calls for diligent virtue; eyewitness testimony and prophecy confirm the kingdom hope.
  2. False teachers and certain judgment — 2 Peter 2
    Past judgments demonstrate that God rescues the godly and reserves corrupt teachers for punishment.
  3. Scoffers, the day of the Lord, and holy readiness — 2 Peter 3
    Creation, flood, fire, divine patience, and promised new creation answer mockery and motivate holiness.

Major Themes

Knowledge That Produces Growth

Grace and peace multiply through knowledge of Christ. True knowledge bears virtue, self-control, endurance, godliness, affection, and love.

Apostolic and Prophetic Certainty

The kingdom promise is neither myth nor private invention. Peter saw Christ’s majesty, and prophecy came as people spoke from God by the Holy Spirit.

False Teachers

Corrupt teachers combine denial, sensuality, greed, arrogance, empty promises of freedom, and return to defilement.

Divine Judgment and Rescue

Angelic sin, the flood, and Sodom demonstrate that judgment is certain and that the Lord knows how to rescue the godly.

The Day of the Lord and New Creation

Apparent delay reflects divine patience, not failure. The day comes unexpectedly, dissolving the present order and leading to the promised dwelling place of righteousness.

Key Passages

2 Peter 1:3–11Divine provision, diligent growth, and kingdom entrance.
2 Peter 1:12–21Final reminder, transfiguration witness, and prophetic word.
2 Peter 2:1–10False teachers, past judgments, and divine rescue.
2 Peter 2:11–22The character and bondage of corrupt teachers.
2 Peter 3:1–10Scoffers, creation, flood, patience, and the day of the Lord.
2 Peter 3:11–18Holy conduct, new creation, Paul’s letters, and growth in grace.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Read the virtue list as growth grounded in divine provision, not a ladder for earning initial salvation.
  • The transfiguration functions as an eyewitness preview and confirmation of Christ’s kingdom majesty.
  • “Private interpretation” in 1:20 concerns prophecy’s origin rather than forbidding careful individual Bible study; the next verse explains that prophecy came from God through the Spirit.
  • Identify false teachers by the combined doctrinal, moral, and exploitative profile Peter supplies, not merely by disagreement.
  • Peter acknowledges Paul’s wisdom and letters while warning that unstable readers distort them. Citation of Paul does not erase Peter’s own audience and prophetic commission.
  • The day of the Lord, cosmic judgment, and new earth retain their prophetic setting and should not be merged with the Body’s distinct heavenly hope.

Study Questions

  1. How do divine provision and human diligence relate in chapter 1?
  2. What confirms Peter’s proclamation of Christ’s kingdom power?
  3. Which traits expose the false teachers?
  4. How do earlier judgments answer claims that judgment will not come?
  5. Why does Peter describe the Lord’s seeming delay as patience?
  6. How should the promised day and new creation shape present conduct?
