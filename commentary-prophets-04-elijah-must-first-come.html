THE PROPHETS SERIES · VOLUME 4

Elijah Must First Come

Malachi’s Promise, John’s Answer, and the Coming the Lord Still Names

Did John the Baptist fulfil Malachi’s promise of Elijah, or does the promise still wait? What do Gabriel, John and the Lord each say, and what does the text leave unsaid?

Malachi promises Elijah the prophet before the day of the LORD. Gabriel says John will go before the Lord in the spirit and power of Elias. John, asked whether he is Elias, says, “I am not.” The Lord says, “if ye will receive it, this is Elias,” and after John’s death, “Elias truly shall first come, and restore all things.” Are these one statement?

It argues that they are three statements in three settings, and that the text points to a further coming of Elijah before the day, in Israel’s line. In Chapter 12 it sets its reading beside modern writers who agree in part and others who hold the promise closed in John.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not list the views held on Malachi 4:5 and leave the choice to the reader. It says what the author believes the passages state: what Malachi promises, what Gabriel, John and the Lord each say in their own settings, and what the text points to. It gives the evidence from the passages and answers the strongest objections by name.

It is the fourth volume of The Prophets Series, each volume of which is built around one narrower question the prophets raise themselves, not a walk through the prophetic books. Volume 1 asks how the prophets say a word of the LORD is known, Volume 2 what they condemned in Israel’s worship, and Volume 3 what each oracle against a nation says of its own ground, term and ending; this volume asks who the Elijah is that Malachi says will be sent, and whether the promise has been met. The two witnesses of Revelation 11 are set beside the question and not named. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which report what a passage says. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout, checked word for word, and the writers named in Chapter 12 are quoted only from texts the author has read.

FROM THE VOLUME

What This Book Sets Out to Do

Gabriel, John and the Lord each speak of John’s relation to Elijah in different words, and each statement stands in its own setting.

The Lord’s “shall first come” and “is come already” are two sentences spoken in order. The volume reads both in that order before it says what they mean together, and the reader will see where the text leads.
