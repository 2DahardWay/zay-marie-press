Does Romans 10:9–10 Make Confession a Condition of the Body’s Salvation?

From Reader Questions, Volume 1: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 1 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

No, because Romans 10 is not addressed to the Body. It sits inside Romans 9–11, Paul’s address to Israel, and verse 9 is Israel’s covenantal salvation formula, drawn from Deuteronomy 30. Confession belongs to that formula and is Israel’s covenantal expression. It has no saving function for the Body, which is saved by belief alone in the gospel Paul preached (1 Corinthians 15:1–4; Ephesians 2:8–9).

Where the Verse Stands

Romans 10 begins with Paul’s prayer for Israel: “my heart’s desire and prayer to God for Israel is, that they might be saved.” The whole of chapters 9 to 11 is about Israel: their present unbelief, their zeal “not according to knowledge,” their own righteousness set against God’s, and the word of Moses that Paul quotes in verse 8. The mouth-and-heart pattern of verse 9 comes from Deuteronomy 30:14, which was spoken to Israel as a people under covenant. Verses 9 and 10 give what Israel is asked to do about their Messiah: confess him as Lord and believe that God raised him from the dead.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: Romans 10 opens with “for Israel,” and the chapter’s subject is Israel’s unbelief and Israel’s righteousness (10:1–4). Chapters 9 to 11 are Paul’s address to Israel.
  • Stated: the pairing “in thy mouth, and in thy heart” is Moses’ (Deuteronomy 30:14), spoken to Israel, and Paul quotes it in 10:8. Verse 9 builds Israel’s response on that pairing.
  • Stated: the gospel Paul preached to the Body names the death, burial and rising of Christ and says the Body is saved by believing it, “by grace… through faith… not of works” (1 Corinthians 15:1–4; Ephesians 2:8–9). Neither passage names a mouth-act.
  • Shown by the context: verse 9 is Israel’s covenantal formula. It belongs to the Deuteronomy 30 pattern, it is addressed to the people Paul is praying for, and it is not the entry point of the Body into Christ. It is also not the gospel Paul preached in 1 Corinthians 15:1–4.
  • Shown by the context: confession in verses 9 and 10 is part of what Israel is asked to do, not a part of the Body’s salvation. The Body is saved by belief alone, with no mouth-act, ritual or verbal expression added, and confession is not part of the gospel of the grace of God.
  • Inferred: that the saying “no difference between the Jew and the Greek” (10:12) and the words “whosoever shall call” (10:13) are Paul reporting how wide the Lord’s name reaches inside his address to Israel, and that they do not turn verse 9 into the Body’s formula. The text does not say what part the Greek has in Israel’s formula, and the entry does not decide it.

The Other Readings

Confession as the outward expression of the faith that saves, for every believer. Douglas Moo (The Epistle to the Romans, NICNT) describes confession as an outgrowth of saving faith and not a second condition, and counts belief in the heart as the crucial requirement. This reading takes Romans 10 as written to the Body and places confession inside the Body’s salvation, which this entry does not do. Confession as submission to lordship. John MacArthur (The Gospel According to Jesus) reads “Lord” as a confession of surrender that belongs to saving faith, and applies it to every believer. It also makes confession part of salvation. Confession as a required act for every believer. Some traditions treat the confession as a condition added to belief. This has to make Romans 10:9 and Ephesians 2:8–9 one message. Israel’s covenantal formula. This is the reading taken here.

What It Means for the Reader

The reader who belongs to the Body is saved by believing the gospel Paul preached, and nothing is added to it: no confession, no mouth-act, no ritual. Romans 10:9 is Israel’s formula and should not be used as a test, a script or a condition for the Body. The passage shows what Israel is asked to do about its Messiah, and the reader can read it as that without placing himself under it.
