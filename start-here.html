Prophecy and Mystery

Every study, commentary and companion on this site reads Scripture by one distinction. This page frames it in five minutes and then gives you the free charts and guides to read next, in order.

Two things Scripture says were revealed in two different ways

Scripture contrasts truth that God had spoken through the prophets with truth that had been kept secret. Peter and Paul describe them in almost the same words, with opposite results.

Prophecy

“…which God hath spoken by the mouth of all his holy prophets since the world began.”
Acts 3:21

Mystery

“…the revelation of the mystery, which was kept secret since the world began.”
Romans 16:25

The same God and the same Lord Jesus Christ stand at the center of both. The contrast concerns what was revealed, to whom, and where it leads.

How the two lines run through Acts

The book of Acts is where a reader most often loses the distinction. These four points keep it in view.

  1. Before Acts 9Prophecy is already in force. Peter and the Twelve address Israel.
  2. Acts 9Paul is called. The Mystery begins with him.
  3. Acts 9–28Both operate side by side. Neither becomes the other.
  4. Acts 28Israel’s active prophetic program is suspended. The Mystery continues.

Three controls to carry into every study

Keep these in hand when a passage seems to blur the two lines:

  • Concurrency is not identity.
  • Suspension is not cancellation.
  • Suspension is not transfer.
