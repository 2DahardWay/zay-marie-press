Who Is “Taken” and Who Is “Left” in Matthew 24:40–41?

From Reader Questions, Volume 2: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 2 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

The one “taken” is taken away in judgment, as the flood “took them all away,” and the one “left” remains to enter the kingdom. The passage is Israel’s separation at the Lord’s coming, spoken to disciples on the Mount of Olives. It is not the gathering of the Body.

Where the Verse Stands

Matthew 24 answers a question put by the disciples “privately” on the mount of Olives: “what shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world?” (24:3). The answer speaks of “this gospel of the kingdom” (24:14), of Judaea and the holy place (24:15–16) and of a flight that must not fall on “the sabbath day” (24:20). The words about two in the field and two at the mill close that answer, and they are tied by the word “as” to the days of Noah.

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
24:3“And as he sat upon the mount of Olives, the disciples came unto him privately, saying, Tell us, when shall these things be? and what shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world?”Disciples ask about “thy coming” and “the end of the world.”
24:37–41“But as the days of Noah were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. Then shall two be in the field; the one shall be taken, and the other left. Two women shall be grinding at the mill; the one shall be taken, and the other left.”The flood “took them all away.” Then one is “taken,” one “left.”
Luke 17:34–37“I tell you, in that night there shall be two men in one bed; the one shall be taken, and the other shall be left. Two women shall be grinding together; the one shall be taken, and the other left. Two men shall be in the field; the one shall be taken, and the other left. And they answered and said unto him, Where, Lord? And he said unto them, Wheresoever the body is, thither will the eagles be gathered together.”The same sayings; the disciples ask “Where, Lord?” The answer is the body and the eagles.
25:34“Then shall the King say unto them on his right hand, Come, ye blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world:”Those on the right hand “inherit the kingdom.”

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the comparison is to Noah: the people “knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be.” The one who is “taken” in the comparison is taken by the flood.
  • Stated: in Luke’s form the disciples ask where, and the answer is “Wheresoever the body is, thither will the eagles be gathered together,” which is the picture of judgment on the dead.
  • Shown by the context: the audience and setting are Israel’s: the disciples ask about the kingdom age, Judaea, the holy place and the sabbath. Nothing in the chapter is addressed to a company called out of the nations.
  • Shown by the context: the ones who are left are the ones who go on to the kingdom. In the next chapter the King says to those on his right hand, “inherit the kingdom prepared for you” (25:34).
  • Inferred: that “taken” in 24:40–41 carries the sense it has in 24:39. The Greek verb is a different one in the two places, so the argument rests on the comparison the Lord himself draws, not on the word.

The Other Readings

The one taken is gathered to the Lord. This is the popular reading, and it takes the sayings as a picture of the gathering of the church. It reads “taken” as received, as in John 14:3, “I will come again, and receive you unto myself.” It must explain why the comparison with the flood, in which the ones taken are the ones destroyed, gives the opposite result, and why a chapter spoken to the disciples about Judaea and the sabbath is turned to a company that has no such setting. The one taken is taken in judgment. Stanley Toussaint (Behold the King) takes the sayings this way, and so do many who hold that the gathering of the Body comes before the tribulation, since this reading finds no gathering of the Body in Matthew 24. That is the reading taken here, and the entry argues it from the flood comparison and the chapter’s own audience.

ReadingWhat it must sayWhere it strains
Taken to the LordThe one taken is received, as in John 14:3.The flood comparison reverses it; the audience and setting are Israel’s.
Taken in judgmentThe one taken is removed as the flood removed the unprepared.The verb differs between verses 39 and 40–41.

What It Means for the Reader

The sayings are a warning about readiness at the Lord’s coming, and a reader who takes them as a promise of the Body’s gathering has taken the wrong end of the comparison. The gathering of the Body is described elsewhere, in words of comfort: “the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds” (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17).
