Habakkuk

Faith amid coming judgment.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreMinor Prophets
Primary SettingJudah before Babylonian invasion
Key MovementFrom protest over injustice to worshipful trust amid national calamity
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

Habakkuk records a prophet’s dialogue with God about violence in Judah and the troubling use of Babylon as an instrument of judgment. God assures him that arrogant power will also be judged and that the righteous must live faithfully while awaiting the appointed vision. The book ends in trembling yet resilient joy.

Historical and Biblical Context

The rise of the Chaldeans places the prophecy near Babylon’s advance against Judah. Unlike many prophetic books, Habakkuk primarily speaks to God and then reports the divine answers.

Evidence and inference: Prophetic texts join historical setting, covenant proclamation, symbolic imagery, and future promise. This summary distinguishes explicit textual claims from debated conclusions about date, structure, chronology, or fulfillment.

Structure and Outline

  1. First complaint and answer — Habakkuk 1:1–11
    Judah’s unchecked violence troubles the prophet; God announces the Chaldeans.
  2. Second complaint and vision — Habakkuk 1:12–2:5
    Habakkuk questions Babylon’s use and is told to wait faithfully for the appointed revelation.
  3. Five woes — Habakkuk 2:6–20
    Plunder, exploitation, violence, immorality, and idolatry condemn the arrogant empire.
  4. Prayer of trust — Habakkuk 3
    The prophet recalls divine triumph and rejoices despite anticipated loss.

Major Themes

Faithful Waiting

The righteous lives by faithfulness while the vision awaits fulfillment.

Divine Sovereignty

God can use a wicked nation without approving or excusing its evil.

Judgment of Pride

The conqueror’s arrogance and violence bring corresponding woes.

Joy amid Loss

Faith rests in God Himself when visible agricultural and economic support disappears.

Key Passages

Habakkuk 1:2–4The prophet protests violence and paralyzed justice.
Habakkuk 1:5–11God announces Babylon’s astonishing rise.
Habakkuk 1:12–2:1The prophet questions the use of a more wicked nation.
Habakkuk 2:2–5The vision awaits its time; pride contrasts with faithfulness.
Habakkuk 2:6–20Five woes pronounce judgment upon exploitation and idolatry.
Habakkuk 3:16–19Trembling coexists with joy in the God of salvation.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Do not detach “the righteous shall live by faith” from waiting for God’s promised judgment in its original setting.
  • God’s sovereign use of Babylon does not make Babylon morally righteous.
  • The woes answer the prophet’s second complaint by guaranteeing accountability.
  • Later apostolic use develops the faith principle without erasing Habakkuk’s historical meaning.

Study Questions

  1. Why is God’s first answer troubling to Habakkuk?
  2. What contrast structures Habakkuk 2:4?
  3. How do the woes answer imperial arrogance?
  4. What enables joy in chapter 3?
