What Does “Baptized for the Dead” Mean in 1 Corinthians 15:29?

From Reader Questions, Volume 2: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 2 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

It names a practice of some at Corinth, spoken of as “they,” and Paul uses it as an argument without commending it or commanding it. The passage is part of his proof that the dead rise. The Body has no ordinance of baptism on behalf of the dead.

Where the Verses Stand

First Corinthians 15 answers some at Corinth who said “there is no resurrection of the dead.” Paul argues from Christ’s rising, then asks what follows if the dead do not rise. The question about baptism for the dead is followed by Paul’s own case: “why stand we in jeopardy every hour?”

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
15:12–13“Now if Christ be preached that he rose from the dead, how say some among you that there is no resurrection of the dead? But if there be no resurrection of the dead, then is Christ not risen:”Some say there is no resurrection; then Christ is not risen.
15:29–30“Else what shall they do which are baptized for the dead, if the dead rise not at all? why are they then baptized for the dead? And why stand we in jeopardy every hour?”“Else what shall they do which are baptized for the dead”; “why stand we in jeopardy?”
15:31–32“I protest by your rejoicing which I have in Christ Jesus our Lord, I die daily. If after the manner of men I have fought with beasts at Ephesus, what advantageth it me, if the dead rise not? let us eat and drink; for to morrow we die.”“I die daily”; “what advantageth it me, if the dead rise not?”
12:13“For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit.”“By one Spirit are we all baptized into one body.”

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: Paul speaks of the baptized “for the dead” as “they.” In the next verse he turns to “we,” asking why he and his companions stand in danger. The practice is placed outside the “we” of the apostle’s own company.
  • Stated: Paul asks what “they” will do “if the dead rise not at all.” The practice is brought forward as something that makes no sense on the denial of the resurrection, not as a rule.
  • Shown by the context: the whole chapter is an argument for the resurrection, and verse 29 stands in the series of arguments (verses 29–32) from what is done: baptism for the dead, Paul’s own danger, his fighting “with beasts at Ephesus.”
  • Shown by the context: Paul’s own statement of the baptism that makes the Body is in 12:13: “by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body.” That baptism is not for another person.
  • Inferred: that the practice was a proxy baptism done by some Corinthians for dead believers. The text does not describe the practice, and the argument rests on the natural reading of “for the dead” and the “they.”

The Other Readings

A proxy baptism practised by some, not approved. The natural reading of the words is a vicarious baptism done by some in Corinth, which Paul mentions without approval. That is the reading taken here. A wide range of other proposals. Anthony Thiselton (The First Epistle to the Corinthians, New International Greek Testament Commentary) surveys dozens of proposals, among them baptism in the hope of joining the dead, baptism in the place of those who died, and baptism taken as suffering. Each must explain the preposition “for” and the unexplained “they.”

ReadingWhat it must sayWhere it strains
Proxy baptism practised by someSome at Corinth are baptized for dead persons; Paul reports it.The text does not describe it.
Baptism in hope of the resurrectionBaptism taken “for” the sake of the dead’s rising.Strains “for the dead” and the “they.”

What It Means for the Reader

The reader should not take from the verse a ceremony for the dead. Paul’s gospel offers no baptism by proxy, and the single baptism he gives the Body is the Spirit’s. The verse is there to show that without the resurrection, much of what believers do makes no sense.
