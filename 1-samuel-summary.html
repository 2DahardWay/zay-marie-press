1 Samuel

Israel moves from the judges to monarchy as Samuel ministers, Saul is rejected, and David is chosen and preserved.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreHistorical Narrative
Primary SettingIsrael during the rise of the monarchy
Key MovementFrom Samuel’s birth to Saul’s death

Purpose

First Samuel explains Israel’s move to kingship and contrasts leadership that listens to God with leadership governed by fear, self-interest, and outward appearance. Samuel, Saul, and David dominate the narrative, but the LORD remains Israel’s true ruler.

Historical and Biblical Context

The book opens in the declining days of Eli’s priesthood. Samuel becomes prophet and judge, the ark narrative exposes false confidence, and Israel demands a king like the nations. Saul’s rise and rejection prepare for David’s anointing and long period of testing.

Evidence and inference: This summary distinguishes the narrative’s explicit claims from historical reconstruction and interpretive inference. Where authorship, date, chronology, or structure is debated, the book’s received form and stated theological movement remain primary.

Structure and Outline

  1. Samuel and the house of Eli — 1 Samuel 1–7
    Samuel is born and called; Eli’s house is judged; the ark is captured and returned.
  2. The request for a king — 1 Samuel 8–12
    Israel demands monarchy, Saul is selected, and Samuel renews the nation’s accountability.
  3. Saul’s disobedience and rejection — 1 Samuel 13–15
    Saul violates God’s commands and is rejected from the dynasty.
  4. David anointed and tested — 1 Samuel 16–27
    David defeats Goliath, serves Saul, and flees repeated attempts on his life.
  5. Saul’s final collapse — 1 Samuel 28–31
    Saul consults a medium and dies in battle with his sons.

Major Themes

Hearing and Obedience

Samuel hears God’s word; Saul repeatedly substitutes expediency for obedience.

Kingship under God

Israel’s king is not autonomous but accountable to the LORD’s command.

Divine Choice

God rejects outward criteria and chooses David according to His purpose.

Providential Preservation

David refuses to seize the throne by killing Saul and waits through sustained danger.

Key Passages

1 Samuel 3:1–21Samuel is called as a prophet.
1 Samuel 7:3–13Israel repents and the LORD grants deliverance.
1 Samuel 8:4–22Israel demands a king like the nations.
1 Samuel 13:8–14Saul’s unlawful action exposes failed kingship.
1 Samuel 15:17–23Samuel declares that obedience is better than sacrifice.
1 Samuel 16:1–13David is anointed while God rejects outward appearance.
1 Samuel 17:32–50David confronts Goliath in the name of the LORD.
1 Samuel 24:1–22David spares Saul and refuses self-appointed vengeance.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Evaluate Israel’s request for a king in light of both earlier provision for monarchy and the motives condemned in 1 Samuel 8.
  • Divine empowerment for office does not guarantee enduring obedience or approval.
  • Do not romanticize David; the narrative commends specific acts while later books disclose serious failures.
  • The ark narratives reject treating sacred objects as guarantees detached from covenant faithfulness.
  • Israel’s monarchy belongs to the kingdom and Prophecy setting and is not an institutional pattern for the Body of Christ.

Study Questions

  1. How do Hannah’s prayer and song anticipate themes of the book?
  2. What does the ark narrative teach about God’s holiness?
  3. Why is Israel’s request for a king condemned?
  4. How do Saul’s failures reveal his governing priorities?
  5. How does David’s conduct toward Saul prepare him for kingship?
