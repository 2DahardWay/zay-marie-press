What Does “Call upon the Name of the Lord” Mean in Romans 10:13?

From Reader Questions, Volume 4: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 4 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

It is Israel’s own way of seeking the Lord’s deliverance. Paul quotes the line from Joel 2:32, where “whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be delivered” belongs to the day of the Lord, to “mount Zion and in Jerusalem” and to “the remnant whom the Lord shall call.” Romans 9–11 is addressed to Israel and concerns Israel, and Paul’s point at 10:12–13 is that the Lord is “the same Lord over all” and that no difference is made between the Jew and the Greek who call on him. The phrase is Israel’s covenantal cry for deliverance; for the Body it is not a sinner’s prayer and not the way of being saved. Calling expresses faith and is not the mechanism of salvation. The Body is saved by believing the gospel Paul preached (1 Corinthians 15:1–4), and what Paul says of the Body’s salvation is in Ephesians 2:8–9.

Where the Verse Stands

Romans 10 opens with Paul’s prayer for Israel, “that they might be saved,” and with the charge that they have “a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge,” going about to establish their own righteousness. He then draws on Deuteronomy 30 for the word that is “nigh thee,” gives the confession and belief of 10:9–10, and closes the thought with two Scripture quotations, Isaiah 28:16 and Joel 2:32. Joel’s verse sits in a passage about the outpouring of the Spirit on Israel, signs in the heavens “before the great and the terrible day of the Lord,” and deliverance in Zion.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: Romans 10:13 quotes Joel 2:32 and Acts 2:21 quotes the same verse, both to Israel. The line has a home in Israel’s prophets before Paul uses it.
  • Stated: Joel gives the verse its terms: the call is made in a time of judgment, the deliverance is “in mount Zion and in Jerusalem,” and it reaches “the remnant whom the Lord shall call.” In Joel the call is the way of Israel’s deliverance on the day of the Lord.
  • Shown by the context: Paul’s chapters concern Israel, whom he longs to see saved (10:1), and his point at 10:12 is that the Lord’s riches reach the Greek who calls as well as the Jew who calls. The “no difference” is about who may call within that promise; it is not an announcement of a way for the Body to be saved.
  • Shown by the context: where Paul says how the Body is saved he does not use this line. He writes of the gospel “which I preached unto you” (1 Corinthians 15:1–4) and of “by grace are ye saved through faith” (Ephesians 2:8). When he describes the Corinthian saints as “all that in every place call upon the name of Jesus Christ our Lord” (1 Corinthians 1:2) he is naming who they are, not giving a condition.
  • Inferred: that the phrase carries Joel’s sense in Romans 10, the cry of the one who looks to the Lord for the deliverance he has promised. The argument rests on the quotation, on Joel’s setting and on the chapters’ concern with Israel.

The Other Readings

A universal gospel promise. Many commentaries read Romans 10:13 as the promise of salvation to everyone who looks to Jesus as Lord, Paul applying Joel’s “Lord” to Christ. It must explain why the chapter’s subject is Israel, why Paul’s own statements of how the Body is saved do not use the line, and why Joel’s verse has its own remnant and Zion. Deliverance of Israel’s remnant. Others read Joel’s verse of the survival and deliverance of the faithful in the day of the Lord, which is the sense taken here.

What It Means for the Reader

The phrase tells what Israel’s remnant does in the day Joel describes, and Paul’s use of it shows that the Greek who calls is not shut out of that promise. It does not give the Body a formula. Where the Body is told how it is saved, the words are belief in the gospel of Christ’s death for our sins, his burial and his rising (1 Corinthians 15:3–4), (Ephesians 2:8).
