The Eternal Purpose of God

One Purpose in Christ, and the Two Callings That Serve It

If God has one purpose, how can it be served by two callings that Scripture will not merge?

God’s purpose was settled in Christ before the ages, and it stands. This commentary argues that it is served through two callings: Israel’s, which is about the promises, the land and the Kingdom on the earth, and the Body’s, the mystery hid in God until it was made known through Paul, whose blessings, seat and hope are in the heavenly places. It argues that the two meet in Christ’s headship over all things in heaven and on earth, and that neither is absorbed by the other.

It follows the purpose from Isaiah through the Gospels and Acts to Paul and Revelation, and it reads the passages most often used to make the church the new Israel, or to fold Israel into the church. In Chapter 9 it sets its reading beside seven modern writers, three who stand with it and four who read the matter differently, and answers each in his own words.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not survey the readings of God’s purpose and leave the choice to the reader. It states what the author believes the passages teach, sets out the evidence from Isaiah through the Gospels to the letters of Paul and the Revelation, and answers the strongest objections in the objectors’ own terms, naming the modern writers who hold them.

It is the fourth volume of the Covenantal Commentary Series. The first asked what God promised Abraham and to whom the promise belongs; the second asked who the seed is; the third asked what the line was given to possess; this one asks what the whole is for. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which walk through a passage and report what the text says. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout, and the writers named in Chapter 9 are quoted only from their own published words.
