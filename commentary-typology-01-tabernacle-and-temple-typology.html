TYPOLOGY COMMENTARY SERIES · VOLUME 1

Tabernacle & Temple Typology

The Pattern Shown in the Mount, Its Fulfilment in Christ, and Where It Does Not Pass to the Body

What was the tabernacle a pattern of, and whose dwelling is it?

God showed Moses a pattern on the mount, and Scripture itself calls what was built from it a shadow, a copy and a figure of heavenly things. This commentary reads the court, the altar, the Holy Place, the veil, the mercy seat and the Day of Atonement, marking which parts the New Testament names as pointing to Christ and which parts it only echoes.

It follows the house from Solomon to the temple of His body, walks Hebrews 8–10 in order, and sets Paul’s temple language for the Body beside the sanctuaries that Daniel, the Lord, Paul and the Revelation place still to come. In Chapter 10 it sets its reading beside modern writers who see it differently.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not survey what has been taught about the tabernacle and leave the choice to the reader. It states what the author believes the passages teach, sets out the evidence from the Law, the Prophets, the Gospels, the Acts, the letters and the Revelation, and answers the strongest objections, naming the modern writers who hold them.

It is the first volume of the Typology Commentary Series. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which walk through passages and report what the text says. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout, and the writers named in Chapter 10 are quoted only from words the author has read in full.

FROM THE VOLUME

What This Book Sets Out to Do

The tabernacle and the temples that followed belong to Israel’s program. They are fulfilled in Christ and in the temple of the Kingdom still to come.

The volume asks of each passage what it names, what it only echoes, to whom it speaks and where it says the pattern ends.
