STUDY 68 · DIGITAL STUDY

The Book of Life

Whose Names Are Written

What does each passage that speaks of a book of names actually say — and to whom is it addressed?

The phrase “book of life” occurs eight times in the King James text, yet the idea of a written register of names runs from Moses to John and attracts strong claims in both directions. The way through is to read each passage where it stands before deciding what any of them means together.

This study begins with Israel’s registers, follows the blotting-out passages, Daniel, Jesus, and Paul, and then reads Revelation from the letter to Sardis to the scene at the throne, holding to one order throughout: meaning first, assignment second, application third.

ABOUT THIS STUDY

Read the Passages in Order: Meaning, Assignment, Application

You will collect Israel’s registers, follow the verb “blot out” to its speakers, read Daniel 12:1, Luke 10:20, and Philippians 4:3 for who is speaking and to whom, and then work through Revelation 3:5, the phrases “from the foundation of the world,” and Revelation 20. Application comes last, and it is drawn only after each passage has been read where it stands.

Readers who already own Study 31 (The Judgments of Scripture), Study 49 (The Resurrections of Scripture), or Study 46 (Secure in Christ) will recognize adjoining ground. Study 31 classifies the judgments by subject, time, standard, and outcome; this study touches the Great White Throne only for the books and the Book of Life that Revelation 20 names. Study 49 traces the distinct resurrections, including the resurrection of the unjust; this study takes that resurrection as given and does not re-derive it. Study 46 argues the Body’s security from the seal, the earnest, and Romans 8; this study reads Revelation 3:5 in its own setting and does not re-argue that security. The question this study alone answers: what does each passage that speaks of a book of names say about it, whose names does it record, and what do the texts state about removal?

WHAT YOU’LL STUDY

Trace the Passages Before You Conclude

Israel’s Registers

Collect the Old Testament registers, including Isaiah 4:3, Ezekiel 13:9, Psalm 87:6, and Malachi 3:16, and observe whose names each one records and for what purpose.

The Verb “Blot Out”

Trace the wording of Exodus 32:32–33 and Psalm 69:28, following the verb “blot out,” the speaker, and the sin each passage names.

Daniel’s Time of Trouble

Follow Daniel 12:1 through its own markers, including the time of trouble, the people addressed, and the verse that follows.

Speaker and Audience

Compare Luke 10:20 and Philippians 4:3 by asking who is speaking, to whom, and what each writer states about the names.

Sardis and the Foundation

Follow the promise of Revelation 3:5 and the phrases “from the foundation of the world” (13:8; 17:8) through the letters’ own markers and the wording of each verse.

The Scene at the Throne

Apply a repeatable discipline for reading Revelation 20:11–15, and for testing four common readings of the Book of Life without transferring any passage to a different audience.

KEY SCRIPTURES

Read the Word in Its Context

Exodus 32:30–35

Moses at Sinai after the calf, and the LORD’s answer about His book.

Psalm 69:19–28

David’s prayer against his enemies and the book of the living.

Daniel 12:1–3

The time of trouble and those found written in the book.

Luke 10:17–20

The seventy return, and names written in heaven.

Philippians 4:1–3

Paul’s fellow labourers, whose names are in the book of life.

Revelation 3:1–5

The letter to Sardis and the promise to the overcomer.

Revelation 13:8; 17:8

The Lamb’s book and names not written from the foundation of the world.

Revelation 20:11–15

The great white throne, the books, and another book.
