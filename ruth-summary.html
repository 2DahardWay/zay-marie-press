Ruth

Through covenant loyalty, providence, and lawful redemption, God preserves a family line that leads to David.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreHistorical Narrative
Primary SettingBethlehem and Moab during the days of the judges
Key MovementFrom emptiness and death to fullness and family

Purpose

Ruth shows God’s providential care through ordinary faithfulness. The story traces Naomi’s movement from loss to restoration, Ruth’s loyal commitment, and Boaz’s responsible action as a redeemer, culminating in the genealogy of David.

Historical and Biblical Context

The narrative occurs during the days of the judges but presents a striking contrast to that era’s disorder. Famine drives Elimelech’s family to Moab, where death leaves Naomi and Ruth vulnerable. Their return to Bethlehem during harvest opens the way for provision and redemption.

Evidence and inference: This summary distinguishes the narrative’s explicit claims from historical reconstruction and interpretive inference. Where authorship, date, chronology, or structure is debated, the book’s received form and stated theological movement remain primary.

Structure and Outline

  1. Loss and return — Ruth 1
    Naomi loses husband and sons; Ruth pledges loyalty and returns with her to Bethlehem.
  2. Providential provision — Ruth 2
    Ruth gleans in the field of Boaz and receives protection and generosity.
  3. Appeal for redemption — Ruth 3
    Ruth asks Boaz to act as redeemer, and he responds honorably.
  4. Redemption and genealogy — Ruth 4
    Boaz resolves the nearer-redeemer claim, marries Ruth, and fathers the line leading to David.

Major Themes

Loyal Love

Ruth’s commitment and Boaz’s generosity embody durable covenant loyalty.

Providence

God’s guidance often appears through timely ordinary events rather than spectacular intervention.

Redemption and Responsibility

Boaz acts within Israel’s legal and familial structures to preserve inheritance and family.

From Emptiness to Fullness

Naomi’s lament is answered through provision, community, and the birth of Obed.

Key Passages

Ruth 1:6–18Ruth binds herself to Naomi, Israel, and Israel’s God.
Ruth 2:1–13Ruth meets Boaz while gleaning.
Ruth 2:19–23Naomi identifies Boaz as a relative-redeemer.
Ruth 3:8–18Boaz promises to pursue redemption honorably.
Ruth 4:1–12The legal redemption is settled at the gate.
Ruth 4:13–22Obed is born and the genealogy leads to David.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Read Ruth against the covenant provisions for gleaning, family responsibility, and inheritance.
  • Do not reduce the threshing-floor scene to romance; the narrative emphasizes Ruth’s appeal and Boaz’s integrity.
  • The exact relation among the levirate and redemption customs requires care because the story draws on related legal institutions.
  • The narrator explicitly identifies divine provision at key points while leaving many providential connections implicit.
  • The Davidic genealogy gives the domestic story a national and royal horizon.

Study Questions

  1. How does Ruth contrast with the wider atmosphere of Judges?
  2. What changes in Naomi’s language from chapter 1 to chapter 4?
  3. How does Boaz go beyond minimal legal obligation?
  4. What does the nearer redeemer’s decision reveal about inheritance and reputation?
  5. Why does the book end with David’s genealogy?
