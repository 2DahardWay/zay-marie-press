Marriage and Singleness

1 Corinthians 7 in Its Own Setting, and Whose Word Each Instruction Is

Whose word is each instruction in 1 Corinthians 7, to whom is it addressed, and what does it say?

First Corinthians 7 is read quickly and carried far. A reader meets “It is good for a man not to touch a woman” or “let him not put her away” and the mind moves at once to rules for every married and every unmarried person. This study slows that movement and reads the chapter in the order Paul wrote it.

It asks four things of every instruction: who is addressed, what is said, whose word Paul says it is, and what the chapter leaves unsaid. It records what each passage says and stops where the text stops.

ABOUT THIS STUDY

Ask Whose Word Each Instruction Is

You will read 1 Corinthians 7 as an answer to a letter, mark the phrases by which Paul distinguishes his instructions (“not I, but the Lord,” “not the Lord,” “by permission,” “my judgment”), read what is said of husband and wife, the unmarried and widows, set verses 10–11 beside Matthew 19 and Mark 10, follow the three statements of one rule in verses 17–24, and test three common readings of the chapter against what the text states.

Readers who already own Study 48 (The Christian Household), Study 1 (The Bride of Christ) or Study 36 (Jews, Gentiles, and the Church of God) will recognize adjoining ground. Study 48 reads the instruction to husbands and wives in Ephesians 5 and Colossians 3; Study 1 reads Paul’s marriage imagery; Study 36 reads other ground in the same letter and its neighbours. This study asks only what 1 Corinthians 7 says to the married, the unmarried and the widowed, and whose word each instruction is. The question this study alone answers: what does Paul say in 1 Corinthians 7, to whom, and on whose authority?

WHAT YOU’LL STUDY

Follow the Chapter Through Its Own Markers

A Letter Answering a Letter

Trace the opening phrase of verse 1 and the same form at verse 25, and note where the chapter says its instructions begin.

Duty, Consent and Widows

Record what verses 1–9 say of husband and wife, of the unmarried and of widows, and which verbs the passage uses.

Three Passages Side by Side

Set 7:10–11 beside Matthew 19 and Mark 10, listing where the wording agrees and where it differs.

The Believer and the Unbeliever

Read verses 12–16 for who is addressed, what each party is told to do and what the text does not say.

One Rule, Three Statements

Observe how verses 17, 20 and 24 repeat one rule with circumcision and bondage as examples.

Verses 25–40 Tested

Sort what the chapter states about virgins, the present distress and the time from what readers add.

KEY SCRIPTURES

Read the Word in Its Context

1 Corinthians 7:1–5

The Corinthians’ letter, and the duty and consent of husband and wife.

1 Corinthians 7:6–9

Permission, not commandment; the gift of each and what is said to the unmarried and widows.

1 Corinthians 7:10–11

“Yet not I, but the Lord”: the wife and the husband and what each is told.

Matthew 19:3–12

The Lord’s answer on putting away, set beside Paul’s statement in its own setting.

1 Corinthians 7:12–16

“To the rest speak I, not the Lord”: a believer and an unbeliever in one house.

1 Corinthians 7:17–24

One rule stated three times, with circumcision and bondage as examples.

1 Corinthians 7:25–35

Virgins, the present distress and the time that is short.

1 Corinthians 7:39–40

The wife bound while her husband lives, and Paul’s judgment at the close.

FROM THE STUDY

A Rule for Reading This Subject

Read each passage in its own setting, with its speaker, its audience and its occasion. Make a link between passages only where a passage makes it.

When a text leaves a matter unsaid, record that and stop.
