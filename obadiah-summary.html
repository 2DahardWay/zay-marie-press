Obadiah

Edom judged; Zion restored.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreMinor Prophets
Primary SettingJudah after a violent assault on Jerusalem
Key MovementFrom Edom’s pride and violence to the LORD’s kingdom
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

Obadiah condemns Edom for pride, violence, gloating, plunder, and betrayal when Jerusalem suffered. Edom’s treatment of Jacob brings corresponding judgment, while Mount Zion receives deliverance and the kingdom belongs to the LORD.

Historical and Biblical Context

The prophecy reflects a catastrophe in Jerusalem, commonly connected with Babylon’s destruction, though the brief book does not state the date directly. The hostility between Edom and Jacob supplies the family and national background.

Evidence and inference: Prophetic texts join historical setting, covenant proclamation, symbolic imagery, and future promise. This summary distinguishes explicit textual claims from debated conclusions about date, structure, chronology, or fulfillment.

Structure and Outline

  1. Edom’s pride brought low — Obadiah 1–9
    Mountain security, wisdom, and allies cannot prevent judgment.
  2. Violence against Jacob — Obadiah 10–14
    Edom stood aloof, rejoiced, plundered, and blocked fugitives.
  3. The day of the LORD — Obadiah 15–16
    Edom’s conduct returns upon its own head as judgment reaches the nations.
  4. Zion restored — Obadiah 17–21
    Jacob receives inheritance, Edom is consumed, and the kingdom is the LORD’s.

Major Themes

Pride

False security exalts Edom against God.

Brotherly Violence

Kinship makes Edom’s betrayal especially grievous.

Corresponding Judgment

As Edom has done, so it will be done.

Zion and Kingdom

Deliverance, holiness, possession, and divine rule conclude the prophecy.

Key Passages

Obadiah 1–4Edom’s lofty pride is challenged.
Obadiah 10–14The prophet catalogs violence against Jacob.
Obadiah 15–16The day of the LORD applies recompense.
Obadiah 17–18Zion receives deliverance while Esau’s house faces judgment.
Obadiah 19–21Israel’s territories are possessed and the kingdom belongs to the LORD.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Do not generalize Edom into a timeless symbol before recognizing the named nation and historical hostility.
  • The exact Jerusalem crisis may be debated, but Edom’s conduct is explicit.
  • The day of the LORD expands judgment beyond Edom without erasing Edom’s particular guilt.
  • Zion and Israel’s inheritance retain their prophetic identity.

Study Questions

  1. Which actions make Edom guilty?
  2. How does the book reverse Edom’s security?
  3. What principle governs the day of the LORD?
  4. How does Zion’s future contrast with Edom’s?
