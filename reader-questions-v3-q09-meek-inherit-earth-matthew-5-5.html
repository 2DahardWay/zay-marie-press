Who Are “the Meek” Who “Inherit the Earth” in Matthew 5:5?

From Reader Questions, Volume 3: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 3 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

This is Kingdom prophecy, spoken by Christ to Israel. The meek are Israel’s faithful remnant, those who wait on the Lord and leave vengeance to him, as Psalm 37 says in the very words the Lord uses: “the meek shall inherit the earth.” They are the ones who will enter and inherit the earthly kingdom of the Messiah. The Body of Christ does not inherit the earth: its destiny is “in heavenly places.” The promise belongs to Israel’s covenant hope and is not transferred to the Body.

Where the Verse Stands

The Sermon on the Mount follows a summary of the Lord’s work: “preaching the gospel of the kingdom” and healing, with crowds from Galilee, Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judaea and beyond Jordan (4:23–25). Seeing the multitudes, he goes up the mountain, and “his disciples came unto him.” The beatitudes are the opening of what he says. Each carries a promise, and two of them end in the same words: “for theirs is the kingdom of heaven” (5:3, 10).

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: Matthew 5:5 repeats Psalm 37:11 almost word for word, and the psalm says who the meek are: those who “wait upon the Lord,” who do not fret at the prosperity of the wicked and who “cease from anger” (37:7–9).
  • Stated: the beatitudes are kingdom promises. The first and the last end with the kingdom of heaven, and the Lord’s own prayer in the same sermon asks, “Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.”
  • Shown by the context: the psalm’s promise is repeated five times (37:9, 11, 22, 29, 34) and the word is “land” as often as “earth.” The promise is the earth under the reign of the righteous and the end of the wicked, and Isaiah 60:21 speaks of Israel’s inheriting “the land for ever.”
  • Shown by the context: the audience is the Lord’s own disciples with the multitudes of Israel around them (4:25). The sermon is given to them, and it speaks of a kingdom they were told was “at hand.”
  • Inferred: that “the earth” is the earth of the coming kingdom under the King, and not a spiritual possession or only the eternal state. The text does not say when, and the argument rests on the kingdom setting of the sermon and the psalm’s own words.

The Other Readings

The new earth and the age to come. Many commentaries take the inheritance as the consummated kingdom, the new earth, shared by all who belong to Christ. It must explain why the promise is stated in Israel’s words to Israel’s disciples and then widened to the Body. The earth of the kingdom the King will rule. Others take the promise as the earthly kingdom of the Messiah, which is the reading taken here, with the added point that it is spoken to Israel’s disciples and is not the Body’s inheritance. A spiritual possession now. Some take the promise as the meek having peace and the earth’s goods in a spiritual sense. It must explain why the psalm and Isaiah speak of the land.

What It Means for the Reader

Meekness in Scripture is not weakness. Moses was “very meek, above all the men which were upon the face of the earth” (Numbers 12:3), and the King says of himself, “I am meek and lowly in heart” (Matthew 11:29). The verse promises that Israel’s faithful remnant, the ones who wait on the Lord and do not take vengeance, will be given the earth when the King reigns. It is part of Israel’s hope and not Pauline doctrine. The Body’s own promise is of another kind, “in heavenly places in Christ.”
