PURPOSE AND BENEFIT

How This Resource Helps You

This resource helps you move beyond collecting isolated verses and toward building doctrinal conclusions that account for context, audience, progressive revelation, and divine program. It provides a disciplined structure for organizing biblical evidence, identifying the strength of each claim, recognizing unresolved questions, and stating conclusions with appropriate care.

  • Define the doctrinal question before beginning research.
  • Keep meaning, audience, and assignment visible.
  • Compare supporting and challenging evidence.
  • Match the certainty of the conclusion to the evidence.

How to Use This Resource

  1. Begin with one clearly defined doctrinal question.
  2. Read every major passage in its immediate and broader context.
  3. Complete one Doctrine Evidence Record for each controlling passage.
  4. Identify the audience, revelatory setting, and program or assignment.
  5. Use the Doctrinal Synthesis Worksheet to compare the evidence.
  6. Record passages that challenge or qualify the proposed conclusion.
  7. Classify the conclusion and write only what the evidence warrants.
  8. Return to the passages and revise the conclusion when necessary.
A DISCIPLINED STUDY MAP

Organize the Evidence Without Erasing Biblical Distinctions

Sound doctrine begins with the meaning of each passage in its own setting. The resource follows the sequence meaning first, assignment second, application third, while preserving audience, progressive revelation, Israel and the Body of Christ, and the Acts 9–28 overlap.

Define the Question

Frame one answerable doctrinal question before collecting passages or defending a conclusion.

Classify the Evidence

Qualify the Conclusion

Distinguish explicit teaching, necessary inference, reasonable inference, and speculation.
