Who Is the Male Child of Revelation 12:5?

From Reader Questions, Volume 2: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 2 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

The male child is the company of the 144,000, Israel’s firstfruits remnant, and not the person of Christ. The child is “caught up unto God,” a word of removal used of those taken up, and the same company reappears with the Lamb on mount Sion in Revelation 14:1–5 as “firstfruits.” The rod of iron is shared with overcomers in Revelation 2:26–27.

Where the Verses Stand

Revelation 12 is a sign: a woman, a dragon and a child. The child’s identity decides how the chapter is read. The usual answer is Christ, and the chapter does say that the child is to “rule all nations with a rod of iron.” But the chapter describes what happens to the child in a way that does not match the Lord’s ascension, and the book itself shows who else is called to the same rule.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the child is “caught up unto God, and to his throne.” The verb is the one rendered “caught up” in 1 Thessalonians 4:17 and 2 Corinthians 12:2, a word of seizing and removal, and it is not the word used of the Lord’s ascension in the Gospels and Acts.
  • Stated: Revelation 2:26–27 gives the rod of iron to the overcomer “even as I received of my Father.” The rule of 12:5 is therefore not reserved to the Lord alone in this book.
  • Shown by the context: the 144,000 appear in 7:3–4 as sealed servants of Israel’s tribes and in 14:1–5 as “redeemed from among men, being the firstfruits.” A company that is sealed, kept and then seen with the Lamb on Sion is a company taken up out of the tribulation scenes.
  • Shown by the context: the child’s removal is followed at once by the woman’s flight for 1,260 days (12:6). The child does not return in the chapter, and the chapter’s action is carried by the woman and the dragon.
  • Inferred: that the male child is the corporate firstfruits of Israel. The text does not name the child, and the argument rests on the verb, the repetition of “firstfruits” and the rod of iron’s second use.

The Other Readings

The child is Christ. Most commentaries take the child as Christ, seeing in 12:5 the birth and ascension compressed. The reading is the common one and rests on Psalm 2 and on the compression of the Lord’s life in a single sign. It must explain why the chapter passes from birth to a rapture without a ministry, a death or a resurrection, and it must take the second use of the rod in 2:26–27 as a share in Christ’s rule that the child does not exhibit in the chapter. The child is the 144,000. This is the reading taken here, and it answers the question from the verb, the firstfruits and the shared rod.

What It Means for the Reader

The chapter is Israel’s, and the child it shows is Israel’s firstfruits, not the Lord. The Body is not in view in Revelation, and a reader should not look for its gathering in a scene of the dragon and the woman.
