PAULINE DOCTRINE SERIES · VOLUME 5

Liberty, Conscience and the Walk

What Governs the Believer Where Paul Gives No Command, and How Liberty Meets the Weaker Brother

Paul tells believers what to eat, what to keep and what to leave. Or does he? Where he gives no command, what governs the believer, and what limits liberty?

In Romans 14 one man eats and another eats herbs, one esteems a day and another every day alike. In 1 Corinthians 8 to 10 the case is meat offered to idols, and in Galatians 5 the call is to liberty. Colossians 2 says let no man judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of days. Paul also commands without exception: flee fornication, flee idolatry, put away the works of the flesh. How do the two fit together?

It argues that Paul gives commands where he commands and sets limits where he leaves a matter open, that the limits are separate and rest on different grounds, and that they are not one rule such as “do all to the glory of God.” In Chapter 17 it sets its reading beside modern writers who see it differently.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not list the practices believers have argued over and leave the choice to the reader. It says what the author believes Paul teaches when he leaves a matter open, gives the evidence from Paul’s own sentences, and answers the strongest objections in the objectors’ own words.

It is the fifth and last volume of the Pauline Doctrine Series, each volume of which is built around one narrower question on a large subject in Paul’s letters. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which report what a passage says. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout, checked word for word, and the writers named in Chapter 17 are quoted only from texts the author has read.

From the Five Questions to the Objections

The Five Questions

Learn to ask of every instruction whether it is a command or a limit, who is told, in what setting and on what ground, and to say when a passage does not answer one.

Romans 14 and 15

Follow the receiving of the one who differs, the stumblingblock and the bearing of the weak clause by clause, and note what Paul says of the weak and what he leaves unsaid.

1 Corinthians 8 to 10

Read the idol’s meat, Paul’s own laid-down right and the table of devils in order, and mark how the limit in chapter 8 differs from the limit in chapter 10.

Galatians 5 and Colossians 2

Read liberty called and guarded, the flesh and the Spirit, and “let no man judge you” beside Romans 14, and see what each leaves open.

Conscience and “All Things Are Lawful”

FROM THE VOLUME

What This Book Sets Out to Do

Paul does not give a rule for every case. Where he commands, he commands; where he leaves a matter open, he sets limits, and the limits are not one.

If a single rule covered every open matter, the limits in Romans 14, 1 Corinthians 8 and 10, Galatians 5 and Colossians 2 would be one limit said five times. The volume asks whether they are, and the reader will have seen why Paul never gives believers a list.
