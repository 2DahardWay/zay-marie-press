What Does “Baptism Doth Also Now Save Us” Mean in 1 Peter 3:21?

From Reader Questions, Volume 4: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 4 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

Peter writes to Israel’s scattered believers, and water baptism belongs to Israel’s covenant response in the kingdom offer and the early part of Acts (“Repent, and be baptized,” Acts 2:38). He calls baptism the answering figure to the ark: as eight souls were brought through the flood by water, so baptism now “saves” in a figure, and he guards the point at once. “Saves” here is deliverance from the judgment that hung over Israel in that prophetic setting, not eternal salvation; and it is “not the putting away of the filth of the flesh,” but “the answer of a good conscience toward God,” which in Israel’s setting is a covenant loyalty, with the saving “by the resurrection of Jesus Christ.” It is not the Body’s baptism, the one baptism of Ephesians 4:5 and the Spirit’s baptism into the one body (1 Corinthians 12:13). Paul says Christ sent him “not to baptize, but to preach the gospel” (1 Corinthians 1:17), which shows water baptism is no part of the saving message of the Body.

Where the Verse Stands

Peter writes to “the strangers scattered” (1:1) who are suffering for doing right, and in 3:18–22 he says that Christ suffered once, the just for the unjust, was put to death in the flesh and was quickened by the Spirit. He then goes back to the days of Noah, when “few, that is, eight souls were saved by water.” Verse 21 says baptism is the like figure, qualifies it in a parenthesis, and attaches the saving to the resurrection. The passage ends with Christ at God’s right hand and every power subject to him.

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
1:1“Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to the strangers scattered throughout Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia,”The readers are the scattered strangers.

3:21–22“The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us (not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God,) by the resurrection of Jesus Christ: Who is gone into heaven, and is on the right hand of God; angels and authorities and powers being made subject unto him.”The like figure; “not the putting away of the filth of the flesh”; “by the resurrection of Jesus Christ.”

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: Peter does not say that water saves. He says it is “not the putting away of the filth of the flesh,” and that the saving is “by the resurrection of Jesus Christ” (3:21).
  • Stated: the thing baptism corresponds to is “the answer of a good conscience toward God.” The baptized person answers God in covenant loyalty, and the rite expresses that answer.
  • Shown by the context: the figure is the ark. The eight were brought safely through the judgment by the ark and the water that bore it, and the likeness Peter draws is of deliverance through a judgment by what God provided. That is why he cannot mean that the water cleanses.
  • Shown by the context: the readers are Israel’s scattered believers (1:1), to whom the Lord’s commission in Matthew 28:19 and Peter’s own word at Pentecost, “Repent, and be baptized” (Acts 2:38), were addressed. Baptism in these passages sits within Israel’s program.
  • Inferred: that the “saving” is deliverance from judgment in Israel’s prophetic setting, not eternal salvation, and that the good conscience is covenant loyalty. The argument rests on Peter’s denial of the water’s cleansing, on his attaching the saving to the resurrection and on the Body’s being told that Christ sent Paul “not to baptize, but to preach the gospel” (1 Corinthians 1:17).

The Other Readings

Baptism as the means of grace. Some argue that in the New Testament baptism is the act in which God’s grace in Christ is received by faith, and take the verse as saying so. It must explain why Peter immediately denies any cleansing by the water, and why the Body’s apostle puts preaching above baptizing. An appeal for a clear conscience. Many commentaries take the answer or appeal to be a pledge or request to God for a clear conscience, with the saving effected by the resurrection and baptism as its outward sign. That is close to the reading taken here, with the added point that the baptism is Israel’s.

What It Means for the Reader

The verse does not make water the ground of anyone’s salvation, and Peter denies it in the verse itself. It speaks to Israel’s remnant and must stay there. For the Body, salvation is by believing the gospel Paul preached, and the Body’s baptism is the work of the Spirit (1 Corinthians 12:13), the subject of Volume 2, entry 4.
