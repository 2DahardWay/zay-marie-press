Why Were Sodom and Gomorrah Destroyed?

From Reader Questions, Volume 1: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 1 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

Scripture names more than one sin, and none is left out. Genesis gives a great cry and a grievous sin, and the city’s men at Lot’s door; Ezekiel gives pride, fulness of bread, idleness and neglect of the poor; Jude gives fornication and going after strange flesh. The judgment is the Lord’s, and the sins belong together.

Where the Question Comes From

The account is often reduced to one sin, and different readers reduce it to different ones. The reasonable way is to read the account in Genesis 18 and 19 and then let Ezekiel, Jude and Peter say what they say about it, without making any one of them the whole answer.

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
Gen 18:20–21“And the Lord said, Because the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and because their sin is very grievous; I will go down now, and see whether they have done altogether according to the cry of it, which is come unto me; and if not, I will know.”The cry is “great” and the sin “very grievous.” The Lord goes down to see.
Gen 19:4–5“But before they lay down, the men of the city, even the men of Sodom, compassed the house round, both old and young, all the people from every quarter: And they called unto Lot, and said unto him, Where are the men which came in to thee this night? bring them out unto us, that we may know them.”All the people, “both old and young.” The demand is for the guests.
Gen 19:24–25“Then the Lord rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire from the Lord out of heaven; And he overthrew those cities, and all the plain, and all the inhabitants of the cities, and that which grew upon the ground.”The judgment is from the Lord, on the cities and the plain.
Ezek 16:49–50“Behold, this was the iniquity of thy sister Sodom, pride, fulness of bread, and abundance of idleness was in her and in her daughters, neither did she strengthen the hand of the poor and needy. And they were haughty, and committed abomination before me: therefore I took them away as I saw good.”Pride, plenty, idleness, no help for the poor; then “abomination.”
Jude 7“Even as Sodom and Gomorrha, and the cities about them in like manner, giving themselves over to fornication, and going after strange flesh, are set forth for an example, suffering the vengeance of eternal fire.”Fornication and “strange flesh.” The cities are “an example.”
2 Pet 2:6–8“And turning the cities of Sodom and Gomorrha into ashes condemned them with an overthrow, making them an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly; And delivered just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked: (For that righteous man dwelling among them, in seeing and hearing, vexed his righteous soul from day to day with their unlawful deeds;)”An ensample. Lot vexed by “unlawful deeds,” day after day.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the Lord says the sin is “very grievous” before the angels reach the city (Genesis 18:20). The judgment was not triggered by one night.
  • Stated: in Genesis 19:4–5 the whole population, “both old and young, all the people from every quarter,” surrounds the house and demands the visitors “that we may know them.”
  • Stated: Ezekiel says “this was the iniquity of thy sister Sodom” and names pride, fulness of bread, idleness and no strength for the poor, and then adds that “they were haughty, and committed abomination.”
  • Stated: Jude says the cities gave themselves “over to fornication” and went “after strange flesh.” Peter says Lot was vexed “from day to day with their unlawful deeds.”
  • Inferred: that the account does not allow a single cause. Ezekiel’s “this was the iniquity” names pride and neglect of the poor as the root condition, and Genesis and Jude name the violent sexual sin as the act the root produced. The prophets and apostles do not set them against each other.

The Other Readings

Sexual sin alone. The traditional reading rests on Genesis 19:4–5 and Jude 7 and treats Ezekiel as secondary. It does not account for Ezekiel’s “this was the iniquity.” Inhospitality alone. Derrick Sherwin Bailey (Homosexuality and the Western Christian Tradition) argued that the sin was a violation of the duty to receive strangers. This reading has to explain Jude’s “fornication” and “strange flesh,” and the demand of the whole city for the guests. Pride and injustice alone. This takes Ezekiel 16:49 as the full answer. It leaves Jude 7 and the account of Genesis 19 unexplained.

ReadingWhat it must sayWhere it strains
All the named sinsPride and neglect of the poor, with the violent sexual sin.None is set as the only cause; the passages are read together.
Sexual sin aloneGenesis 19 and Jude 7 are the whole.Ezekiel 16:49 says “this was the iniquity.”
Inhospitality aloneThe sin was violating strangers.Jude names “fornication” and “strange flesh.”
Pride and injustice aloneEzekiel 16:49 is the whole.Genesis 19 and Jude 7 are left out.

What It Means for the Reader

The account does not give the reader one sin to condemn in others and excuse in himself. It gives a city whose pride and plenty left the poor unhelped and whose men also sought to do violence to strangers, and it says the Lord saw it and judged it. Lot is “just” in Peter’s words, and the account sets the judgment beside deliverance.
