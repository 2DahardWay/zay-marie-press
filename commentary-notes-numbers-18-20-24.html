Aaron’s Portion and the Levites’ Tithes

A close reading of Numbers 18:20–24

A close reading of Aaron’s portion and the Levites’ tithes within Israel’s sanctuary order in Numbers 18:20–24.

Open the free two-page PDFRead on this page

A priestly instruction within a larger chapter

Numbers 18 concerns duties and provisions connected with the sanctuary. The Lord speaks to Aaron about responsibility for priestly service and the gifts assigned to the priests (vv. 1–19). Verse 20 then turns to Aaron’s portion in relation to the land. Verses 21–24 speak of the Levites, whose service and support are distinguished from the priests’ specific duties.

The chapter’s arrangement matters. A statement about Aaron’s portion cannot simply be substituted for the detailed provision made to all the Levites, and neither statement cancels the tribal allotments made elsewhere in Israel’s history.

Aaron has the Lord as his portion Numbers 18:20

The Lord tells Aaron that he will have no inheritance in Israel’s land and says, “I am thy part and thine inheritance,” in the language of the passage. The sentence places the absence of a tribal landed share beside a positive statement of divine provision. It does not suggest that Aaron has no real place in Israel or no material support; the preceding verses have already described priestly portions from offerings.

This is not a general definition that every biblical inheritance must be immaterial. It is an explicit exception within a national distribution of land. The named recipient, the object withheld, and the provision affirmed are all part of the meaning.

The Levites receive the tithes Numbers 18:21–22

The Lord gives the Levites the tithes in Israel as an inheritance in return for their service at the tent of meeting. The tithes are thus a named provision linked to assigned work. Verse 22 also marks a boundary around sanctuary service: the other Israelites are not to come near the tent in a way that incurs guilt.

The text does not describe the tithe as a free-floating percentage rule addressed to every later community. It identifies a people, a service, and a provision within Israel’s sanctuary order. The boundary concerning access reinforces that the arrangement is specific.

Service and inheritance repeated Numbers 18:23–24

The Levites are to perform the work of the tent and bear the responsibility attached to that work. The Lord then repeats the reason they have no inheritance among the children of Israel: the tithes offered by Israel have been given to them as their inheritance. The repeated conclusion closes the small unit and keeps service, support, and the absence of a tribal share together.

A reader should neither call the Levites destitute because they lack a tribal allotment nor make their tithe provision identical to the Lord’s declaration to Aaron in verse 20. Both belong to the same sanctuary order, but the passage states them with different subjects and wording.

What this inheritance text can and cannot settle

Numbers 18:20–24 shows that “inheritance” can refer to a portion other than a tribal land allotment even within Israel. The passage names Aaron’s relation to the Lord and the Levites’ tithes, while distinguishing their sanctuary service from the allotments of the other tribes. It does not erase the land promises to Israel or define the Body of Christ’s inheritance.

Read Numbers 18:1–24 and note each change of addressee and each assigned provision. Then compare Numbers 26:52–56, where tribal land shares are allocated. Digital Study 56, Inheritance, follows the giver, heir, promised object, basis, pledge, and timing across Genesis, Paul, and Peter.
