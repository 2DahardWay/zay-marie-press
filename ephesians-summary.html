Ephesians

Paul unfolds God’s eternal purpose in Christ: the heavenly blessings of the Body of Christ, the Mystery revealed through Paul, and the worthy walk flowing from the believer’s new position.

TestamentNew Testament
GenrePauline Epistle
RecipientsSaints and faithful believers
Central EmphasisThe Body of Christ and heavenly calling

Purpose

Ephesians gives believers a comprehensive view of what God has accomplished and purposed in Christ. Paul celebrates spiritual blessings in heavenly places, salvation by grace, the creation of one new man, reconciliation in one Body, and Christ’s headship over the church.

The letter then calls believers to walk worthy of this calling through unity, renewed thinking, holiness, love, wisdom, ordered relationships, prayer, and steadfast resistance against spiritual wickedness.

Historical and Biblical Context

Paul writes as a prisoner for Christ Jesus on behalf of the Gentiles. Ephesus was a major center of Paul’s earlier ministry, though the absence of “in Ephesus” from some early manuscripts and the letter’s broad tone have led many to understand it as a circular letter for assemblies in Asia.

Ephesians explicitly identifies the Mystery as previously hidden and now revealed through Paul, with Spirit-mediated communication to holy apostles and prophets. Its revelation concerns the Body’s joint status in Christ and must not be collapsed into Israel’s covenants, earthly kingdom, or national promises.

Structure and Outline

  1. Spiritual blessings and God’s purpose in Christ — Ephesians 1
    Election, adoption, redemption, sealing, inheritance, prayer, and Christ’s exalted headship.
  2. Grace, reconciliation, and the one new man — Ephesians 2
    God makes the dead alive by grace and reconciles believing Jews and Gentiles in one Body.
  3. The Mystery and Paul’s prayer — Ephesians 3
    Paul explains his stewardship and prays for inward strength and comprehension of Christ’s love.
  4. The worthy walk and Body growth — Ephesians 4
    Unity, gifted service, maturity, renewed thinking, and transformed conduct.
  5. Love, light, wisdom, and ordered relationships — Ephesians 5–6:9
    Believers imitate God and express Spirit-filled wisdom in household relationships.
  6. The armor of God and closing appeal — Ephesians 6:10–24
    Believers stand in God’s strength through truth, righteousness, the gospel, faith, salvation, the word, and prayer.

Major Themes

In Christ

The believer’s identity, blessing, redemption, access, inheritance, and hope are grounded in union with the risen and exalted Christ.

Salvation by Grace

God makes spiritually dead sinners alive with Christ. Salvation is His gift, not a result of works, and leads to good works prepared for the believer’s walk.

The One New Man and One Body

Christ abolishes the enmity and creates believing Jews and Gentiles into one new man, reconciling both to God in one Body.

The Mystery Revealed through Paul

Paul received a stewardship concerning a truth hidden in God: Gentiles are joint heirs, joint members, and joint participants in Christ through the gospel.

Position Expressed in Practice

The doctrinal realities of chapters 1–3 govern the worthy walk of chapters 4–6. Grace produces unity, holiness, love, wisdom, service, and steadfastness.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Let Ephesians define the Mystery: it concerns truths previously hidden in God and revealed through Paul, not merely any difficult or symbolic teaching.
  • Distinguish the one new man from Israel’s prophetic restoration. Reconciliation in the Body does not transfer Israel’s national covenants or earthly promises.
  • “Apostles and prophets” in Ephesians 3:5 should be read with Paul’s stated reception and stewardship and the Spirit’s communication during the revelatory foundation.
  • Chapter 2’s “covenants of promise” describe the Gentiles’ former alienation; the passage’s solution is creation in one new man, not retroactive covenant membership.
  • The husband-wife analogy in chapter 5 illuminates Christ and the church without identifying the Body of Christ as Israel’s prophetic bride.
  • Keep doctrine and practice joined: the imperatives of chapters 4–6 flow from the accomplished grace of chapters 1–3.

Study Questions

  1. Which blessings does Paul identify as belonging to believers in Christ?
  2. How does Ephesians 2:1–10 relate grace, faith, works, and the believer’s walk?
  3. What did Christ create according to Ephesians 2:11–22?
  4. How does Paul define his stewardship of the Mystery?
  5. What preserves unity while producing maturity in chapter 4?
  6. How does the believer’s position shape relationships and spiritual resistance?
