How Do I Deal with Spiritual Dryness? Colossians 3:16

From Reader Questions, Volume 5: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 5 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

Dryness often comes from neglecting Scripture, prayer or fellowship, and renewal comes through Paul’s doctrine opened by the Spirit. The Body is not told to seek revival through signs or emotional experience: “we walk by faith, not by sight.” The word is the remedy: “Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly.” Prayer turns the heart toward God, and fellowship with mature believers strengthens faith, as Paul wrote to Rome, that “I may be comforted together with you by the mutual faith both of you and me.” Dry seasons end when the believer keeps at the truth and the discipline: “let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap.” God uses them to teach dependence, as Paul learned in affliction.

Where the Verses Stand

Colossians 3 sets the believer’s mind on things above and then says how the word is to live in the assembly: teaching and admonishing one another and singing to the Lord. Paul’s other words are on the Spirit’s teaching in 1 Corinthians 2, walking by faith in 2 Corinthians 5, endurance in Galatians 6 and mutual comfort in Romans 1.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: Paul’s command: “Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom; teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs” (Colossians 3:16). The word is to be taken in and shared.
  • Stated: Paul says faith is walked, not felt: “we walk by faith, not by sight” (2 Corinthians 5:7); feeling is not made the measure.
  • Shown by the context: Paul tells the Corinthians that the Spirit teaches by “comparing spiritual things with spiritual” (1 Corinthians 2:13), which is the renewing of the mind by Scripture. Hebrews 10:25, written to Hebrew believers, speaks of not forsaking “the assembling of ourselves together,” a point Paul makes in his own words in Romans 1:12 and Colossians 3:16.
  • Shown by the context: Paul’s own seasons of weakness drove him to God: “we had the sentence of death in ourselves, that we should not trust in ourselves, but in God which raiseth the dead” (2 Corinthians 1:9).
  • Shown by the context: Paul tells the Galatians that weariness is real: “let us not be weary in well doing” (Galatians 6:9). He assumes believers will tire and speaks to the tiredness, not to a defect of faith.
  • Inferred: that dryness commonly follows neglect and is cured by returning to the word, prayer and fellowship. Paul gives the remedies; he does not diagnose every dry season as neglect, and the argument does not claim he does.

The Other Readings

Disciplines renew the believer. Teachers of the spiritual disciplines call the believer to Scripture, prayer, fellowship and other practices. This is close to the reading taken here, with the added point that Paul’s doctrine is the content. The dark night. Gerald May (The Dark Night of the Soul) describes seasons when God seems absent as part of a deepening of faith. It must explain why Paul gives the Body practical remedies and not a path through absence.

What It Means for the Reader

The reader in a dry season is sent back to the word, to prayer and to other believers, and is told not to measure faith by feeling. Seasons pass when the truth is kept and the work continued. The passage does not say that a dry season is always the believer’s fault, or that it will end by a set day. It gives the word, prayer and the assembly as the means, and it leaves the timing to God.
