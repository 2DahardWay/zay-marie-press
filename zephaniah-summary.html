Zephaniah

The day of the LORD and restoration.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreMinor Prophets
Primary SettingJudah during the reign of Josiah
Key MovementFrom sweeping judgment to a purified remnant and rejoicing God
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

Zephaniah announces the near and ultimate dimensions of the day of the LORD against Judah and the nations. Idolatry, complacency, violence, and corrupt leadership bring judgment, yet the book calls the humble to seek the LORD and promises a purified remnant, restored speech, gathered exiles, and divine rejoicing over Zion.

Historical and Biblical Context

The prophecy is dated to Josiah’s reign and traces Zephaniah’s royal ancestry. Whether it precedes or accompanies Josiah’s reforms, it addresses entrenched sin in Jerusalem and Judah.

Evidence and inference: Prophetic texts join historical setting, covenant proclamation, symbolic imagery, and future promise. This summary distinguishes explicit textual claims from debated conclusions about date, structure, chronology, or fulfillment.

Structure and Outline

  1. Judah’s day of judgment — Zephaniah 1
    Sweeping judgment confronts idolatry, complacency, and the approaching day.
  2. Call and nations judged — Zephaniah 2
    The humble are summoned to seek the LORD; surrounding nations face judgment.
  3. Jerusalem indicted and restored — Zephaniah 3
    Corrupt leaders are exposed, then a purified people and restored Zion are promised.

Major Themes

The Day of the LORD

The day brings darkness and judgment against covenant rebellion and human pride.

Universal Judgment

Nations surrounding Judah are accountable to the same sovereign God.

The Humble Remnant

A lowly people takes refuge in the LORD’s name.

Restored Zion

God gathers, saves, removes shame, and rejoices over His people.

Key Passages

Zephaniah 1:4–18Judah’s idolatry and complacency meet the day of wrath.
Zephaniah 2:1–3The humble are called to seek righteousness and humility.
Zephaniah 2:4–15Philistia, Moab, Ammon, Cush, and Assyria face judgment.
Zephaniah 3:1–8Jerusalem’s officials, prophets, and priests are indicted.
Zephaniah 3:9–13Purified speech and a humble remnant are promised.
Zephaniah 3:14–20The King dwells in Zion, rejoices, gathers, and restores.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Observe movement from Judah to nations and back to Jerusalem.
  • The day of the LORD includes historical judgments while reaching toward a wider prophetic consummation.
  • The humble remnant is defined by refuge and truthful conduct.
  • Zion’s restoration retains its Israelite and kingdom setting.

Study Questions

  1. Which sins make Judah liable to the day?
  2. Why are the nations included?
  3. What characterizes the remnant?
  4. How does the closing portrait reverse shame and exile?
