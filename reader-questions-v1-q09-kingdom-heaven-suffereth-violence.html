What Does “The Kingdom of Heaven Suffereth Violence” Mean?

From Reader Questions, Volume 1: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 1 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

The kingdom has been assaulted since the days of John. John is in prison when the saying is spoken, and “the violent” who “take it by force” are its opponents. The saying is about the kingdom’s resistance, not about eager seekers pressing in.

Where the Verse Stands

Matthew 11 opens with John “in the prison,” sending two disciples to ask, “Art thou he that should come, or do we look for another?” The Lord answers by pointing to his works, and then speaks of John to the multitudes. John had been put in prison for telling Herod “It is not lawful for thee to have her” (14:3–4). The saying in verse 12 comes in the Lord’s tribute to John, and it measures the kingdom’s history “from the days of John the Baptist until now.”

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
11:2–3“Now when John had heard in the prison the works of Christ, he sent two of his disciples, And said unto him, Art thou he that should come, or do we look for another?”John is in prison.

And from the days of John the Baptist until now the kingdom of heaven suffereth violence, and the violent take it by force.”The kingdom “suffereth violence” “from the days of John.”
11:13–14“For all the prophets and the law prophesied until John. And if ye will receive it, this is Elias, which was for to come.”John is the herald; Israel is offered the choice.
14:3–5“For Herod had laid hold on John, and bound him, and put him in prison for Herodias’ sake, his brother Philip’s wife. For John said unto him, It is not lawful for thee to have her. And when he would have put him to death, he feared the multitude, because they counted him as a prophet.”The herald is bound and nearly killed for his rebuke.
Luke 16:16“The law and the prophets were until John: since that time the kingdom of God is preached, and every man presseth into it.”A different setting and wording.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the kingdom “suffereth violence, and the violent take it by force,” from “the days of John the Baptist until now.”
  • Stated: John is in prison as the saying is spoken (11:2), and Matthew tells why: Herod had “bound him, and put him in prison” for John’s rebuke (14:3–4).
  • Shown by the context: the Lord has just said “ye will receive it, this is Elias” (11:14), an offer to Israel that depends on receiving. Verse 12 follows by saying the herald is already bound and the kingdom is under assault.
  • Shown by the context: Luke 16:16 has its own wording, “every man presseth into it,” in its own setting. Matthew’s sentence is read by Matthew’s surroundings, and the two are not made one saying.
  • Inferred: that the violent are those who oppose, as Herod did, and that “take it by force” is the seizing and holding back of the kingdom by force. The text does not name the violent in the verse.

The Other Readings

Eager seekers pressing in. This reads the violent as people who press into the kingdom with zeal, and cites Luke 16:16. This reading has to explain why the verse follows John’s imprisonment and Herod’s act. The kingdom advances with power. This takes the verb in a sense of the kingdom moving forcefully, and has to explain “the violent take it by force.” The kingdom is assaulted. This is the reading taken here.

What It Means for the Reader

The saying is about the kingdom’s history in Israel’s ministry, not about how a believer should seek. The one who announced it was imprisoned, and the kingdom was met with force. The reader sees what the Lord saw: the offer was real and the opposition was real.
