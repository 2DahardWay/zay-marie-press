Sarah Isaac and Ishmael in the Covenant Speech

A close reading of Genesis 17:15–21

A close reading of Sarah’s promised son, Abraham’s request for Ishmael, and God’s answer about each son in Genesis 17:15–21.

Open the free two-page PDFRead on this page

The covenant speech in context

Earlier in Genesis 17 God identifies himself to Abram, announces a covenant, changes Abram’s name to Abraham, and gives circumcision as a covenant sign (vv. 1–14). The speech then turns to Sarai. The change of her name to Sarah is followed immediately by a promise that she will bear a son. Her place in the account is not an incidental explanation added after the covenant terms; God names her in the announcement of its future line.

The passage should be read alongside the existing promise to Abraham rather than as the first time any offspring was mentioned. Ishmael has already been born (Gen. 16). The question before Abraham in this scene concerns the promised son and the future God now specifies.

Sarah named and blessed Genesis 17:15–16

God tells Abraham not to call his wife Sarai but Sarah. He promises to bless her, give Abraham a son by her, and make her a mother of nations, with kings of peoples coming from her. The repeated attention to Sarah rules out an account in which the line is defined only by Abraham’s fatherhood.

These words announce what God will do. They do not explain every later nation or king, nor do they authorize a reader to merge Sarah’s named role with every benefit God gives to others. The identity of the promised mother matters to this particular covenant announcement.

Abraham asks about Ishmael Genesis 17:17–18

Abraham falls on his face and laughs as he considers the ages of himself and Sarah. He then asks that Ishmael might live before God. The text gives the reader his response and request, but it does not require us to reduce his words to either a complete rejection of God’s promise or an unqualified model of faith. God’s reply is the passage’s decisive interpretation of what will happen next.

The question about Ishmael is understandable within the story: he is Abraham’s living son. God does not ignore him or treat Abraham’s concern as if Ishmael had no future. Yet the answer distinguishes what is promised to Ishmael from what is established with the son Sarah will bear.

Two answers with distinct content Genesis 17:19–21

God first says that Sarah will bear a son, names him Isaac, and says he will establish his covenant with him and his offspring. He then answers Abraham’s request concerning Ishmael: God has heard, will bless him, make him fruitful, multiply him, and make him a great nation. The promise concerning Ishmael is substantial and stated positively.

The final sentence returns to the distinction: God will establish his covenant with Isaac, whom Sarah will bear at the appointed time in the next year. The text thus presents blessing for Ishmael and the specified covenant line through Isaac. It does not permit the reader to deny Ishmael’s blessing in order to preserve Isaac’s role, or to erase Isaac’s named role because Ishmael also receives blessing.

What this close reading permits

Genesis 17:15–21 identifies Sarah as the mother of the promised son, gives Isaac his name before birth, answers Abraham’s concern for Ishmael, and states the covenant line explicitly. It does not, on its own, explain every later use of “Abraham’s seed” or assign the national and territorial terms to a different audience.

Read Genesis 16:15–17:21 in sequence and list each promise by its named recipient. Then compare how Paul uses Abrahamic language in Romans 4 and Galatians 3. Digital Study 40, Who Is Abraham’s Seed?, traces those different uses and the precise benefits believers receive through Christ.
