Is the “Everlasting Father” of Isaiah 9:6 God the Father?

From Reader Questions, Volume 1: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 1 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

The title belongs to the child born, who sits on David’s throne. It names him as a father to his people who does not die, a role Isaiah himself gives to a faithful steward, “a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem.” It does not say the Son is the Father.

Where the Verse Stands

Isaiah 9:1–7 answers the gloom of the end of chapter 8. The people “that walked in darkness have seen a great light,” and the reason follows: “unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given.” The child bears a list of names and a government that has no end “upon the throne of David.” The passage sits inside Isaiah 7 to 12, a section about the house of David and the promise to it.

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
9:2“The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light: they that dwell in the land of the shadow of death, upon them hath the light shined.”Light to those in darkness.
9:6“For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace.”A child “born,” a son “given.” Five names.
9:7“Of the increase of his government and peace there shall be no end, upon the throne of David, and upon his kingdom, to order it, and to establish it with judgment and with justice from henceforth even for ever. The zeal of the Lord of hosts will perform this.”The throne of David; no end.

And the key of the house of David will I lay upon his shoulder; so he shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open.”Government, the key of David’s house “upon his shoulder,” “a father to the inhabitants.”
Job 29:16“I was a father to the poor: and the cause which I knew not I searched out.”“A father” as a role of care.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the child is “born” and a son is “given,” and the government is “upon his shoulder.” The five names are given to him, and the government is “upon the throne of David.”
  • Stated: Isaiah 22:21 says Eliakim “shall be a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and to the house of Judah,” and 22:22 lays “the key of the house of David” on his shoulder. Government on the shoulder and a father to the people occur together there too.
  • Shown by the context: the names are a list of titles of the king’s office and character. The list includes “The mighty God,” which says the child is more than a man, and “The everlasting Father,” which names the care he gives his people for ever, because his government “shall be no end.”
  • Shown by the context: Job’s “I was a father to the poor” shows “father” as a word for the one who protects and provides. The word describes what the king is to the people.
  • Inferred: that “everlasting Father” is the title of the Davidic King as the unending father of his people. The text does not say in the verse that the Son is the Father in person, and it does not need to.

The Other Readings

The Son is the Father. Some read the title as identifying the Son with the Father, a reading associated with Oneness teaching. It has to explain “a son is given” in the same verse. Father of eternity. Many commentaries read the child as the Messiah and the title as the one whose fatherly care lasts for ever. That is the reading taken here, and it treats the verse as describing the king’s relation to his people, not his identity with the Father. A title for a human king. This takes the names as honorific for a royal child in the line of David and has to explain “The mighty God.”

What It Means for the Reader

The verse promises a King on David’s throne whose government never ends and whose care for his people never ends. The title is not a statement about who the Son is in himself but about what the King is to those he rules, and Isaiah’s own book shows a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem as the shape of such rule.
