The Upper Room

The King’s Last Words to the Twelve Before the Cross

To whom are the promises of John 13–17 given, and in what setting?

On the night before the Cross, the Lord spoke in an upper room to the men who had followed him. Judas had gone out, and eleven remained. The discourse of John 13–17 holds some of the most loved promises in the Gospels: a place prepared, another Comforter, the vine, peace and the prayer for those who should believe. This commentary reads them in the room where they were spoken.

It argues that the promises are given to the eleven by the Lord before the Cross, that they are not the Body’s charter, and that the Body’s calling, revealed through Paul, is set beside them and not read into them. In Chapter 12 it sets its reading beside modern writers who see it differently.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not survey how the Upper Room Discourse has been used and leave the choice to the reader. It states what the author believes the text says, sets out the evidence from the Gospel, hears the writers who disagree in their own words, and answers them from Scripture.

It is the fifth volume of the Gospel Commentary Series, each volume of which is built around one narrower question on the four Gospels. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which report what a passage says. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout, and the writers named in Chapter 12 are quoted only from words the author has read in full. Its own question is narrow: to whom are the promises of John 13–17 given, and in what setting?

WHAT THE VOLUME COVERS

From the Question to the Objections

The Setting and the Audience

Read John 13 in order: the supper, the going out of Judas and the company that remains, and mark who is in the room.

The Towel and the Commandment

Follow the washing of the feet and the new commandment, and trace what the Lord says to the eleven and what he says about the world.

“In My Father’s House”

Read the place prepared and the coming again beside the Gospels’ own line of his coming, and mark what the verses say and leave unsaid.

Another Comforter

Set the promises of the Comforter beside John 7:39 and 20:22 and Acts 1:4–8, and note to whom each is spoken.

The Vine and the Hatred of the World

Read the vine beside Psalm 80, Isaiah 5 and Jeremiah 2, then the warnings of hatred and the putting out of the synagogues.

The Prayer and the Objections

Read John 17 in its own words, then hear named modern writers who take the discourse as the church’s charter and weigh each reply.

FROM THE VOLUME

What This Book Sets Out to Do

The Lord speaks in the upper room to the eleven, and he says so: “ye are my friends,” “ye are clean,” “ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you.” The volume asks what happens to the promises if the room is kept in view.

The Body’s calling is set beside the discourse and not read into it.
