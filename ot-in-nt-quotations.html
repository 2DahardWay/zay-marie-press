How each use is classified (open for the definitions)

Quotation (direct): the New Testament gives Old Testament words from one source. Quotation (composite): words from more than one source joined or placed under one introduction. Allusion (lexical): no quotation, but the wording clearly matches. Allusion (narrative): the verse points to an Old Testament account or person and says so. Allusion (typological): an Old Testament type set beside a New Testament antitype in a pattern the New Testament text itself names; each shows the type, the antitype and the justification. Allusion (thematic): the New Testament text itself signals an Old Testament theme without quoting it. Echo (verbal resonance): distinctive Old Testament vocabulary too rare to be accidental. Parallel (non-dependent similarity): the similarity is clear, but the New Testament gives no signal of dependence. Interpretive Device (Pauline): Galatians 4:21–31 only, where Paul himself says “an allegory”; it is not counted as an allusion or as typology. No other allegorical reading is in the list.

Tags. “Marked by the text” means the verse itself says it is quoting or pointing back (for example “it is written”). “Shown by the context” means no such words appear, but the link is clear from the passage. “Not signalled by the text” is used only for parallels. The tag says how the link is shown, not how to read the verse.

Shortcuts. Press / to jump to the search box and Esc to clear it. “Table” shows every result in one compact list; select a row to open its card. “Copy list” and “Download CSV” take the results now shown.
