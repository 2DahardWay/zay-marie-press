Can a Believer Lose Salvation According to Hebrews 6:4–6?

From Reader Questions, Volume 2: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 2 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

No, because the passage is not a warning to the Body. Hebrews is written to Israel, and the warning is Israel’s danger of rejecting the Messiah after receiving the kingdom’s signs (“the powers of the world to come”), which is to crucify the Son afresh. The Body’s security rests on promises the passage never touches: “neither death, nor life … shall be able to separate us.”

Where the Verses Stand

Hebrews 5:11 to 6:12 is one argument. The writer rebukes the readers for being “dull of hearing” and in need of milk, urges them to leave “the principles of the doctrine of Christ,” and in the middle of that urging gives the sentence about those who fall away. It ends with a turn: “But, beloved, we are persuaded better things of you.”

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
2:3–4“How shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation; which at the first began to be spoken by the Lord, and was confirmed unto us by them that heard him; God also bearing them witness, both with signs and wonders, and with divers miracles, and gifts of the Holy Ghost, according to his own will?”The message was confirmed by “signs and wonders, and with divers miracles, and gifts of the Holy Ghost.”
3:1“Wherefore, holy brethren, partakers of the heavenly calling, consider the Apostle and High Priest of our profession, Christ Jesus;”“Holy brethren, partakers of the heavenly calling.”

For when for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye have need that one teach you again which be the first principles of the oracles of God; and are become such as have need of milk, and not of strong meat.”They should be teachers and need milk.
6:1–2“Therefore leaving the principles of the doctrine of Christ, let us go on unto perfection; not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works, and of faith toward God, Of the doctrine of baptisms, and of laying on of hands, and of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.”The “principles”: repentance, baptisms, laying on of hands, resurrection, judgment.
6:4–6“For it is impossible for those who were once enlightened, and have tasted of the heavenly gift, and were made partakers of the Holy Ghost, And have tasted the good word of God, and the powers of the world to come, If they shall fall away, to renew them again unto repentance; seeing they crucify to themselves the Son of God afresh, and put him to an open shame.”“Impossible … to renew them again unto repentance” for those who “fall away.”
6:7–9“For the earth which drinketh in the rain that cometh oft upon it, and bringeth forth herbs meet for them by whom it is dressed, receiveth blessing from God: But that which beareth thorns and briers is rejected, and is nigh unto cursing; whose end is to be burned. But, beloved, we are persuaded better things of you, and things that accompany salvation, though we thus speak.”Earth that bears thorns is “nigh unto cursing.” Then: “better things of you.”
Rom 8:38–39“For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.”Nothing “shall be able to separate us from the love of God.”

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the sentence is conditional and its subject is described by what they received: enlightened, tasted of the heavenly gift, partakers of the Holy Ghost, tasted the good word and the powers of the world to come. The consequence is stated for “if they shall fall away.”
  • Stated: the writer closes the argument with “better things … and things that accompany salvation, though we thus speak” (6:9). The warning is not spoken as a verdict on the readers.
  • Shown by the context: the “principles” they are urged to leave are the elementary teachings of Israel’s practice (6:1–2): baptisms, the laying on of hands, resurrection and judgment. The readers are being pressed to go on, and the chapter’s argument is that to turn back after Christ is to join those who “crucify to themselves the Son of God afresh.”
  • Shown by the context: the letter’s covenant is the one quoted in chapter 8 from Jeremiah 31, which belongs to the house of Israel and the house of Judah. The Body is not a party to that covenant.
  • Shown by the context: the experiences listed in 6:4–5 match the kingdom signs recorded in 2:3–4: “signs and wonders, and with divers miracles, and gifts of the Holy Ghost.” “The powers of the world to come” are the powers of the coming kingdom, given to Israel as a foretaste.
  • Inferred: that “fall away” means turning from the Messiah after those signs, whether by a return to the old system or by rejecting him, and not a loss of the Body’s salvation. The word is used once in the New Testament, and the argument rests on the letter’s own setting.
  • Inferred: that the Body’s question is answered elsewhere. Paul tells the Ephesians they were “sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession” (Ephesians 1:13–14).

The Other Readings

True believers can fall and be lost. I. Howard Marshall (Kept by the Power of God) argues that the passage describes real believers who may forfeit salvation by persistent apostasy. It has the strongest literal fit with “once enlightened” and “partakers of the Holy Ghost,” and it must set Hebrews 6 against Romans 8:38–39 and Ephesians 1:13–14. The people were never saved. Wayne Grudem (Systematic Theology) takes the language as describing those who share the blessings of the community without a true saving faith. It protects the security of the believer but strains the phrase “partakers of the Holy Ghost.” Believers who lose reward and maturity. Zane Hodges (“Hebrews,” in The Bible Knowledge Commentary) reads the passage as a warning to real believers of loss of reward. A warning to Israel against rejecting the Messiah after the kingdom signs. This is the reading taken here: a real warning in a real setting, tied to Israel’s prophetic privileges, not one that reaches the Body’s standing.

What It Means for the Reader

The Body is not asked to hold its salvation by watchfulness over a covenant it was never in. The reader is asked to hear the warning for what it is, a sober word to Israel, and to rest on promises given to the Body.
