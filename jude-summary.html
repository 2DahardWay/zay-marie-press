Jude

Jude calls believers to contend for the once-delivered faith against infiltrators who corrupt grace, reject authority, and face the certainty of divine judgment.

TestamentNew Testament
GenreGeneral Epistle
AuthorJude, servant of Jesus Christ and brother of James
Central EmphasisContending for the faith amid apostasy
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

Jude had intended to write about the shared salvation but changes course because ungodly people have slipped into the community. He urges the called to contend earnestly for the faith delivered once for all to the saints.

Through scriptural judgments, traditional examples, vivid images, and apostolic warnings, Jude exposes the infiltrators. He then directs believers to remember, build themselves up, pray in the Holy Spirit, remain in God’s love, wait for Christ’s mercy, rescue the wavering carefully, and trust God’s preserving power.

Historical and Biblical Context

Jude identifies himself through James, most naturally the prominent brother of the Lord, making Jude another of Jesus’ brothers. The recipients are not named by location but are described as called, loved, and kept.

Jude shares extensive material with 2 Peter 2, though the precise literary relationship is debated. He also refers to Jewish traditional material associated with 1 Enoch and the dispute over Moses’ body. Inspired use of a known tradition does not automatically endorse every part of its source.

Structure and Outline

  1. Greeting and urgent appeal — Jude 1–4
    The kept community must contend for the faith against grace-corrupting infiltrators.
  2. Examples and certainty of judgment — Jude 5–16
    Israel, angels, Sodom, Cain, Balaam, Korah, and Enoch’s prophecy expose rebellion and doom.
  3. Remember and remain faithful — Jude 17–23
    Apostolic warnings prepare believers to build, pray, remain, wait, show mercy, and rescue discerningly.
  4. Doxology to the preserving God — Jude 24–25
    God is able to keep His people from stumbling and present them blameless with joy.

Major Themes

The Once-Delivered Faith

The apostolic faith is a received deposit, not an endlessly revised message. It must be defended against corrupt redefinition.

Grace Distorted into License

The intruders use grace to excuse sensuality and deny the rightful rule of Jesus Christ. Their conduct reveals their doctrine’s corruption.

Rebellion and Certain Judgment

Israel’s unbelief, angelic rebellion, Sodom, Cain, Balaam, and Korah demonstrate that privilege does not prevent judgment.

Apostolic Remembrance and Perseverance

The faithful are not surprised by mockers. They answer by building on the faith, praying, remaining in love, and awaiting mercy.

Merciful Discernment

Different conditions require careful responses: mercy toward doubters, urgent rescue for some, and fearfully guarded compassion toward others.

Key Passages

Jude 1–4The called and kept community, shared salvation, and the command to contend.
Jude 5–7Israel, rebellious angels, and Sodom as warnings.
Jude 8–13Presumption, Michael’s restraint, Cain, Balaam, Korah, and vivid portraits.
Jude 14–16Enoch’s prophecy and the intruders’ grumbling self-interest.
Jude 17–19Remembering apostolic warnings about last-time mockers.
Jude 20–23Building, praying, keeping, waiting, mercy, and rescue.
Jude 24–25God’s keeping power, joyful presentation, and eternal majesty.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Contending for the faith means protecting apostolic truth and holy conduct, not cultivating a quarrelsome disposition.
  • Identify the infiltrators from Jude’s full portrait: doctrinal denial, sensuality, rejected authority, greed, divisiveness, boasting, and absence of the Spirit.
  • Jude’s references to Jewish traditions should be interpreted from the inspired argument he makes, without assuming canonical authority for an entire outside work.
  • Textual variation affects verse 5, where strong witnesses read “Jesus” as the one who saved a people from Egypt and later judged unbelief; translations may differ.
  • The calls to keep themselves in God’s love and God’s ability to keep them belong together as responsible perseverance grounded in divine preservation.
  • Jude’s last-time apostasy setting should be preserved before its patterns are applied more generally.

Study Questions

  1. Why does Jude change the subject he intended to address?
  2. What is “the faith” for which believers must contend?
  3. How do Jude’s judgment examples expose the infiltrators?
  4. Which behaviors and motives characterize the false teachers?
  5. What practices keep the faithful stable amid apostasy?
  6. How do mercy, rescue, caution, and God’s preserving power work together?
