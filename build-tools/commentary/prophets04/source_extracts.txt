Malachi clearly teaches that Elijah will make a visit during the tribulation to the nation of Israel (Mal. 4:5).
So Elijah is still to come.
John the Baptist fulfilled a different prediction, that of Malachi 3:1 and Isaiah 40:3-5.
Thus, because of Israel’s rejection, John the Baptist was John the Baptist (My messenger) and not Elijah.
John then was Elijah as an earnest, but we still await the other Elijahs
and especially that final Elijah the prophet before the great and terrible day of our Lord.
From our examination of Malachi's prophecy it is clear that we should adopt the third alternative.
Since this present is coupled with a future tense, the present must be interpreted as a futuristic present
Many scholars have argued that the fulfillment of the Elijah prophecy (of Mal. 4:5), is to be confined to John the Baptist.
But this does not force us to conclude that there is no future work for Elijah to do.
At the second coming of Messiah as Conqueror and King, Elijah himself returns to prepare the way.
Elijah in our text is John the Baptist, not the literal Tishbite revived.
Malachi 4:5 speaks of Elijah's office rather than His person.
Similarly, Malachi is not prophesying that Elijah will return and labour again.
Rather, one like Elijah will do a work like Elijah.
However, Malachi did not predict a return of the very same Elijah in the flesh.
The Jews were expecting a literal return of Elijah, and in that respect, John the Baptist certainly was not Elijah.
