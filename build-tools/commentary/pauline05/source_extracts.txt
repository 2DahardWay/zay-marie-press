# CONSTABLE: Expository Notes on Romans 14, https://www.studylight.org/commentaries/eng/dcc/romans-14.html
In view of what Paul wrote about the weak they appear to have been mainly Jewish Christians who refrained from certain foods.
observed certain days because they remained loyal to the Mosaic Law.
God has not forbidden Christians from eating any food (1 Timothy 4:3-4).
The weak in faith have an overly sensitive conscience about doing things that are permissible for a Christian.
# CONSTABLE: Expository Notes on 1 Corinthians 8, https://www.studylight.org/commentaries/eng/dcc/1-corinthians-8.html
Paul wrote that the real aim of the faith should not be knowledge but love.
He would make love for his brethren the governor over his knowledge of what was permissible.
Foods do not make us more or less pleasing to God.
# CONSTABLE: Expository Notes on 1 Corinthians 10, https://www.studylight.org/commentaries/eng/dcc/1-corinthians-10.html
The power behind pagan religion is demonic (cf. Deuteronomy 32:17; Psalms 106:37).
It was wise not to ask if someone had offered the meat to an idol.
What glorifies God is what puts His preferences, plans, and program first (cf. Colossians 3:17).
# CONSTABLE: Expository Notes on Colossians 2, https://www.studylight.org/commentaries/eng/dcc/colossians-2.html
The dietary and festival observances were like shadows of Christ.
They wanted to limit it by prohibiting certain perfectly legitimate activities.
# CONSTABLE: Expository Notes on Galatians 5, https://www.studylight.org/commentaries/eng/dcc/galatians-5.html
He also warned them against using their liberty as a license to sin to prevent them from overreacting.
Under grace we are free to fulfill the Law by loving one another.
Paul urged his readers to live unbound to the Law of Moses (Galatians 5:1-12).
# PIPER: How to Welcome a Weak Brother (Romans 14:1–6), Desiring God, https://www.desiringgod.org/messages/how-to-welcome-a-weak-brother
Notice first that those who are weak in faith don’t eat meat and don’t drink wine.
Typically the strong will be tempted to ‘despise’ the weak—look down on them with a patronizing air.
# PIPER: Do Not Destroy the Work of God (Romans 14:14–23), Desiring God, November 6, 2005, https://www.desiringgod.org/messages/do-not-destroy-the-work-of-god
This is what it means to put a stumbling block in someone’s way: enticing them to do what their conscience condemns.
What is at stake is the destruction of the weaker brother, and maybe ourselves in the process.
# LEE: Paul and the Sabbath, Jon English Lee, Founders Ministries, October 17, 2013, https://founders.org/2013/10/17/paul-and-the-sabbath/
Paul is dealing with the ceremonial and cultic attachments to certain days; he is not abrogating the Sabbath command.
Similar to the Romans passage discussed above, Paul is not removing the command for one Sabbath day of rest per week.
He is addressing the ceremonial and cultic patterns that the Colossians were using to ‘pass judgment’ upon believers.
# BAIDOO: The “Weak” and the “Strong” in Romans 14:1–15:13, Journal of AIIAS African Theological Association 9.1, 2020–2021, https://journals.aiias.edu/aata/article/download/1028/869
This study argues that Romans 14–15 is a targeted response to the postClaudian reintegration crisis in Rome.
This day may point refer to other Jewish feast days.
