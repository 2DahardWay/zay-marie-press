Daniel

God rules over Gentile kingdoms, preserves faithful servants in exile, reveals successive empires, and promises the triumph of His everlasting kingdom.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreMajor Prophets
Primary SettingBabylon and Persia during Judah’s exile
Key MovementFrom faithful witness in Gentile courts to visions of kingdoms, conflict, and final deliverance

Purpose

Daniel encourages faithfulness under Gentile dominion by demonstrating that the Most High rules human kingdoms. Court narratives display divine preservation and humbling of rulers; visions disclose successive empires, persecution, judgment, the Son of Man’s dominion, Israel’s future distress, and ultimate resurrection.

Historical and Biblical Context

Daniel and other Judean youths are taken to Babylon and trained for imperial service. The book moves between Hebrew and Aramaic and between third-person court narratives and first-person visions. Its dated revelations extend from Babylonian rule into the Persian period.

Evidence and inference: Prophetic texts join historical setting, inspired proclamation, symbolic vision, and future promise. This summary distinguishes explicit textual claims from debated conclusions about authorship, date, chronology, structure, or fulfillment.

Structure and Outline

  1. Faithfulness in Babylon — Daniel 1
    Daniel and his companions remain undefiled while serving the empire.
  2. Kingdom dreams and court deliverances — Daniel 2–6
    God reveals mysteries, preserves faithful witnesses, humbles kings, and judges arrogant rule.
  3. Four beasts and the Son of Man — Daniel 7
    Gentile kingdoms are judged and everlasting dominion is given to one like a son of man.
  4. Ram, goat, and oppressive ruler — Daniel 8
    A vision portrays empires and a fierce desecrating power.
  5. Prayer and seventy sevens — Daniel 9
    Daniel confesses Israel’s sin, appeals to covenant mercy, and receives a timed prophecy concerning his people and holy city.
  6. Final revelation — Daniel 10–12
    Spiritual conflict, imperial struggle, unparalleled distress, deliverance, and resurrection are revealed.

Major Themes

Divine Sovereignty over Kingdoms

The Most High appoints, humbles, removes, and judges rulers.

Faithfulness in Exile

Daniel and his companions serve responsibly without yielding worship or covenant identity.

Revealed Mystery and Human Limitation

God alone discloses the meaning and course of kingdoms, while Daniel himself does not understand every detail.

The Everlasting Kingdom

Beastly empires give way to the dominion of the Son of Man and the saints.

Israel, Distress, and Resurrection

The final visions concern Daniel’s people, Jerusalem, desecration, deliverance, and resurrection.

Key Passages

Daniel 1:1–21The exiles maintain fidelity while gaining wisdom in Babylon.
Daniel 2:24–49God reveals the statue dream and the kingdom that will never be destroyed.
Daniel 3:13–30The three Hebrews are preserved in the furnace.
Daniel 4:28–37Nebuchadnezzar is humbled and acknowledges heaven’s rule.
Daniel 6:10–28Daniel continues prayer and is delivered from the lions.
Daniel 7:9–28The Ancient of Days judges the beasts and grants everlasting dominion.
Daniel 9:1–27Daniel’s covenant prayer is answered with the seventy-sevens prophecy.
Daniel 12:1–13Distress, deliverance, resurrection, and sealed revelation conclude the book.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Identify which parts of each vision receive inspired interpretation before extending the symbols further.
  • Daniel 9 explicitly concerns Daniel’s people and holy city; do not detach the seventy sevens from that stated frame.
  • Historical correlations can clarify visions, but disputed identifications should be distinguished from the text’s explicit claims.
  • The book’s chronology places faithful service within Gentile rule without equating service with worship.
  • Daniel’s kingdom expectations belong to Israel’s Prophecy Program and must remain distinct from the Mystery revealed through Paul.

Study Questions

  1. How do chapters 1–6 define faithful life under Gentile power?
  2. What repeated confession about God’s sovereignty unites the court narratives?
  3. How do the four beasts develop the kingdom sequence of chapter 2?
  4. What controls does Daniel 9 give for interpreting the seventy sevens?
  5. How do resurrection and everlasting dominion answer the book’s persecution scenes?
