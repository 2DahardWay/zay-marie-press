Micah

Judgment and messianic hope.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreMinor Prophets
Primary SettingJudah and Israel during the Assyrian era
Key MovementFrom covenant indictment to Bethlehem’s ruler and restored Zion
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

Micah announces judgment upon Samaria and Jerusalem for idolatry, corrupt leadership, exploitation, and false prophecy. Yet the book promises a remnant, a ruler from Bethlehem, Zion’s future exaltation, forgiveness, and fulfillment of promises sworn to the fathers.

Historical and Biblical Context

Micah of Moresheth ministers during Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah. Rural injustice, royal policy, and approaching Assyrian aggression shape his message to both kingdoms.

Evidence and inference: Prophetic texts join historical setting, covenant proclamation, symbolic imagery, and future promise. This summary distinguishes explicit textual claims from debated conclusions about date, structure, chronology, or fulfillment.

Structure and Outline

  1. Judgment and remnant hope — Micah 1–2
    Samaria and Judah are indicted; land seizure is condemned; a gathered remnant is promised.
  2. Corrupt rulers and future Zion — Micah 3–5
    Leaders destroy justice, Jerusalem faces ruin, and the Bethlehem ruler brings peace.
  3. Covenant lawsuit and final hope — Micah 6–7
    The LORD states His case, defines faithful response, and promises compassionate forgiveness.

Major Themes

Justice and Leadership

Rulers, priests, and prophets are accountable for exploiting the people.

Zion’s Future

Present judgment gives way to instruction, peace, and divine reign.

The Bethlehem Ruler

A Davidic ruler shepherds in the LORD’s strength.

Covenant Mercy

God pardons and performs truth and mercy promised to Abraham and Jacob.

Key Passages

Micah 1:2–9The LORD descends in judgment upon Samaria and Judah.
Micah 2:1–13Land theft is condemned and a remnant gathered.
Micah 3:9–12Corrupt leaders turn Jerusalem toward ruin.
Micah 4:1–8Zion becomes the center of instruction and peace.
Micah 5:2–5The ruler from Bethlehem shepherds in strength.
Micah 6:6–8Covenant response requires justice, loyal love, and humble walking.
Micah 7:18–20God’s pardon fulfills patriarchal promises.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Micah 6:8 belongs within a covenant lawsuit and should not be detached into vague moralism.
  • Distinguish present Jerusalem under judgment from promised future Zion.
  • The Bethlehem prophecy retains its Davidic and kingdom features.
  • Restoration is anchored in promises to Israel’s fathers.

Study Questions

  1. What leadership sins receive judgment?
  2. How does land injustice violate covenant life?
  3. What future does Micah describe for Zion?
  4. How does the final doxology connect mercy to Abraham and Jacob?
