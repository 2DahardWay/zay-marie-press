ACTS COMMENTARY SERIES · VOLUME 4

Cornelius and the Council

Acts 10–15

What was asked at Jerusalem, and who answered which part?

In Acts 11 Peter is asked why he went in to men uncircumcised and ate with them. In Acts 15 certain men say that Gentile believers must be circumcised and keep the law of Moses. At Jerusalem Peter, Barnabas and Paul, James and a letter each give an answer.

This commentary argues that one question reached Jerusalem in two forms, that the four answers speak to four parts of it, and that the council neither founds the body of Christ nor transfers Israel’s hope. In Chapter 15 it sets its reading beside modern writers who see it differently.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not survey how Cornelius and the council have been read and leave the choice to the reader. It states what the author believes Acts 10 to 15 teaches, sets out the evidence scene by scene, hears the writers who disagree, and answers them from Scripture.

It is the fourth of five volumes in the Acts Commentary Series, each of which walks one section of the book in order. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which report what a passage says. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout, and the writers named in Chapter 15 are described in the volume’s own words. Its own question is narrow: what was asked at Jerusalem, and which speaker answered which part?

FROM THE VOLUME

What This Book Sets Out to Do

One question reached Jerusalem in two forms, and Luke gives each part of the answer to its own speaker.

The volume asks of each scene who speaks, to whom, from what ground, and what is left unsaid, and keeps the four answers apart as Luke does.
