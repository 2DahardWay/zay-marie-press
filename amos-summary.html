Amos

Justice, judgment, and restoration.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreMinor Prophets
Primary SettingNorthern Israel during prosperity under Jeroboam II
Key MovementFrom universal judgment to Israel’s accountability and Davidic restoration
On This PagePurposeHistorical and Biblical ContextStructure and OutlineMajor ThemesKey PassagesInterpretive ConsiderationsStudy Questions

Purpose

Amos announces that prosperity and religious activity cannot shield Israel from judgment. The LORD condemns injustice, exploitation, idolatry, and complacency, insisting that covenant privilege increases accountability. The book ends with restoration of David’s fallen shelter and Israel in its land.

Historical and Biblical Context

Amos is a shepherd and cultivator from Judah sent to prophesy against the northern kingdom during outward stability and deep inequality.

Evidence and inference: Prophetic texts join historical setting, covenant proclamation, symbolic imagery, and future promise. This summary distinguishes explicit textual claims from debated conclusions about date, structure, chronology, or fulfillment.

Structure and Outline

  1. Oracles against nations and Israel — Amos 1–2
    Judgment circles surrounding peoples and lands decisively upon Israel.
  2. Charges and warnings — Amos 3–6
    Privilege, worship, luxury, and injustice are exposed.
  3. Visions and confrontation — Amos 7–9:10
    Five visions portray judgment; Amaziah opposes the prophet.
  4. Davidic and national restoration — Amos 9:11–15
    The fallen house is raised and Israel is planted in its land.

Major Themes

Justice

Worship divorced from righteousness becomes offensive.

Covenant Accountability

Election does not grant immunity from discipline.

The Day of the LORD

Israel’s presumed day of victory becomes darkness because of sin.

Restoration

Judgment is not the final word for David’s house and Israel.

Key Passages

Amos 2:6–16Israel’s injustice receives the climactic indictment.
Amos 3:1–8Covenant knowledge grounds accountability and prophetic necessity.
Amos 5:14–24The call to seek good exposes empty worship.
Amos 6:1–7Complacent luxury ignores national ruin.
Amos 7:10–17Amaziah rejects Amos at Bethel.
Amos 9:11–15Davidic restoration and secure life in the land are promised.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Read the oracles as a deliberate movement that draws Israel into judgment.
  • Social justice and covenant worship are inseparable in Amos’s charge.
  • The closing promise explicitly concerns David’s house and Israel’s land.
  • Later citation does not cancel the prophecy’s national and kingdom setting.

Study Questions

  1. How does the opening sequence expose Israel?
  2. Why does election increase accountability?
  3. What worship does God reject and why?
  4. How does the final restoration answer the book’s judgments?
