What Does “He That Endureth to the End Shall Be Saved” Mean in Matthew 10:22 and 24:13?

From Reader Questions, Volume 4: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 4 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

It is a promise to Israel’s remnant in the Tribulation: the one who holds out to the end of those days will be brought through alive. “The end” is the end of the Tribulation, when the Son of man comes, and not the end of a person’s life; “saved” is physical deliverance through the persecution and the deceptions of that time, and not eternal salvation. The Lord says it first to the Twelve, sent “to the lost sheep of the house of Israel,” and again on the mount of Olives in answer to a question about “the end of the world.” Endurance here is survival, not merit, and eternal salvation is never made to hang on endurance in any program. The saying was not spoken to the Body, which is not in the Tribulation and is not under kingdom requirements; its salvation rests on believing the gospel Paul preached (1 Corinthians 15:1–4).

Where the Verses Stand

The saying appears twice. In Matthew 10 the Lord sends out the Twelve, forbids them the way of the Gentiles and of the Samaritans, and tells them what they will meet: hatred, courts, betrayal. The saying closes that warning, and the next verse says they will not have gone over the cities of Israel before the Son of man comes. In Matthew 24 the disciples ask for the sign of his coming and “of the end of the world,” and the Lord’s answer runs through wars, afflictions, betrayal and cooling love to the same words, followed by the preaching of “this gospel of the kingdom” and then the end.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: in Matthew 10 the audience is the Twelve, sent to Israel and told to avoid the Gentiles (10:5–6), and the saying sits inside a mission that is to run on “till the Son of man be come” (10:23). The saying belongs to that setting.
  • Stated: in Matthew 24 the disciples ask about the end of the age, and the preaching that precedes it is “this gospel of the kingdom” (24:14), the message the Twelve carried to Israel, not the gospel of the grace of God.
  • Shown by the context: “saved” in the same chapter sits beside the rescue of living people: “except those days should be shortened, there should no flesh be saved: but for the elect’s sake those days shall be shortened” (24:22), and the elect are then gathered by the angels at the coming of the Son of man (24:30–31). To be saved here is to be kept alive through the affliction and delivered at the coming of the Son of man.
  • Shown by the context: the people addressed are called “the elect” (24:22, 31), already chosen. The text does not describe endurance as a purchase of that standing; it describes what the chosen are brought through, and the end it names is the one the disciples asked about (24:3), the close of the age and the coming of the Son of man.
  • Inferred: that “the end” is the close of the Tribulation, that “saved” is deliverance of the living and that endurance is survival and not merit. The verse says only that the one who endures “shall be saved”; the argument rests on the audience, on 24:22, where the rescue is of “flesh,” and on the rest of Scripture, where faith and never endurance is the means of eternal salvation.

The Other Readings

A promise to every believer through the age. Many commentaries read the saying as the perseverance by which genuine disciples are known in any age, leading to final salvation. It must explain why the setting is so specific, with Israel’s cities, the Twelve’s mission and the abomination and the Son of man’s coming, and why Paul never tells the Body its salvation waits on endurance. A deliverance for Israel’s remnant. Others read the discourse as addressed to Israel and the saved as those brought through the tribulation to the kingdom, which is the reading taken here. Rescue of the living through the Tribulation. This reading takes “the end” as the end of the Tribulation and “saved” as deliverance of the body, which fits 24:22 and is taken here.

What It Means for the Reader

The verse is not asking the reader in the Body to endure in order to be saved. Paul says the Body was “sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption” (Ephesians 1:13–14), and he does ask the Body to be “stedfast, unmoveable” in the Lord’s work (1 Corinthians 15:58), but he never says its salvation hangs on it. The verse tells the reader what the Lord promised the faithful in Israel in the days of that affliction.
