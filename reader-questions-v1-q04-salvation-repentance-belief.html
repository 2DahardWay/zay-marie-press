Is Salvation by Repentance or by Belief?

From Reader Questions, Volume 1: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 1 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

The Body is saved by believing the gospel Paul preached, by grace through faith and not of works. Repentance is the change of mind that goes with believing. The passages that command it are each addressed in their own setting, and none of them adds a second ground to the gospel of grace.

Where the Question Comes From

The question arises because Scripture uses the word “repent” in several places and in several settings, and because Peter’s words to Israel, “Repent, and be baptized,” sound very different from Paul’s “by grace are ye saved through faith.” The way through is to read each passage where it stands, with its audience, and to ask what each says the hearer must do and what it says the hearer is saved by.

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: Paul’s gospel is “Christ died for our sins… was buried… rose again the third day,” and the saved are those who “have believed” it (1 Corinthians 15:1–4). Ephesians 2:8–9 says the same: grace, faith, “not of works.”
  • Stated: Peter’s words in Acts 2:38 and 3:19 join repentance to baptism, to “the remission of sins” and to “times of refreshing” that come “from the presence of the Lord.” Paul does not join repentance to baptism or to the times of refreshing in any passage on this list.
  • Shown by the context: Peter’s words to Israel are program-specific. They belong to the offer of the kingdom to Israel in the early chapters of Acts and are not made a condition of the Body’s salvation. Repentance, baptism and covenant readiness are never the Body’s ground.
  • Shown by the context: When Paul names repentance, he names it with faith (20:21) or as what God’s goodness produces (Romans 2:4). In 26:20 the “works meet for repentance” are what fits the change, not what earns it.
  • Shown by the context: Second Corinthians 7:10 is written to a church Paul already calls saved. The “salvation” there is not the entry point, and the sorrow in view is over a wrong done among believers.
  • Inferred: that “repent” in Paul’s ministry names the change of mind that turns from idols or from self-righteousness to God, and that it is not a separate act that must be added to faith. The text pairs the words and does not define “repent” in a sentence of its own.

The Other Readings

Repentance as part of saving faith. John MacArthur (The Gospel According to Jesus) holds that repentance is integral to saving faith and that the faith that saves is a faith that submits. This reading takes Peter’s and Paul’s words as one message. Repentance not required for justification. Zane Hodges (Absolutely Free!) holds that the only condition of eternal life is believing, and that repentance is a matter of Christian living. Charles Ryrie (So Great Salvation) takes a similar view of the free gift while treating repentance as the change of mind that belongs to believing. Repentance as a separate condition. This reading adds repentance to belief as something done before salvation, and it has to make Acts 2:38 and Ephesians 2:8–9 one message.

What It Means for the Reader

The reader is not asked to complete a list. The gospel Paul preached is believed, and the believer is saved. Where Scripture says repent, it is asking for a real change of mind toward God, not an added work, and it is addressed to the hearers it names.
