Joshua

Israel enters Canaan under Joshua, receives the land by God’s power, and is called to covenant faithfulness within its inheritance.

TestamentOld Testament
GenreHistorical Narrative
Primary SettingCanaan after the death of Moses
Key MovementFrom wilderness encampment to tribal inheritance

Purpose

Joshua records Israel’s entrance into Canaan, the defeat of major opposition, the distribution of tribal inheritances, and the renewal of covenant loyalty. The book demonstrates that the LORD keeps His promises while requiring Israel to trust His word and reject the worship of the nations.

Historical and Biblical Context

Joshua continues directly from Deuteronomy. Moses has died, Joshua has been commissioned, and the generation on the plains of Moab is prepared to cross the Jordan. The conquest narratives and land allotments belong to Israel’s covenant history and stated inheritance.

Evidence and inference: This summary distinguishes the narrative’s explicit claims from historical reconstruction and interpretive inference. Where authorship, date, chronology, or structure is debated, the book’s received form and stated theological movement remain primary.

Structure and Outline

  1. Entry into the land — Joshua 1–5
    Joshua is commissioned; spies visit Jericho; Israel crosses the Jordan and prepares at Gilgal.
  2. Conquest of central, southern, and northern Canaan — Joshua 6–12
    Jericho falls, Achan’s sin brings judgment, and Israel’s campaigns break organized resistance.
  3. Distribution of inheritance — Joshua 13–21
    Land is assigned to the tribes, refuge cities are appointed, and the Levites receive towns.
  4. Covenant unity and renewal — Joshua 22–24
    A potential tribal conflict is resolved, and Joshua calls Israel to exclusive loyalty.

Major Themes

Promise and Fulfillment

The land distributions are presented as fulfillment of what God swore to Israel’s fathers.

Divine Presence and Courage

Joshua’s courage rests upon God’s presence and careful obedience to His revealed word.

Holiness and Corporate Responsibility

Achan’s sin shows that covenant violation can affect the wider community.

Inheritance and Covenant Loyalty

Possessing the land brings responsibility to serve the LORD and reject idolatry.

Key Passages

Joshua 1:1–9Joshua is commissioned to lead with courage and fidelity to the law.
Joshua 3:7–17Israel crosses the Jordan on dry ground.
Joshua 6:1–27Jericho falls according to God’s instruction.
Joshua 7:1–26Achan’s violation brings defeat and judgment.
Joshua 21:43–45The narrator summarizes God’s faithfulness to His promises.
Joshua 24:14–28Joshua calls Israel to choose whom the nation will serve.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Read the conquest within God’s stated judgment upon Canaan and His covenant promise to Israel, not as a general authorization for religious violence.
  • Distinguish the defeat of organized resistance from the continuing work of occupying and governing the land.
  • Treat geographical lists and allotments as theological evidence of inheritance, not as expendable detail.
  • Do not transfer Israel’s land promises or national commission to the Body of Christ.
  • Later applications should preserve the original historical meaning and programmatic assignment.

Study Questions

  1. How does Joshua 1 connect Joshua’s leadership to Moses and the written law?
  2. What purposes do the Jordan crossing and memorial stones serve?
  3. How does Achan’s sin clarify covenant responsibility?
  4. What does the distribution of land contribute to the book’s argument?
  5. Why does Joshua end with covenant renewal?
