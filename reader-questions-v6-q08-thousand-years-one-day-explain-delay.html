Does “A Thousand Years Is as One Day” Explain the Delay? Psalm 90:4 & 2 Peter 3:8

From Reader Questions, Volume 6: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 6 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

Psalm 90 speaks of God’s eternity. Peter quotes it to answer scoffers and to show that delay does not deny promise. The psalm does not give a formula; Peter uses it to correct a charge.

Where the Verses Stand

Psalm 90 contrasts God’s eternity with man’s brevity. 2 Peter 3 answers scoffers about the promise.

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
Ps 90:1–4“Lord, thou hast been our dwelling place in all generations. Before the mountains were brought forth, or ever thou hadst formed the earth and the world, even from everlasting to everlasting, thou art God. Thou turnest man to destruction; and sayest, Return, ye children of men. For a thousand years in thy sight are but as yesterday when it is past, and as a watch in the night.”“From everlasting to everlasting”; “a thousand years in thy sight are but as yesterday.”
Ps 90:12“So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom.”“So teach us to number our days.”
2Pet 3:3–4“Knowing this first, that there shall come in the last days scoffers, walking after their own lusts, And saying, Where is the promise of his coming? for since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of the creation.”Scoffers: “Where is the promise of his coming?”
2Pet 3:8–9“But, beloved, be not ignorant of this one thing, that one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day. The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”“One day is with the Lord as a thousand years”; “not slack” and “longsuffering.”
Rev 20:6“Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with him a thousand years.”“Reign with him a thousand years.”

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the psalm speaks of God’s eternity.
  • Shown by the context: Peter answers scoffers.
  • Inferred: the psalm is not a timetable.

The Other Readings

  • Prophetic formula: strains at the psalm’s meaning.
  • No relevance: strains at Peter’s use.
  • Corrective use: fits both.
ReadingWhat it must sayWhere it strains
A day-age formulaOne day equals a thousand years.Peter’s saying runs both ways.
Revelation’s thousand years are symbolic3:8 shows the number is not literal.Peter does not mention Revelation 20.
God is not bound by timeThe verse answers delay with longsuffering.None; it follows 3:9.

What It Means for the Reader

God’s time is not man’s; delay is not denial.
