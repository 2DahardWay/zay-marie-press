ABOUT THIS VOLUME

One Verse, Argued from Its Own Setting

Each entry answers one question about one verse or short passage. It does not begin with a theory and look for verses to fit; it begins with the verse, reads it in its own passage and in the chapter or chapters before it, and lets the rest of Scripture support what the context shows.

It is the fifth volume of the Readers Question Series. It is shorter than a Commentary Volume and takes one question at a time, and it is a different kind of book from a Digital Study, which reports what a passage says and leaves the question open. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout. Each entry gives the whole reasoning for its answer.

FREE SHORT ANSWERS

The Ten Questions, Answered in Brief

Open a question to read its short answer. The full entry in the volume gives the whole reasoning, the other readings and the modern writers behind them.

1. How Do I Know God’s Will? Romans 12:2

The Body of Christ knows God’s will through Paul’s revelation, not through signs, dreams or prophetic guidance. Paul says the way is a renewed mind: “be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.”

2. Why Does God Allow Suffering? Romans 5:3–4

For the Body, suffering is not covenant punishment, because the Body is not under the Law. Paul says “tribulation worketh patience; And patience, experience; and experience, hope,” and that to believe is also “to suffer for his sake.” God uses suffering to show Christ’s strength in weakness: “my strength is made perfect in weakness.”

3. How Do I Deal with Doubt? Ephesians 1:13 and 2 Timothy 3:15

Doubt is met by returning to what Paul wrote, which steadies the mind, and the Body is not asked to look for signs to confirm it. Believers were “sealed with that holy Spirit of promise” when they believed, so doubt is not a sign of lost salvation. The Spirit helps by opening what God has given, “that we might know the things that are freely given to us of God.”

4. How Do I Deal with Temptation? 1 Corinthians 10:13

Temptation is resisted by walking in the Spirit. The Body is not under the Law, so victory does not come through legalism. Paul tells believers to “reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God,” and promises that God “will with the temptation also make a way to escape.”

5. How Do I Deal with Unanswered Prayer? 2 Corinthians 12:8–9

Unanswered prayer is not a covenant judgment on the Body, which is not under the Law and not Israel. God answers according to his wisdom, and Paul’s own case shows what that can mean: he besought the Lord three times that the thorn “might depart from me,” and the answer was “My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.”

6. How Do I Deal with Spiritual Dryness? Colossians 3:16

Dryness often comes from neglecting Scripture, prayer or fellowship, and renewal comes through Paul’s doctrine opened by the Spirit. The Body is not told to seek revival through signs or emotional experience: “we walk by faith, not by sight.” The word is the remedy: “Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly.”

7. How Do I Deal with Guilt? Colossians 2:13–14

Guilt is dealt with by believing that the Body is fully forgiven in Christ. Paul says God has “forgiven you all trespasses” and the “handwriting of ordinances that was against us” is gone. “Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God.”

8. How Do I Deal with Fear? 2 Timothy 1:7

Fear is met by grounding the heart in what God has given the Body. Paul says “God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind,” and that believers have not received “the spirit of bondage again to fear.” The Body is not appointed to wrath: “God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation.”

9. What Is the Fate of Infants Who Die? 2 Samuel 12:23

Infants who die are safe with God. They have not reached the understanding by which a person is held accountable, so the Law does not condemn them, conscience has not charged them and they have not rejected a gospel. David’s word over his dead child, “I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me,” shows that God receives one who had no moral capacity.

10. What Is the Conscience? Romans 2:15

The conscience is the inward moral witness God put in every person. Paul says it bears witness, “their thoughts the mean while accusing or else excusing one another.” It is not infallible: some have a weak conscience that is easily wounded and “defiled.”
