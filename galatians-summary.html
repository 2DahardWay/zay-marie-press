Galatians

Paul defends his gospel and apostleship, declares justification by faith apart from works of law, and calls believers to stand in liberty and walk by the Spirit.

TestamentNew Testament
GenrePauline Epistle
RecipientsThe churches of Galatia
Central EmphasisJustification and liberty in Christ

Purpose

Paul writes urgently because teachers are pressuring Gentile believers to receive circumcision and live under the law. He declares that this message is not a harmless supplement but a distortion of the gospel of Christ.

To answer it, Paul establishes the divine source of his gospel, recounts recognition of his distinct apostleship, demonstrates from Scripture that justification and sonship are received by faith, and explains liberty as Spirit-governed service rather than self-indulgence.

Historical and Biblical Context

The identity of the Galatian recipients and the precise date remain debated, affecting whether the letter is placed before or after the Jerusalem council of Acts 15. The central crisis, however, is clear: Gentile believers are being compelled toward circumcision and law observance as necessary religious standing.

Galatians belongs within the Acts 9–28 overlap and provides direct evidence of two concurrent apostolic lines. Paul’s gospel was revealed through Paul, and the Jerusalem leaders recognized his apostleship to the uncircumcision while Peter’s apostleship concerned the circumcision.

Structure and Outline

  1. The gospel and Paul’s apostleship defended — Galatians 1–2
    Paul’s message came through revelation of Jesus Christ and was recognized without subordination to Jerusalem.
  2. Justification, promise, law, and sonship — Galatians 3–4
    Faith, Abraham, the purpose of the law, inheritance, and adoption expose the error of returning to bondage.
  3. Liberty and life by the Spirit — Galatians 5–6
    Believers must resist circumcision as obligation, serve through love, walk by the Spirit, restore gently, and sow to the Spirit.

Major Themes

The Divine Source of Paul’s Gospel

Paul did not receive his gospel from men. Its revelation through Paul and later recognition by Jerusalem protect its authority and distinct commission.

Justification by Faith

God justifies by faith, not works of law. Abraham’s believing response precedes circumcision and illustrates righteousness received apart from legal performance.

Promise and the Purpose of the Law

The law, added because of transgressions, does not cancel the prior promise. It served a temporary custodial function leading to Christ.

Sonship and Liberty

Believers are sons and heirs rather than slaves. Returning to elementary bondage contradicts their God-given standing.

Walking by the Spirit

Grace does not license the flesh. The Spirit produces love and godly character and enables believers to bear one another’s burdens.

Interpretive Considerations

  • Do not reduce the controversy to generic legalism. Circumcision, law, Jewish-Gentile table fellowship, and demanded programmatic conformity are explicit.
  • Galatians 2:7–9 presents recognized distinctions within concurrent ministries; it should not be rewritten as rivalry or a single undifferentiated commission.
  • Distinguish revelation from recognition: Paul received his gospel through revelation of Jesus Christ, while Jerusalem later recognized the grace and apostleship given to him.
  • Trace Paul’s varied uses of “seed” and “children of Abraham” within each argument rather than assuming every use has identical national or programmatic meaning.
  • The Hagar-Sarah discussion is Paul’s inspired allegorical use of Genesis; it does not license uncontrolled allegorizing elsewhere.
  • Christian liberty is freedom from imposed bondage for Spirit-enabled love and service, not independence from moral responsibility.

Study Questions

  1. Why does Paul treat the teachers’ message as a different gospel?
  2. What do Galatians 1–2 reveal about the source and recognition of Paul’s apostleship?
  3. How does Abraham support Paul’s argument for justification by faith?
  4. What temporary purpose does Paul assign to the law?
  5. How do sonship and inheritance answer the temptation to return to bondage?
  6. What distinguishes walking by the Spirit from gratifying the flesh?
