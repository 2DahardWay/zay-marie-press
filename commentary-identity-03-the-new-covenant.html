The New Covenant

Promised to Israel, Ratified at the Cross, Awaiting Its Day

With whom is the new covenant made, and what does the church have from its blood?

God promised a new covenant to “the house of Israel, and the house of Judah,” and filled it with a law written on the heart, the knowledge of the LORD, forgiveness and the land. The Lord named it in his blood at the supper, and Hebrews says his death put it in force. This commentary argues that the covenant is Israel’s from the first clause to the last, that it was ratified at the cross, and that it will be fulfilled in the nation when the Deliverer comes out of Zion.

It reads the covenants that came before, Jeremiah 31 clause by clause, the companion promises in Ezekiel, Isaiah and Hosea, the upper room, Hebrews, the Acts and 2 Corinthians 3. It then sets out what the Body of Christ has from the same blood, answers the readings that give the covenant to the church or divide it in two, and follows the covenant to its day in the kingdom and the new earth.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not survey the views and leave the choice to the reader. It states what the author believes the new covenant passages teach, sets out the evidence from the law and the prophets to the Revelation, and answers those who disagree where they are strongest, including the reading that makes the church the covenant’s people and the reading that finds two new covenants.

It is the third volume of the Prophetic Identity Commentary Series, which asks who the people of the prophets and of Revelation are. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which walk through a passage and report what the text says. The volume keeps the blood for everyone it has redeemed, and asks only that the covenant be left with the people to whom the prophets gave it. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout.
