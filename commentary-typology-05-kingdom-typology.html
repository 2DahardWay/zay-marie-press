TYPOLOGY COMMENTARY SERIES · VOLUME 5

Kingdom Typology

David, Solomon, the Temple, and the Messianic Kingdom

What does the pattern of the King teach, and where does it stop?

Scripture sets two kings side by side: David, the shepherd chosen, anointed and refused, who wins the kingdom through conflict, and Solomon, the son of rest who reigns in peace and builds the house. This commentary reads them in order and holds firm the places where the New Testament names Christ as the Son of David and one greater than Solomon.

It then follows both kings’ failures, the Branch and the kingdom the prophets describe on the earth, and asks what Paul says to the Body. In Chapter 12 it sets its reading beside modern writers who see it differently.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not survey what has been taught about David, Solomon and the kingdom and leave the choice to the reader. It states what the author believes the passages teach, sets out the evidence from the history, the Psalms, the Prophets, the Gospels and the letters, hears the writers who disagree in their own words, and answers them from Scripture.

It is the fifth and last volume of the Typology Commentary Series. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which walk through passages and report what the text says. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout, and the writers named in Chapter 12 are quoted only from words the author has read in full. Its own question is narrow: which features of David and Solomon does Scripture itself set forward as the pattern of the coming King, and where does the pattern stop?
