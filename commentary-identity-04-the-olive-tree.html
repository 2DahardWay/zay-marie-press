The Olive Tree

Israel’s Tree, the Gentiles’ Share and the Mystery of Romans 11

Whose tree is the olive tree, and what do the Gentiles share in it?

Paul says that some of the branches were broken off, that a wild olive was graffed in among them, and that it partakes “of the root and fatness of the olive tree.” This commentary argues that the tree is Israel’s from root to branch, that the figure was Israel’s long before Paul used it, and that the Gentiles of Romans 11 are participants in Israel’s blessing who do not become Israel, enter Israel’s covenants or become natural branches.

It reads the olive tree through the Law, the Psalms and the prophets, walks Romans 9 to 11 in order, and takes up the root, the broken branches, the wild olive and the warning to the Gentile. It then reads the mystery of blindness in part and the fulness of the Gentiles, the saving of all Israel, and the tree in the kingdom, and sets out why the Body of Christ, described in other places in other terms, is not the tree.

ABOUT THIS VOLUME

A Commentary That Argues a Position

This volume does not survey the views and leave the choice to the reader. It states what the author believes Romans 11 teaches, sets out the evidence from the Law, the prophets and Paul’s other letters, and answers those who disagree where they are strongest, including the reading that makes the church the olive tree and the reading that says Israel has been replaced.

It is the fourth volume of the Prophetic Identity Commentary Series, which asks who the people of the prophets and of Revelation are. It is a different kind of book from the Digital Studies, which walk through a passage and report what the text says. The volume asks that the tree be left with the people to whom the Scriptures gave it, and that the Gentile’s share in it be read as Paul wrote it. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version throughout.
