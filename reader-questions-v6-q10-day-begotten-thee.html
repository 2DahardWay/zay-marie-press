What Does “This Day Have I Begotten Thee” Mean? Psalm 2:7, Acts 13:33, and Hebrews 1:5

From Reader Questions, Volume 6: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 6 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

Psalm 2 speaks of the king God sets on Zion. Acts 13 applies the line to the resurrection; Hebrews applies it to sonship. The psalm’s line is used in two settings without merging them.

Where the Verses Stand

Psalm 2 speaks of God’s king. Acts 13 applies the line to resurrection. Hebrews 1 applies it to sonship.

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
Ps 2:1–2“Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing? The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against the LORD, and against his anointed, saying,”The nations rage “against the LORD, and against his anointed.”
Ps 2:7–9“I will declare the decree: the LORD hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee. Ask of me, and I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance, and the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession. Thou shalt break them with a rod of iron; thou shalt dash them in pieces like a potter's vessel.”“Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee … the heathen for thine inheritance.”
Ac 13:32–34“And we declare unto you glad tidings, how that the promise which was made unto the fathers, God hath fulfilled the same unto us their children, in that he hath raised up Jesus again; as it is also written in the second psalm, Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee. And as concerning that he raised him up from the dead, now no more to return to corruption, he said on this wise, I will give you the sure mercies of David.”“Raised up Jesus again; as it is also written in the second psalm.”
Heb 1:5“For unto which of the angels said he at any time, Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee? And again, I will be to him a Father, and he shall be to me a Son?”“For unto which of the angels said he at any time”
Heb 5:5“So also Christ glorified not himself to be made an high priest; but he that said unto him, Thou art my Son, to day have I begotten thee.”“Christ glorified not himself to be made an high priest.”
Rom 1:3–4“Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh; And declared to be the Son of God with power, according to the spirit of holiness, by the resurrection from the dead:”“Declared to be the Son of God with power … by the resurrection.”

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the psalm speaks of God’s king.
  • Shown by the context: Acts applies it to resurrection; Hebrews to sonship.
  • Inferred: the line is used in two settings.

The Other Readings

  • Only resurrection: strains at Hebrews.
  • Only sonship: strains at Acts.
  • Two uses: fits both.
ReadingWhat it must sayWhere it strains
He became Son at the resurrection“This day” is his beginning.Hebrews 1:5; “declared.”
An eternal begettingThe line speaks of eternity.Acts 13:33; Romans 1:4.
A declaration at exaltation“This day” is when he was shown to be Son.None; it fits all three texts.

What It Means for the Reader

The psalm’s line is true in both settings without merging them.
