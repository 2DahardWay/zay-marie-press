Why Did Jesus Cry “My God, My God, Why Hast Thou Forsaken Me?” Matthew 27:46 & Psalm 22:1

From Reader Questions, Volume 6: Questions on Difficult Bible Verses. Free to read here; the whole volume is a free PDF.

FreeDownload Volume 6 (PDF)
THE ANSWER

Jesus speaks the first line of Psalm 22 at the end of the darkness, and the forsaking is real in the hour of the cross. Matthew has already built the scene from Psalm 22 before the cry. The psalm itself turns from abandonment to being heard, and Hebrews places its praise in His mouth. The Gospels do not explain the forsaking; Paul gives that explanation to the Body.

Where the Verses Stand

Matthew records the crucifixion in order: garments parted, heads wagged, taunts spoken, darkness falling, and the cry at the ninth hour. Psalm 22 describes the mocked sufferer and the turning to praise. The setting is Israel’s, and Matthew uses the psalm’s details before the cry.

VerseKJV textWhat to observe
Ps 22:1–2“My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? why art thou so far from helping me, and from the words of my roaring? O my God, I cry in the daytime, but thou hearest not; and in the night season, and am not silent.”The question “why”; the sufferer says “thou hearest not.”
Ps 22:7–8, 18“All they that see me laugh me to scorn: they shoot out the lip, they shake the head, saying, He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him, seeing he delighted in him.” … “They part my garments among them, and cast lots upon my vesture.”“They shake the head”; “He trusted on the LORD”; “They part my garments.”
Mt 27:35“And they crucified him, and parted his garments, casting lots: that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet, They parted my garments among them, and upon my vesture did they cast lots.”The garments are parted, as in Psalm 22:18.
Mt 27:39–43“And they that passed by reviled him, wagging their heads, And saying, Thou that destroyest the temple, and buildest it in three days, save thyself. If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross. Likewise also the chief priests mocking him, with the scribes and elders, said, He saved others; himself he cannot save. If he be the King of Israel, let him now come down from the cross, and we will believe him. He trusted in God; let him deliver him now, if he will have him: for he said, I am the Son of God.”Heads wagged and “He trusted in God,” as in Psalm 22:7–8.
Mt 27:45–47“Now from the sixth hour there was darkness over all the land unto the ninth hour. And about the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, saying, Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani? that is to say, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? Some of them that stood there, when they heard that, said, This man calleth for Elias.”Darkness, the cry in Aramaic with its meaning, and the bystanders hearing “Elias.”
Ps 22:21, 24“Save me from the lion's mouth: for thou hast heard me from the horns of the unicorns.” … “For he hath not despised nor abhorred the affliction of the afflicted; neither hath he hid his face from him; but when he cried unto him, he heard.”“Thou hast heard me”; “when he cried unto him, he heard.”
Lk 23:46“And when Jesus had cried with a loud voice, he said, Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit: and having said thus, he gave up the ghost.”Luke records a different word at the same hour: “Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit.”
Heb 2:10–12“For it became him, for whom are all things, and by whom are all things, in bringing many sons unto glory, to make the captain of their salvation perfect through sufferings. For both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one: for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren, Saying, I will declare thy name unto my brethren, in the midst of the church will I sing praise unto thee.”Hebrews puts Psalm 22:22 in his mouth: “I will declare thy name unto my brethren.”

What Carries the Weight

  • Stated: the cry is spoken with a loud voice at the ninth hour.
  • Shown by the context: Matthew has already used Psalm 22’s details.
  • Inferred: the forsaking is real, not merely felt.
  • Inferred: Paul’s explanation belongs to the Body and is not read back into the Gospels.

The Other Readings

  • Real abandonment: must explain Psalm 22:24 and John 16:32.
  • Quotation of the whole psalm: must explain the question “why” and the darkness.
  • Felt, not real: must explain why the Gospels record the cry at all.
ReadingWhat it must sayWhere it strains
A real abandonmentThe Father turned away at the cross.Psalm 22:24; John 16:32.
Quotation of the whole psalmThe cry points to the vindication.“Why”; the darkness; the Aramaic kept.
Felt, not realThe Son felt forsaken; the Father did not leave.“Forsaken” is asked as a real question.

What It Means for the Reader

The cry belongs to the psalm’s scene and its answer. The reader does not build a doctrine of the Godhead on one line. The Body receives Paul’s explanation of the cross.
